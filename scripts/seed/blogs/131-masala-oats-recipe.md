> **Quick answer:** For homemade **masala oats**, temper **mustard, cumin and curry leaves** in **1 tsp oil**, sauté **onion, ginger, green chilli and chopped vegetables** (beans, carrot, peas, tomato), add **turmeric and a little pepper**, then stir in **40 g rolled oats** with **1–1¼ cups water** and cook **3–4 minutes**. Finish with lemon and coriander. A bowl has about **190–200 kcal, 6–7 g protein and 5 g fibre**. Add **2 boiled eggs, paneer or a side of sprouts** to make it a 20 g protein breakfast.

Packaged masala oats are convenient, but many are high in salt and flavour enhancers. Homemade masala oats take 10 minutes, taste like a light upma, and you control what goes in. This is close to the **Masala Oats** carb side in our BroTin Box menu.

# Ingredients (1 serving)

- 40 g rolled oats (about ½ cup)
- 1 tsp oil
- ¼ tsp mustard seeds
- ¼ tsp cumin seeds
- 6–8 curry leaves
- 1 small onion, finely chopped
- ½ tsp grated ginger
- 1 green chilli, chopped
- ½ cup chopped vegetables (beans, carrot, peas, capsicum)
- 1 small tomato, chopped
- ¼ tsp turmeric
- A pinch of black pepper
- Salt to taste
- 1–1¼ cups water
- Lemon juice and coriander to finish

# Method

## 1. Optional: dry roast the oats

Roast the oats in a dry pan for **2 minutes** on low heat until lightly fragrant. This gives a nuttier taste and less mushy texture. Keep aside.

## 2. Temper

Heat oil. Add mustard seeds; when they splutter, add cumin and curry leaves.

## 3. Cook the vegetables

Add onion, ginger and green chilli. Cook until the onion is soft. Add the vegetables and tomato, a pinch of salt, and cook **3–4 minutes** until slightly tender.

## 4. Add spices and water

Add turmeric and pepper. Pour in the water and bring to a boil.

## 5. Add oats

Stir in the oats. Cook on medium-low for **3–4 minutes**, stirring, until thick. Add a little more hot water if you like it softer.

## 6. Finish

Turn off the heat. Add lemon juice and coriander.

# Nutrition (1 bowl)

- **Calories:** about 190–200 kcal
- **Protein:** about 6–7 g
- **Carbs:** about 30–32 g
- **Fibre:** about 5 g
- **Fat:** about 4–5 g

Our kitchen's 120 g Masala Oats side is about **195 kcal and 6.5 g protein**.

# Make it high protein

Oats alone are moderate in protein. Add one of these:

- **2 boiled eggs** on the side: +12 g
- **50 g crumbled paneer** stirred in at the end: +9–10 g
- **A cup of sprouts or sundal**: +8–10 g
- **A bowl of hung curd**: +10 g

**Example: masala oats + 2 boiled eggs + curd** = about 400 kcal and 22–25 g protein.

# Is masala oats good for weight loss?

Yes. It is filling, high in fibre and low in fat. A homemade bowl is lighter in salt than most packets. Watch the oil and add protein. Compare it with idli in [oats vs idli](/blog/oats-vs-idli).

# Homemade vs packaged masala oats

**Homemade**

- Your choice of oil and salt
- Real vegetables
- No flavour enhancers

**Packaged**

- Very quick
- Often high in sodium
- Fewer vegetables
- Check labels. See [how to read nutrition labels](/blog/how-to-read-nutrition-labels-india).

# Tips

- **Use rolled oats,** not instant, for better texture
- **Do not overcook;** oats turn gluey
- **Make the vegetable base the night before** for a 5-minute breakfast
- **Gluten note:** oats are often processed with wheat. If you must avoid gluten, look for certified gluten-free oats.

# Frequently asked questions

**Can diabetics eat masala oats?**
Rolled oats with vegetables and protein are usually a better choice than polished-rice breakfasts. Follow your doctor's advice on portions.

**Can I make it with milk?**
Masala oats are usually made with water. Sweet oats are made with milk.

**Can I eat oats every day?**
Yes. Vary your breakfasts for different nutrients.
