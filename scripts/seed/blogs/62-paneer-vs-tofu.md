> **Quick answer:** **Paneer has more protein per 100 g** (about **18–20 g**) than most firm tofu (about **12–17 g**), but it also has about **three times the fat** and roughly **twice the calories** (260–320 kcal vs 120–150 kcal). For weight loss, **tofu gives more protein per calorie**. For muscle gain or if you prefer the taste, **paneer is a great choice**. Both are good vegetarian protein.

Paneer and tofu look similar and get used in the same dishes. But one is made from milk and the other from soybeans, and that changes the numbers. Here is a clear comparison.

# Paneer vs tofu: nutrition per 100 g

**Full-fat paneer** (typical)

- Calories: about 260–320 kcal
- Protein: about 18–20 g
- Fat: about 20–25 g
- Carbs: about 1–3 g
- Calcium: high

**Firm tofu** (typical)

- Calories: about 120–150 kcal
- Protein: about 12–17 g
- Fat: about 7–9 g
- Carbs: about 2–4 g
- Calcium: high if made with calcium sulphate (check the label)

Numbers vary a lot by brand and firmness. Soft or silken tofu has much less protein (about 5–8 g). Low-fat paneer has more protein and much less fat. Always check the pack.

# Protein per 100 kcal

This is the number that matters most when you are losing weight:

- **Paneer:** about 6–7 g protein per 100 kcal
- **Firm tofu:** about 10–11 g protein per 100 kcal

So for the same calories, tofu gives you roughly **50% more protein**.

# Which is better for weight loss?

**Tofu**, for most people. It fills you up with fewer calories.

That said, paneer is fine in measured amounts. A 100 g portion of paneer in a meal is reasonable. The problem is usually the dish, not the paneer: paneer butter masala or paneer 65 can easily cross 500 kcal per serving.

Read more in our full guide to [protein in paneer](/blog/protein-in-paneer).

# Which is better for muscle gain?

**Either works.** When you need extra calories, paneer's fat helps you reach your target without huge portions.

Both have good-quality protein:

- **Paneer** is a dairy protein, mostly casein, which digests slowly.
- **Tofu** is a soy protein, one of the few complete plant proteins.

What matters most is your total protein for the day. See [how much protein per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day).

# Taste, cooking and cost

**Paneer**

- Familiar taste in Indian cooking
- Easy to find fresh in every Coimbatore shop
- Holds its shape in tikka, bhurji and curries

**Tofu**

- Mild, absorbs marinades well
- Needs pressing to remove water before cooking
- Harder to find in small shops; usually in supermarkets

Tip for tofu: press it for 15 minutes, cut into cubes, marinate in curd, chilli and spices, then grill or pan-sear. It takes on a tikka flavour very well.

# Who should choose which?

- **Losing weight:** tofu, or low-fat paneer
- **Gaining weight or muscle:** paneer
- **Lactose intolerant:** tofu
- **Soy allergy:** paneer
- **Vegan:** tofu
- **Want variety:** use both across the week

# Is soy safe for men?

Yes, in normal food amounts. Studies have not shown that eating tofu or soya lowers testosterone in men at normal intakes. We cover this in detail in [soya chunks protein](/blog/soya-chunks-protein).

# Frequently asked questions

**Is paneer healthier than tofu?**
Neither is "healthier" in general. Tofu is leaner. Paneer has more protein per gram but more fat.

**Can I eat paneer every day?**
Yes, 75–100 g a day is fine for most people, as part of a balanced diet.

**Is tofu good for women?**
Yes. Tofu is a good source of protein, calcium and iron.
