> **Quick answer:** A **high-protein diet** means eating **more protein than the basic minimum**, usually about **1.2–2.2 g per kg of body weight a day**, or roughly **25–35% of your calories from protein**. India's ICMR-NIN suggests about **0.8 g per kg** as the basic requirement for adults, which prevents deficiency but is lower than what helps most people **lose fat, build muscle or stay full**. For healthy people, high-protein diets are safe. People with **kidney disease** should follow their doctor's advice.

"Eat more protein" is everywhere, but what does a high-protein diet actually mean, how much is "high", and who needs it? Here is a clear explanation.

# Basic requirement vs high protein

- **Basic requirement (to avoid deficiency):** about **0.8 g per kg** of body weight, the figure ICMR-NIN uses for Indian adults
- **Active people:** about **1.2–1.6 g per kg**
- **Strength training and muscle gain:** about **1.6–2.2 g per kg**
- **Losing weight while keeping muscle:** about **1.2–2 g per kg**

**Example for 70 kg:**

- Basic: about 56 g a day
- High-protein: about 85–150 g a day

Many Indians eat **close to or below the basic amount**, often 40–60 g a day. See [how much protein per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day).

# Why people choose a high-protein diet

## 1. Stays full longer

Protein is the most filling macronutrient. People often eat less without trying.

## 2. Protects muscle during weight loss

In a calorie deficit, enough protein helps make sure the weight you lose is mostly fat. See [fat loss vs weight loss](/blog/fat-loss-vs-weight-loss).

## 3. Builds muscle with training

Protein supplies the building blocks your muscles need to repair and grow.

## 4. Burns a few more calories in digestion

Digesting protein uses more energy than digesting carbs or fat. The effect is small but real.

## 5. Supports healthy ageing

Older adults lose muscle naturally; more protein plus strength training slows that.

# What a high-protein Indian day looks like

**About 110–120 g protein (70 kg, active)**

- **Breakfast:** 3-egg omelette and 2 chapatis (about 25 g)
- **Lunch:** 150 g chicken or paneer, rice, dal, curd (about 45 g)
- **Evening:** sundal or sprouts with buttermilk (about 12 g)
- **Dinner:** fish, tofu or soya with chapatis and dal (about 35 g)

Vegetarian? Use paneer, tofu, soya, dal, curd and milk. See [high-protein veg lunch box ideas](/blog/high-protein-veg-lunch-box-ideas).

# Is a high-protein diet safe?

For **healthy adults**, studies have not shown harm from protein intakes up to about 2 g per kg.

**Be careful if you have:**

- **Chronic kidney disease:** your doctor may advise limiting protein
- **Liver disease**
- **Gout:** some high-purine foods (organ meats, some fish) may need limiting

**Myths:**

- "Protein damages healthy kidneys": not supported by research in healthy people
- "Protein weakens bones": research suggests adequate protein supports bone health

# High protein is not "only protein"

A good high-protein diet still includes:

- **Vegetables and fruit** for fibre and vitamins
- **Carbs** (rice, chapati, millets) for energy, especially if you train
- **Healthy fats** from nuts, seeds and fish
- **Water**

It is not the same as a meat-only or "zero-carb" diet. Compare in [types of diet](/blog/types-of-diet).

# Easy ways to eat more protein

- **Add an egg or curd** to breakfast
- **Double the dal**, or add chana or sprouts to lunch
- **Choose tikka or grilled dishes** over gravies
- **Snack on** roasted chana, peanuts, boiled eggs or paneer. See [high-protein Indian snacks](/blog/high-protein-indian-snacks).
- **Keep soya chunks** at home: the cheapest protein

# Frequently asked questions

**Is 100 g of protein a day too much?**
For most active adults, no. It is a common, healthy target.

**Can I get high protein on a veg diet?**
Yes, with paneer, tofu, soya, dal, curd, milk and whey if needed.

**Does a high-protein diet cause weight gain?**
Only if total calories are above your needs. Protein itself helps control hunger.
