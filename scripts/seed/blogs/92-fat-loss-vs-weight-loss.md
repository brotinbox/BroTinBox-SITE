> **Quick answer:** **Weight loss** is any drop on the scale: fat, water, muscle, glycogen or food in your gut. **Fat loss** is a drop in body fat specifically. You want fat loss. To make sure most of the weight you lose is fat, keep **protein high (1.2–1.6 g per kg)**, **strength train 2–4 times a week**, lose at **0.5–1% of body weight a week**, and track your **waist and photos**, not just the scale.

Two people can both lose 5 kg and end up looking completely different. One looks leaner and stronger; the other looks smaller but softer and tired. The difference is **what** they lost.

# What makes up your body weight

Your weight on the scale includes:

- **Fat**
- **Muscle**
- **Water** (about 50–60% of your body)
- **Glycogen** (stored carbs in muscles and liver, which hold water)
- **Bones and organs**
- **Food and waste in your gut**

Only one of these is what you actually want to lose: **fat**.

# Why the scale can mislead you

- **Water** can swing 1–2 kg in a day from salt, carbs, heat, hormones or a hard workout.
- **Glycogen:** cutting carbs drops 1–3 kg quickly, mostly water.
- **Muscle loss** from crash diets shows as weight loss, but makes you weaker and slows your metabolism.
- **Muscle gain** from new strength training can hide fat loss on the scale.

Read more in [why your weight loss stopped](/blog/weight-loss-plateau-why-scale-stopped).

# Signs you are losing fat (not just weight)

- **Your waist measurement is going down**
- **Clothes fit looser** around the belly and hips
- **Strength stays the same or goes up** in the gym
- **You look leaner in photos**, even if the scale moved slowly
- **Weight drops steadily** at 0.5–1 kg a week, not 3 kg in a week

# Signs you are losing muscle

- **Strength dropping fast**
- **Feeling weak, tired and cold**
- **Very fast weight loss** on a very low calorie diet
- **Looking "flat" or saggy**, not leaner
- **Low protein intake**

# How to make sure you lose fat

## 1. Eat enough protein

**1.2–1.6 g per kg** of body weight (up to 2 g if you train hard). Protein protects muscle in a calorie deficit. See [how much protein per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day).

## 2. Lift weights

Strength training 2–4 times a week tells your body to keep muscle. Even home workouts help. See [how to lose weight without a gym](/blog/lose-weight-without-gym).

## 3. Do not crash diet

A moderate deficit of about **500 kcal a day** keeps muscle better than extreme diets. Fast loss on 800 kcal a day burns more muscle.

## 4. Sleep 7–8 hours

Poor sleep shifts weight loss towards muscle loss and increases hunger.

## 5. Walk every day

Steps burn fat without draining your recovery like intense cardio can.

# How to measure fat loss at home

- **Waist:** measure at the navel, every week, same time, relaxed (not sucking in)
- **Photos:** front, side and back every 2–4 weeks, same light
- **Scale:** weigh 3–4 times a week and use the weekly average
- **Strength:** write down your main lifts
- **Body fat machines:** home scales and gym machines (BIA) are not very accurate, but the trend over months can help

# What is body recomposition?

**Recomposition** means losing fat and gaining muscle at the same time, so your weight may barely change while your body looks much better.

It happens most in:

- Beginners to strength training
- People returning after a break
- People with more body fat

It requires high protein, strength training and a small deficit or maintenance calories.

# Frequently asked questions

**Is it better to lose fat or weight?**
Fat. Losing muscle and water makes you look and feel worse and makes regain more likely.

**How fast can I lose fat?**
About 0.5–1% of body weight a week for most people.

**Why did I lose 3 kg in my first week?**
Mostly water and glycogen. Fat loss is slower and steadier.
