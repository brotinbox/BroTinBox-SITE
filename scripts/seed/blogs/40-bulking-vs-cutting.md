> **Quick answer:** **Cut first** if you have a lot of body fat (roughly above 20% for men or 30% for women) or your belly is the first thing you notice. **Bulk first** if you are lean and skinny and want to look bigger. If you are a **beginner somewhere in the middle**, you can often do a **recomp**: eat around maintenance, eat high protein and train hard, and you will lose fat and gain muscle at the same time.

"Should I bulk or cut?" is the most common question new lifters ask, and the one most often answered badly in gym WhatsApp groups. Here is a clear way to decide.

# What bulking and cutting mean

- **Bulking:** eating more calories than you burn (a **surplus**) so your body has the energy to build muscle. You also gain some fat.
- **Cutting:** eating fewer calories than you burn (a **deficit**) so your body uses stored fat. The goal is to lose fat while keeping muscle.
- **Recomp (body recomposition):** eating around maintenance with high protein and hard training, slowly losing fat and gaining muscle at the same time.

# How to decide: a simple test

Stand in front of a mirror and be honest.

## Cut first if:
- You have a visible belly or "love handles"
- Your waist is large compared with your shoulders
- You have been eating a lot of junk food and want to reset
- Body fat is roughly over 20% (men) or 30% (women)

Bulking from a high body fat level means more fat gain, and a much longer cut later.

## Bulk first if:
- You are slim, with a flat stomach but little muscle
- Clothes feel loose around your arms and chest
- You have been training for a while but your weight is not moving
- Body fat is roughly under 15% (men) or 23% (women)

## Recomp if:
- You are a beginner (less than about a year of proper training)
- You are "skinny fat": not overweight, but soft with little muscle
- You are in between and do not want to go through a full bulk or cut

Beginners and people returning to training respond very well to a recomp.

# How to bulk properly ("lean bulk")

- **Surplus:** about **200–300 kcal** above maintenance
- **Rate of gain:** about **0.25–0.5% of body weight per week**. For a 65 kg person, that is about 0.15–0.3 kg a week.
- **Protein:** about **1.6–2.2 g per kg** a day
- **Training:** progressive overload, 3–6 days a week

If you gain faster than this, much of the extra weight is fat. That is a **dirty bulk**, and we will cover it in its own post.

# How to cut properly

- **Deficit:** about **300–500 kcal** below maintenance
- **Rate of loss:** about **0.5–1% of body weight per week**
- **Protein:** keep it high, about **1.6–2.2 g per kg**, to protect muscle
- **Training:** keep lifting heavy. Lifting tells your body to keep the muscle.
- **Steps:** 7,000–10,000 a day helps a lot

Work out your numbers with our [calorie deficit guide](/blog/calorie-deficit-how-to-calculate).

# How long should each phase last?

- **Cut:** usually 8–16 weeks, or until you reach a body fat level you are happy with. Take a 1–2 week break at maintenance if you feel drained.
- **Bulk:** usually 4–6 months or more. Muscle is slow to build.

Avoid switching every few weeks. Short bulks and cuts mostly just move water and food weight up and down.

# Common mistakes

1. **Bulking to "get big" while already overweight.** You end up with more fat, not more visible muscle.
2. **Cutting too hard.** Very low calories make you lose muscle and strength, and often end in a binge.
3. **Low protein during a cut.** This is how people lose muscle and end up "skinny fat".
4. **Not tracking anything.** Weigh yourself 3–4 times a week in the morning and look at the weekly average.
5. **Judging by one week.** Water weight hides real progress. Look at 2–4 weeks.

# Frequently asked questions

**Can I bulk and cut at the same time?**
Not exactly, but a beginner recomp comes close: you lose fat and gain muscle slowly at maintenance calories.

**Should I do cardio while bulking?**
Yes, some. Walking and light cardio keep your heart and appetite healthy. Just eat enough to cover it.

**How do I know my maintenance calories?**
Start at about your body weight in kg × 30, track your weight for two weeks, and adjust. If your weight stays stable, that is your maintenance.

**Is rice okay during a cut?**
Yes, in measured portions. See [is rice bad for weight loss](/blog/is-rice-bad-for-weight-loss).
