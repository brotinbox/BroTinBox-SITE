> **Quick answer:** **Regular homemade curd** has about **3–4 g protein per 100 g**. **Greek yoghurt or hung curd** has about **8–10 g protein per 100 g**, because the watery whey is strained out. Both give **calcium and probiotics** (if not heated after setting). For **more protein**, hung curd or Greek yoghurt wins. For **everyday digestion, buttermilk and curd rice**, regular curd is perfect. You can make **hung curd at home** for a fraction of the price of Greek yoghurt.

Greek yoghurt has become a "health food" in Indian supermarkets, at several times the price of regular curd. Is it worth it? Here is the comparison, and how to make your own.

# What is the difference?

- **Curd (thayir):** milk set with a little curd starter. Thin to medium texture.
- **Greek yoghurt:** yoghurt strained to remove much of the whey, so it is thick and creamy.
- **Hung curd:** the Indian version of Greek yoghurt: curd hung in a cloth to drain the whey.

Hung curd and Greek yoghurt are **nutritionally very similar**.

# Nutrition per 100 g (approx.)

**Curd from toned milk**

- Calories: about 60–70 kcal
- Protein: about 3–4 g
- Fat: about 3–4 g
- Calcium: about 120–150 mg

**Hung curd / Greek yoghurt (plain, from toned or low-fat milk)**

- Calories: about 70–100 kcal
- Protein: about 8–10 g
- Fat: about 2–5 g (depends on the milk)
- Calcium: somewhat lower than curd, as some is lost in the whey

**Full-fat versions** have more calories.

# Which is better for protein?

**Hung curd or Greek yoghurt.** A 150 g bowl gives:

- Regular curd: about 5–6 g protein
- Hung curd or Greek yoghurt: about 12–15 g protein

That makes hung curd a great high-protein snack or breakfast. See [high-protein Indian snacks](/blog/high-protein-indian-snacks).

# Which is better for weight loss?

Both are good. Hung curd keeps you fuller because of the extra protein. Watch **flavoured Greek yoghurts**: many have **10–15 g of added sugar** per cup. Choose **plain**, and add fruit yourself.

# Which is better for digestion?

Both contain **live cultures (probiotics)** if they are fresh and not heated. Regular curd, buttermilk and curd rice are traditional and gentle on the stomach.

# Cost

- **Homemade curd:** the cheapest
- **Hung curd at home:** costs about the same as the curd you start with, but you get about half the amount
- **Packaged Greek yoghurt:** usually much more expensive per gram of protein

# How to make hung curd at home

1. Take **500 g of thick curd** (homemade or packaged).
2. Place a **clean muslin or cotton cloth** over a strainer set on a bowl.
3. Pour the curd in, gather the cloth and tie it loosely.
4. Keep it in the **fridge for 4–8 hours** (or overnight for very thick).
5. You will get about **200–250 g of thick hung curd**.

**Do not throw the whey.** Use it in dough for chapati, in rasam, or as a drink with salt and cumin.

# Ways to use hung curd

- **Marinades:** the base for tikka and grilled chicken. See [mint grilled chicken](/blog/mint-grilled-chicken-recipe) and [tandoori paneer tikka](/blog/tandoori-paneer-tikka-recipe).
- **Dips:** mix with garlic, mint, salt and pepper
- **Breakfast bowl:** with fruit, a few nuts and seeds
- **Shrikhand-style dessert:** with cardamom, a little jaggery or honey and saffron
- **Sandwich spread** instead of mayonnaise

# Curd at night?

For most healthy people, curd at night is fine. Read [is curd good at night](/blog/is-curd-good-at-night).

# Frequently asked questions

**Is Greek yoghurt better than curd?**
For protein, yes. For everyday eating and digestion, regular curd is just as good and much cheaper.

**Can I eat hung curd every day?**
Yes, a bowl a day is a great protein addition for most people.

**Is hung curd good for muscle gain?**
Yes. It is high in protein, easy to eat, and works as a snack or marinade.
