> **Quick answer:** **Your liver, kidneys, lungs, gut and skin already detox your body every day.** There is **no good evidence** that detox juices, teas, cleanses or "flush" drinks remove toxins or fat any better. Detox diets cause quick weight loss mainly from **water, low calories and laxatives**, and it usually returns. Some detox teas contain **laxatives or diuretics** that can cause dehydration and harm. To support your body's natural detox: **drink enough water, eat fibre, sleep well, limit alcohol, and avoid smoking**.

Detox teas, 3-day juice cleanses, lemon-honey water "fat cutters" and "liver flush" kits are sold everywhere. Here is what they really do.

# What "detox" really means

In medicine, **detox** means treating someone for alcohol or drug dependence, or poisoning. In marketing, it means vague claims about "toxins" without naming them.

Your body's real detox system:

- **Liver:** breaks down alcohol, medicines and waste products
- **Kidneys:** filter blood and remove waste in urine
- **Gut:** removes waste in stool
- **Lungs:** breathe out carbon dioxide
- **Skin:** sweats (minor role)

If these organs work, you do not need a cleanse. If they do not, you need a doctor, not a juice.

# Common detox products and what they do

## Juice cleanses

- Very low protein and calories
- Quick weight loss, mostly water and glycogen
- Hunger, fatigue, headaches
- Weight returns when you eat normally

See [does a liquid diet reduce weight](/blog/does-liquid-diet-reduce-weight).

## Detox teas

- Many contain **senna or other laxatives**, or diuretics
- You lose water and stool, not fat
- Regular use can cause **dehydration, cramps, electrolyte problems and laxative dependence**
- Some products have been found with undeclared ingredients

## Lemon-honey or jeera water "fat cutters"

- Fine as drinks, but they **do not melt fat**
- Lemon water can replace sugary drinks, which helps a little

## Foot pads, "liver flush" kits and colon cleanses

- No good evidence they remove toxins
- Colon cleanses can damage the bowel and upset electrolytes

# Why people feel better on a "detox"

Often because, for a few days, they:

- Stop alcohol, junk food and sugary drinks
- Drink more water
- Eat more fruit and vegetables
- Sleep a little better

These are good habits. You can keep them without the expensive product.

# Who should never do a detox cleanse

- People with **diabetes** (risk of low or high sugar)
- People with **kidney, liver or heart disease**
- **Pregnant or breastfeeding** women
- Anyone on **regular medicines**
- People with a history of **eating disorders**

# How to actually support your liver and kidneys

- **Limit alcohol**
- **Do not smoke**
- **Drink enough water.** See [how much water per day](/blog/how-much-water-per-day).
- **Eat fibre:** vegetables, fruit, dal, whole grains
- **Keep a healthy weight:** fatty liver is very common in India, and losing even 5–10% of body weight helps
- **Avoid unnecessary painkillers and supplements**
- **Get regular health checks**

# A real "reset week"

Instead of a cleanse, try 7 days of:

- **No sugary drinks** or alcohol
- **Protein and vegetables** at every meal
- **8,000–10,000 steps** a day
- **7–8 hours of sleep**
- **Home-cooked or measured meals**

You will likely feel better and lose 1–2 kg, some of it fat, and the habits can continue.

# Frequently asked questions

**Do detox drinks help weight loss?**
Only through water loss or replacing high-calorie drinks. They do not burn fat.

**Is warm lemon water in the morning good?**
It is a fine drink, but it does not detox or melt fat.

**Are Ayurvedic detox powders safe?**
Some may be fine, but quality varies and some contain laxatives or heavy metals. Speak to a qualified doctor before using them.
