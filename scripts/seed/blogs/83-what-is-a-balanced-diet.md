> **Quick answer:** A **balanced diet** gives your body all the nutrients it needs in the right amounts: **carbohydrates, protein, healthy fats, vitamins, minerals, fibre and water**. On an Indian plate, that means about **half vegetables and fruit**, about **a quarter grains** (rice, chapati, millets) and about **a quarter protein** (dal, eggs, chicken, fish, paneer), plus **curd or milk** and a small amount of **nuts and oil**. India's national nutrition body, ICMR-NIN, recommends a similar "My Plate for the Day".

"Eat a balanced diet" is the most common health advice, but rarely explained. Here is what it means, with an Indian example you can follow.

# The 7 parts of a balanced diet

## 1. Carbohydrates

Your main energy source. Choose mostly whole and minimally processed: rice, chapati, millets (ragi, kambu, thinai), oats, fruit, vegetables.

## 2. Protein

Builds and repairs muscle, skin, hair and enzymes. Many Indian diets are low in protein. Sources: dal, chana, rajma, eggs, chicken, fish, paneer, curd, soya.

See [how much protein per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day).

## 3. Fats

Needed for hormones and absorbing vitamins A, D, E and K. Use oil and ghee in measured amounts and include nuts, seeds and fish.

## 4. Vitamins

Found mostly in vegetables, fruit, dairy, eggs, meat and whole grains. Vitamin B12 is mostly in animal foods; vitamin D comes mainly from sunlight.

## 5. Minerals

Calcium (milk, curd, ragi), iron (greens, dal, meat), zinc, potassium and more.

## 6. Fibre

Keeps digestion healthy and helps you feel full. Found in vegetables, fruit, dal, whole grains and millets. See [high-fibre Indian foods](/blog/high-fibre-indian-foods).

## 7. Water

About 2–3 litres a day for most adults, more in hot weather or with exercise.

# The balanced Indian plate

Picture one plate at lunch or dinner:

- **Half the plate: vegetables and fruit.** Poriyal, kootu, keerai, salad, sambar vegetables.
- **About a quarter: grains.** About 1 cup cooked rice, or 2 chapatis, or 2–3 idlis.
- **About a quarter: protein.** Dal, chana, eggs, chicken, fish, paneer or tofu.
- **On the side:** a bowl of curd or buttermilk.
- **In the cooking:** 1–2 tsp oil per person per meal.

ICMR-NIN's 2024 dietary guidelines for Indians use a similar plate, with vegetables and fruit making up a large share and cereals limited to a smaller share than most Indians eat today.

# What a typical Indian plate gets wrong

- **Too much rice or chapati,** filling most of the plate
- **Too little protein:** a small spoon of dal or a few pieces of chicken in gravy
- **Too much oil** in tadka, fries and gravies
- **Too few vegetables** at breakfast and dinner
- **Sugar** in tea, coffee and snacks

Fixing these five things makes most Indian diets balanced.

# A balanced day (about 1,800 kcal)

- **Breakfast:** 2 dosas with sambar and chutney, 2 boiled eggs (or a cup of sprouts)
- **Mid-morning:** a fruit
- **Lunch:** 1 cup rice, fish or chicken curry or paneer, poriyal, salad, curd
- **Evening:** tea and a cup of sundal
- **Dinner:** 2 chapatis, dal, mixed vegetable, small salad

Vegetarian? Swap fish or chicken for paneer, tofu, extra dal or soya.

# Balanced diet for different goals

- **Weight loss:** same plate, smaller grain portion, more vegetables, keep protein high
- **Muscle gain:** same plate, bigger protein portion, a little more grain
- **Diabetes:** smaller grain portion, more fibre and protein, regular meal times, guided by your doctor

# Does "balanced" mean no junk food?

No. A balanced diet can include sweets, biryani or fried snacks **occasionally**. Aim for about 80–90% nourishing foods and leave room for the rest. See [junk food vs healthy food](/blog/junk-food-vs-healthy-food).

# Frequently asked questions

**Is rice part of a balanced diet?**
Yes. Rice is a good energy source. Just watch the portion and pair it with protein and vegetables.

**Do I need supplements for a balanced diet?**
Usually not, except possibly vitamin D or B12 (common deficiencies in India). Check with a blood test and your doctor.

**How do I know if my diet is balanced?**
If most meals look like the plate above, you are close.
