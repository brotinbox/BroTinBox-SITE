> **Quick answer:** A sustainable Indian weight loss plan keeps you in a **300–500 kcal daily deficit**, includes protein at every meal, fills half your plate with vegetables, and still uses familiar foods like idli, chapati, dal, curd and rice in measured portions. Expect to lose about **0.5–1% of your body weight per week**. The 7-day plan below shows exactly how.

Most weight loss plans fail for one reason: they are not food you would eat anyway. Two weeks of salads and soups, then a family function, then back to square one.

This plan is built from everyday South Indian food. It is designed for a target of roughly **1,500–1,800 kcal per day**, which suits many adults trying to lose weight. Your exact number depends on your size and activity, so adjust portions up or down.

> This is general guidance for healthy adults. If you have diabetes, thyroid conditions, kidney disease, or take regular medication, speak with your doctor before changing your diet.

# The 4 rules behind this plan

1. **Protein at every meal.** It keeps you full and protects muscle while you lose fat. Aim for 20–35 g per meal.
2. **Half the plate is vegetables.** Volume without many calories.
3. **Measured carbs, not zero carbs.** Rice and chapati stay, in measured portions.
4. **Oil is measured.** One tablespoon of oil is about 120 kcal. This is where most hidden calories live.

# The 7-day Indian weight loss meal plan

## Day 1 (Monday)
- **Breakfast:** 3 idlis + 2 boiled eggs + sambar
- **Lunch:** 1 cup rice + 1 katori dal + vegetable poriyal + curd
- **Snack:** buttermilk + small bowl of chana sundal
- **Dinner:** grilled chicken (150 g) or paneer (100 g) with 2 chapatis and salad

## Day 2 (Tuesday)
- **Breakfast:** 2 pesarattu + curd
- **Lunch:** 2 chapatis + soya chunk curry + cucumber raita
- **Snack:** 1 fruit + 10 almonds
- **Dinner:** vegetable oats upma + sprouted green gram salad

## Day 3 (Wednesday)
- **Breakfast:** egg bhurji (3 eggs) + 1 chapati
- **Lunch:** 1 cup rice + fish curry + vegetable kootu
- **Snack:** roasted chana
- **Dinner:** ragi dosa + chicken or paneer side + tomato chutney

## Day 4 (Thursday)
- **Breakfast:** oats pongal with moong dal + 2 boiled eggs
- **Lunch:** 2 chapatis + rajma + salad
- **Snack:** buttermilk + guava or apple
- **Dinner:** clear chicken or vegetable soup + grilled paneer or chicken

## Day 5 (Friday)
- **Breakfast:** 2 moong dal chillas + hung curd
- **Lunch:** 1 cup sambar rice + beans poriyal + 1 boiled egg or curd
- **Snack:** peanut chaat
- **Dinner:** grilled fish + green peas + broccoli or cabbage salad

## Day 6 (Saturday)
- **Breakfast:** 3 idlis + kollu sundal
- **Lunch:** 1 cup lemon rice + chicken or paneer + curd
- **Snack:** sprouts salad
- **Dinner:** 2 chapatis + egg curry or dal + salad

## Day 7 (Sunday)
- **Breakfast:** masala omelette + 1 chapati
- **Lunch:** your favourite family meal, in a measured portion (one plate, no refills)
- **Snack:** fruit + buttermilk
- **Dinner:** light: rasam + small cup of rice + vegetables + a protein side

Yes, Day 7 includes a normal family lunch. Plans that ban your favourite foods are the ones you quit.

# How much weight will you lose?

Roughly, a deficit of **7,700 kcal** equals about 1 kg of body fat. A daily deficit of 500 kcal therefore adds up to **about 0.5 kg per week**. The first week often shows a bigger drop due to water and lower salt and carbs; that is normal.

Track your weight every morning and look at the weekly average, not single days. Also track your waist: for South Asian adults, a waist above **90 cm for men** or **80 cm for women** signals higher health risk. # Frequently asked questions

**Can I eat rice and still lose weight?**
Yes. Weight loss comes from the total calorie balance, not from one food. Keep rice to a measured cup and pair it with protein and vegetables.

**Is intermittent fasting better?**
It works for some people because it limits eating time. It is not magic. The calorie deficit is what causes fat loss.

**Why am I not losing weight even though I eat healthy?**
Usually hidden oil, larger portions than you think, liquid calories (sweet coffee, juices), or weekend overeating. Our [calories in South Indian food](/blog/calories-in-south-indian-food) guide helps you spot them.

**Should I walk or go to the gym?**
Both help. Walking (8,000–10,000 steps) burns extra calories, and strength training helps you keep muscle as you lose fat.
