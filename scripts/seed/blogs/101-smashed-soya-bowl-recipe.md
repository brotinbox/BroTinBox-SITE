> **Quick answer:** For a home-style **smashed soya bowl**, boil **50 g dry soya chunks per person** for 5 minutes, squeeze out the water, then roughly **smash or pulse** them and stir-fry with **onion, tomato, ginger-garlic, green chilli, pepper, garam masala and 1 tsp oil**. Finish with lemon and coriander. Each 50 g (dry) serving gives about **25–26 g protein for about 200–230 kcal**, one of the cheapest high-protein veg meals you can make.

Soya chunks are a protein powerhouse, but many people find them rubbery. Smashing them changes everything: they soak up masala like keema and cook in minutes. This is a home-style version of the **Smashed Soya Bowl** on our veg menu.

# Ingredients (serves 2)

**Soya**

- 100 g dry soya chunks (or granules)
- Water to boil, with a pinch of salt

**Masala**

- 1 tsp oil
- ½ tsp cumin seeds
- 1 medium onion, finely chopped
- 1 tsp ginger-garlic paste
- 1–2 green chillies, chopped
- 1 medium tomato, finely chopped
- ¼ tsp turmeric
- 1 tsp Kashmiri chilli powder
- 1 tsp coriander powder
- ½ tsp black pepper powder
- ½ tsp garam masala
- Salt to taste

**To finish**

- Juice of ½ lemon
- Chopped coriander and a few mint leaves
- Optional: ½ cup green peas or chopped capsicum

# Method

## 1. Boil and squeeze

- Boil water with a pinch of salt. Add soya chunks and boil **5 minutes**.
- Drain and rinse in cold water.
- **Squeeze out as much water as you can**, handful by handful. This removes the "soya smell" and helps them absorb flavour.

## 2. Smash

Pulse the squeezed chunks in a mixer **2–3 times** for a coarse, keema-like texture, or smash them with a heavy bowl. Do not make a paste.

## 3. Cook the masala

- Heat oil, add cumin seeds.
- Add onion and cook until golden.
- Add ginger-garlic paste and green chilli, cook 1 minute.
- Add tomato and the powders, cook until soft and the oil separates.

## 4. Add soya

- Add the smashed soya (and peas or capsicum if using).
- Stir-fry on medium-high for **5–6 minutes** so it browns lightly and soaks up the masala.
- Add a splash of water if it sticks.

## 5. Finish

Turn off the heat. Add lemon juice, coriander and mint.

# Nutrition per serving

For 50 g dry soya per person:

- **Calories:** about 200–230 kcal
- **Protein:** about 25–26 g
- **Carbs:** about 18–20 g
- **Fibre:** about 6–7 g
- **Fat:** about 3–5 g

That is more protein than 4 eggs, for fewer calories. See [soya chunks protein](/blog/soya-chunks-protein).

# Is soya safe to eat often?

Yes, for most people, in normal food amounts. A serving of 30–50 g dry soya a day is common. Studies have not shown that normal soya intake lowers testosterone in men. People with a soy allergy should avoid it, and people with thyroid conditions should take their medicine as prescribed and keep soya intake consistent.

# Tips

- **Granules cook faster** than chunks and need no smashing.
- **Do not skip the squeeze;** watery soya tastes bland.
- **Make ahead:** keeps 2 days in the fridge. Great for [veg lunch boxes](/blog/high-protein-veg-lunch-box-ideas).
- **Spicy version:** add Chettinad masala or extra pepper.

# Frequently asked questions

**How much soya can I eat a day?**
About 30–50 g dry is a common, safe amount for most adults.

**Why do soya chunks taste rubbery?**
They are under-squeezed or over-boiled. Boil 5 minutes, squeeze hard, then smash.

**Is soya good for weight loss?**
Yes. It is high in protein and fibre and low in fat.
