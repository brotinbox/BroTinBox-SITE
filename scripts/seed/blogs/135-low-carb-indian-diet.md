> **Quick answer:** A **low-carb Indian diet** usually means about **50–130 g of carbs a day** (not the 20–50 g of keto), with **more protein and vegetables** and **smaller portions of rice, chapati, idli and dosa**. A simple version: **one small carb portion per meal** (½–1 cup rice or 1–2 chapatis), **protein at every meal**, half the plate vegetables, and **no sugar, juices or sweets** most days. It can help weight loss and blood sugar for many people, and it is much easier to follow than keto. People with **diabetes on medicines** should plan it with their doctor.

Most Indian meals are carb-heavy: rice, chapati, idli, dosa, upma, poha. A low-carb approach does not mean giving them up. It means **less of them, and more protein and vegetables**. Here is how.

# How low is "low carb"?

- **Typical Indian diet:** often 250–400 g carbs a day
- **Moderate low carb:** about 100–130 g a day
- **Low carb:** about 50–100 g a day
- **Keto:** under about 20–50 g a day. See [keto diet for Indians](/blog/keto-diet-indian).

For most people, **moderate low carb (100–130 g)** gives the benefits without the struggle.

# Carbs in common Indian foods

- **1 cup cooked rice (150 g):** about 40–42 g
- **1 chapati (no ghee):** about 18–20 g
- **1 idli:** about 12 g
- **1 plain dosa:** about 20–25 g
- **1 cup cooked dal:** about 20–25 g (plus 9–10 g protein)
- **1 banana:** about 25 g
- **1 tsp sugar:** about 5 g

# What to eat more of

- **Protein:** eggs, chicken, fish, paneer, tofu, soya, curd
- **Non-starchy vegetables:** greens (keerai), cabbage, beans, cauliflower, capsicum, cucumber, brinjal, ladies finger, mushroom
- **Healthy fats, in measure:** nuts, seeds, a little coconut, oil

# What to eat less of

- **Large rice portions**
- **Multiple chapatis, parottas, puris**
- **Sugar, sweets, juices, soft drinks**
- **Bakery items and biscuits**
- **Potato-heavy dishes** in large amounts

# A low-carb Indian day (about 100–120 g carbs, 1,600 kcal)

**Breakfast**

- 3-egg vegetable omelette or paneer bhurji
- 1 chapati or 1 small dosa

**Lunch**

- ½–¾ cup rice
- 150 g chicken, fish, paneer or tofu
- Large serving of poriyal and salad
- Curd

**Snack**

- Buttermilk and a handful of roasted chana or peanuts

**Dinner**

- 1 chapati or millet roti
- Dal or egg curry
- Vegetables
- Salad

**Vegetarian?** Use paneer, tofu, soya and curd for protein. Dal and chana add carbs as well as protein, so keep portions moderate.

# Benefits

- **Weight loss:** often easier because protein and vegetables are filling
- **Blood sugar:** smaller carb portions mean smaller spikes, which helps many people with prediabetes or type 2 diabetes (with medical guidance)
- **Less snacking:** fewer sugar crashes

# Downsides to watch

- **Low fibre** if you cut carbs but not add vegetables
- **Low energy for hard training;** athletes may need more carbs around workouts
- **Social eating** can be harder
- **Medication changes:** people on diabetes medicines may need dose adjustments. Do not start without your doctor.

# Low carb vs simply eating less

Studies comparing low-carb and other diets with **similar calories** usually find **similar fat loss** over the long term. Low carb works best for people who **find it easier to control hunger** this way. See [types of diet](/blog/types-of-diet).

# Tips

- **Start by halving your rice,** not removing it
- **Fill the empty space with vegetables**
- **Protein first** at every meal
- **Watch hidden carbs:** sugar in tea, sauces, packaged snacks
- **Keep fruit,** just 1–2 portions a day

# Frequently asked questions

**Can I eat rice on a low-carb diet?**
Yes, in a small portion of about ½–¾ cup.

**Is chapati low carb?**
No, but 1–2 chapatis fit a moderate low-carb plan.

**Is low carb safe long term?**
A moderate low-carb, high-vegetable diet is generally safe for healthy people. Very low carb needs more care.
