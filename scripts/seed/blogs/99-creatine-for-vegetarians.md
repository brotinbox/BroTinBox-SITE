> **Quick answer:** **Yes, creatine is suitable for vegetarians, and they often benefit more than meat-eaters.** Supplement creatine monohydrate is **made synthetically**, not from meat, so it is vegetarian and usually vegan. Because vegetarians get **almost no creatine from food**, their muscle stores start lower, and studies often show **bigger gains in strength and lean mass** when they supplement. Take **3–5 g a day**. Check the capsule shell, since some use gelatin.

India has more vegetarian gym-goers than almost any country. Many wonder if creatine is "non-veg" or whether it works without meat in the diet. Here are the answers.

# Is creatine vegetarian?

**Yes.** Creatine is naturally found in meat and fish, but **supplement creatine is made in factories** from simple chemicals (sarcosine and cyanamide). No animal products are used.

Watch out for:

- **Capsules:** many use **gelatin**, which is from animals. Choose powder or veg capsules.
- **Flavoured blends:** check for other ingredients you avoid.
- **Labels:** look for the **green veg symbol** on Indian packs.

# Why vegetarians may benefit more

- Your body makes about **1 g of creatine a day** on its own.
- Meat-eaters get another **0.5–1 g a day** from food.
- Vegetarians get **almost none** from food. See [foods that contain creatine](/blog/foods-that-contain-creatine).

As a result, vegetarians usually have **lower muscle creatine stores**. When they supplement, their stores rise more, and studies often show:

- **Bigger strength gains**
- **More lean mass gain** with training
- Possibly some **brain benefits** (memory and mental tiredness), which appear more often in vegetarians in research

# How to take it

- **Creatine monohydrate, 3–5 g a day,** every day
- Mix in **water, milk, buttermilk or a shake**
- **Timing:** any time; after training with a meal is easy
- **Loading:** optional (about 20 g a day for 5–7 days)

Full details in [how to take creatine](/blog/how-to-take-creatine).

# Is it safe?

For healthy adults, creatine monohydrate at normal doses is one of the safest supplements studied.

- **Water weight:** 1–2 kg in the first weeks, inside the muscles. Not fat.
- **Kidneys:** no damage found in healthy people at normal doses. If you have kidney disease, talk to your doctor first. See [does creatine cause kidney problems](/blog/does-creatine-cause-kidney-problems).

# Get your protein right too

Creatine helps you train harder, but muscles are built from **protein**. Vegetarians often fall short.

Aim for **1.6–2.2 g of protein per kg** if you are training for muscle. Good veg sources:

- **Paneer:** about 18–20 g per 100 g
- **Soya chunks:** about 50 g per 100 g dry
- **Tofu:** about 12–17 g per 100 g (firm)
- **Curd and milk:** about 3–4 g per 100 g
- **Dal, chana, rajma:** about 7–9 g per 100 g cooked
- **Whey protein:** about 20–25 g per scoop (made from milk)

See [paneer vs tofu](/blog/paneer-vs-tofu) and [high-protein veg lunch box ideas](/blog/high-protein-veg-lunch-box-ideas).

# Other nutrients vegetarians should watch

- **Vitamin B12:** very common deficiency; get tested
- **Iron:** pair greens and dal with vitamin C
- **Vitamin D:** often low across India
- **Omega-3:** walnuts, flaxseed, chia (or algae-based supplements)

# Creatine vs whey for vegetarians

If your protein intake is low, **whey or more protein-rich food** should come first. If your protein is already good, **creatine** gives you more benefit per rupee. Many vegetarian lifters use both. Read [creatine vs whey](/blog/creatine-vs-whey).

# Frequently asked questions

**Is creatine allowed for Jains?**
The powder itself is synthetic, but practices vary. Check the full ingredient list and your own guidelines.

**Will creatine work without meat in my diet?**
Yes, and often better, because your starting levels are lower.

**Can vegetarian women take creatine?**
Yes. Same dose, similar benefits.
