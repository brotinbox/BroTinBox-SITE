> **Quick answer:** **Losing 20 kg in 60 days is not safe or realistic** for almost anyone without medical treatment. **Losing 10 kg in 60 days** is possible mainly for people starting at about **110–120 kg or more**, at around 1–1.2 kg a week. For most people, a safe 60-day target is **4–8 kg**. If you have a lot of weight to lose, plan for **6–12 months** and get support from a doctor, especially if you have diabetes, high blood pressure or other conditions.

Big goals are great. But "lose 20 kg in 2 months" plans usually end in muscle loss, exhaustion and regain. Here is what is actually possible and how to plan a big weight loss the right way.

# The maths

- **1 kg of fat ≈ 7,700 kcal**
- **10 kg of fat in 60 days:** about **1,280 kcal deficit a day**
- **20 kg of fat in 60 days:** about **2,570 kcal deficit a day**

A daily deficit of 2,500+ kcal is more than most people burn in a whole day. It would mean eating almost nothing for two months.

# What is safe?

A common guideline is **0.5–1% of body weight a week**. Over 60 days (about 8.5 weeks):

- **80 kg:** about 3.5–7 kg
- **100 kg:** about 4–8.5 kg
- **120 kg:** about 5–10 kg
- **140 kg:** about 6–12 kg

Add 1–3 kg of early water loss. So **10 kg in 60 days** is realistic only for people with a high starting weight. **20 kg** is not.

# When does very fast loss happen?

- **Bariatric surgery,** under a surgical team
- **Very low calorie diets (around 800 kcal)** with complete meal-replacement formulas, **supervised by a doctor**
- **Starting weight of 150 kg or more,** where early losses are larger

Even in these cases, the plan is monitored with regular blood tests.

# Risks of losing weight too fast

- **Muscle loss:** a slower metabolism and weaker body
- **Gallstones:** a known risk of rapid weight loss
- **Hair fall:** often shows 2–4 months later
- **Low energy, dizziness, low blood pressure**
- **Missed periods**
- **Nutrient deficiencies**
- **Regain:** most extreme dieters regain much of the weight

Read [why the scale stops moving](/blog/weight-loss-plateau-why-scale-stopped) to understand how the body fights back after very low eating.

# A better plan for big weight loss

## First 60 days: a strong, safe start

- **Calories:** 500–1,000 kcal below maintenance (the larger deficit only if you are very heavy), never below about 1,200 kcal (women) or 1,500 kcal (men) without medical supervision
- **Protein:** 1.2–1.6 g per kg of **target** body weight
- **Steps:** start where you are and add 1,000 a day each week, up to 8,000–10,000
- **Strength training:** 2–3 times a week, even simple bodyweight exercises
- **Sleep:** 7–8 hours
- **Health check:** blood sugar, blood pressure, thyroid, lipids, liver

**Realistic 60-day result for someone at 110–120 kg: 8–10 kg.**

## Months 3–6: keep going

- Adjust calories every 4–6 weeks as you get lighter
- Add more movement as fitness improves
- Expect 0.5–1 kg a week

## Months 6–12: reach and hold

Many people lose **15–25 kg in 6–12 months** this way, and keep it off far better than with crash diets.

# When to get medical help

See a doctor before starting if you:

- Have **diabetes, high blood pressure, heart, kidney or liver disease**
- Take regular medicines (doses may need to change as you lose weight)
- Have a **BMI over 35**
- Have tried many times and regained

Doctors can also discuss newer weight loss medicines and surgery when appropriate.

# Frequently asked questions

**Can I lose 15 kg in 2 months?**
Only if you start very heavy, and ideally with medical supervision. For most people, 15 kg takes 4–8 months.

**Is it okay to lose 2 kg a week?**
In the first 1–2 weeks it is common (much of it is water). After that, 2 kg a week is too fast for most people unless supervised.

**Will walking alone help me lose 10 kg?**
Walking helps a lot, but diet does most of the work. Combine both.
