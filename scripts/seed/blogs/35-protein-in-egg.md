> **Quick answer:** **One large egg (about 50 g) has about 6 g of protein** and about 70–75 kcal. The **egg white** has about **3.5 g** of protein and the **yolk** has about **2.7 g**. Most Indian eggs are a little smaller (45–50 g), so count roughly **5.5–6 g of protein per egg**. Boiling, poaching or making an omelette does not change the protein.

Eggs are the cheapest, easiest high-quality protein most of us can buy. But how much protein does one egg actually give you, and is it worth throwing away the yolk?

# Protein in one egg

For one **large egg (about 50 g without shell)**:

- **Protein:** about 6.3 g
- **Calories:** about 72 kcal
- **Fat:** about 5 g
- **Carbs:** under 1 g

Indian eggs vary in size. A typical egg from the shop is about 45–50 g, so:

- **Small egg (about 40 g):** about 5 g protein
- **Medium egg (about 45 g):** about 5.5–5.7 g protein
- **Large egg (about 50 g):** about 6–6.3 g protein

# Egg white vs whole egg

From one large egg:

- **Egg white:** about 3.6 g protein, 17 kcal, almost no fat
- **Egg yolk:** about 2.7 g protein, 55 kcal, about 4.5 g fat

So the white has a little more than half the protein, and the yolk has almost half too. The yolk also carries most of the vitamins: vitamin A, vitamin D, B12, choline, and healthy fats.

**Should you skip the yolk?**
For most healthy people, no. Whole eggs are more filling and more nutritious. If you are on a strict calorie limit, a common approach is 2 whole eggs plus 2–3 extra whites. That gives a lot of protein for fewer calories.

Worried about cholesterol? Read [how many eggs a day is safe](/blog/how-many-eggs-a-day-is-safe). For most healthy people, the research is far more relaxed about yolks than it was 20 years ago.

# Protein in eggs, by how many you eat

Using about 6 g per egg:

- **2 eggs:** about 12 g protein, 145 kcal
- **3 eggs:** about 18 g protein, 215 kcal
- **4 eggs:** about 24 g protein, 290 kcal
- **6 eggs:** about 36 g protein, 430 kcal
- **4 egg whites:** about 14 g protein, 70 kcal

# Does cooking change the protein?

Boiled, poached, scrambled or omelette: the protein stays the same. What changes is:

- **Oil or butter added:** a one-egg omelette with a teaspoon of oil adds about 40 kcal.
- **Digestibility:** cooked egg protein is absorbed much better than raw egg protein. A study found the body uses about 90% of cooked egg protein but only about 50–60% from raw eggs.

So skip the "raw egg shake" from old movies. Cooked is better, and safer.

# Are eggs a "complete" protein?

Yes. Eggs contain all nine essential amino acids in good amounts. They are often used as the reference standard when scientists compare protein quality. Eggs are also rich in leucine, the amino acid that signals your muscles to grow.

# How to use eggs in an Indian diet

- **Breakfast:** 3-egg onion omelette with 2 chapatis or a dosa
- **Lunch:** egg curry, or 2 boiled eggs on the side of your rice meal
- **Snack:** 2 boiled eggs with pepper and salt
- **Dinner:** egg bhurji with vegetables and chapati
- **Post-workout:** 3 boiled eggs and a banana

# Eggs vs other protein sources

Per 100 g, eggs have about 12–13 g of protein. That is less than chicken breast (about 31 g cooked) or paneer (about 18–20 g), but eggs are cheap, need almost no cooking, and are easy to carry.

# Frequently asked questions

**How much protein is in 2 boiled eggs?**
About 11–13 g, depending on egg size.

**How much protein is in one egg white?**
About 3.5 g.

**Is it okay to eat eggs every day?**
For most healthy people, yes. 2–3 whole eggs a day fits easily into a balanced diet.

**Brown eggs or white eggs: which has more protein?**
Shell colour depends on the hen's breed. The protein is the same.

**Country eggs (naatu muttai) vs farm eggs?**
Country eggs are often smaller, so each one may have slightly less protein. The quality of the protein is similar.
