> **Quick answer:** The most common types of diet are **balanced, high-protein, low-carb, keto, intermittent fasting, vegetarian, vegan, Mediterranean, DASH, low-fat, liquid or meal-replacement, and calorie or macro counting**. For weight loss, the one that works best is the one that keeps you in a **calorie deficit with enough protein** that you can follow for months. For most Indians, a **balanced, high-protein version of your normal food** is the easiest place to start.

There are dozens of diets online, each claiming to be the best. Most of them work in the same way, just with different rules. Here are 12 popular diets explained simply, with who each one suits.

# 1. Balanced diet

**What it is:** all food groups in sensible portions: grains, protein, vegetables, fruit, dairy and healthy fats.

**Indian version:** half the plate vegetables, a quarter protein (dal, eggs, chicken, fish, paneer), a quarter rice or chapati, plus curd.

**Best for:** almost everyone, long term.

# 2. High-protein diet

**What it is:** more protein than usual, often 1.2–2.2 g per kg of body weight.

**Why it works:** protein keeps you full, protects muscle during weight loss and helps you build muscle with training.

**Best for:** weight loss, muscle gain, gym-goers. See [how much protein per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day).

# 3. Low-carb diet

**What it is:** fewer carbs, usually under about 100–150 g a day, with more protein, vegetables and fats.

**Indian version:** smaller portions of rice and chapati, more eggs, chicken, paneer, vegetables and dal.

**Best for:** people who overeat carbs, some people with diabetes (with medical guidance).

# 4. Keto diet

**What it is:** very low carb (often under 20–50 g a day) and high fat, so your body runs on ketones.

**Pros:** fast early weight loss, reduced appetite for some people.

**Cons:** very hard with Indian food (no rice, chapati, dal or most fruit), low fibre, and not suitable for people with some medical conditions.

**Best for:** a few people who enjoy it, ideally with medical guidance.

# 5. Intermittent fasting

**What it is:** eating within a time window, such as **16:8** (eat in 8 hours, fast for 16) or **5:2** (two low-calorie days a week).

**Why it works:** a shorter eating window often means fewer calories.

**Best for:** people who are not hungry in the morning or who snack late at night. Not ideal for people with diabetes on medication without a doctor's advice. Related: [eating late at night and weight loss](/blog/eating-late-at-night-weight-loss).

# 6. Vegetarian diet

**What it is:** no meat or fish; dairy and often eggs are included.

**Watch:** protein, vitamin B12 and iron. Paneer, curd, dal, soya and tofu help. See [protein in dal, chana and rajma](/blog/protein-in-dal-chana-rajma).

# 7. Vegan diet

**What it is:** no animal foods at all, including dairy and eggs.

**Watch:** protein, B12, calcium, iron, omega-3 and vitamin D. A B12 supplement is usually needed.

# 8. Mediterranean diet

**What it is:** vegetables, fruit, whole grains, legumes, nuts, olive oil and fish, with little red meat and sugar.

**Why it is popular:** strong research for heart health.

**Indian version:** more vegetables and dal, fish twice a week, nuts, less fried food and sweets.

# 9. DASH diet

**What it is:** designed to lower blood pressure. Rich in vegetables, fruit, low-fat dairy, whole grains and legumes, with low salt.

**Best for:** people with high blood pressure.

# 10. Low-fat diet

**What it is:** limiting fat, especially fried food, ghee, butter and full-fat dairy.

**Note:** cutting oil is one of the easiest ways to cut calories in Indian cooking. 1 tbsp oil is about 120 kcal.

# 11. Liquid and meal-replacement diets

**What it is:** shakes, soups or juices instead of some or all meals.

**Reality:** fast scale drop, but often regained. Read [what is a liquid diet](/blog/what-is-liquid-diet) and [does a liquid diet reduce weight](/blog/does-liquid-diet-reduce-weight).

# 12. Calorie and macro counting

**What it is:** tracking calories and protein, carbs and fat, without banning foods. Often called "IIFYM" (if it fits your macros).

**Best for:** people who like numbers and want flexibility. See [how to read nutrition labels](/blog/how-to-read-nutrition-labels-india).

# Which diet is best for weight loss?

Studies comparing popular diets find that **most produce similar weight loss** when calories are similar. What matters more:

- A **calorie deficit** you can keep up
- **Enough protein**
- **Foods you enjoy** and can eat with family
- A plan you can **follow for months**, not days

Avoid extreme diets that promise very fast results. They usually end in a [weight loss plateau](/blog/weight-loss-plateau-why-scale-stopped) or regain.

# Which diet is best for muscle gain?

A **high-protein, balanced diet** with a small calorie surplus. See [how to gain muscle in 30 days](/blog/how-to-gain-muscle-in-30-days).

# Frequently asked questions

**What is the healthiest type of diet?**
Balanced, mostly whole-food diets like the Mediterranean or a well-planned Indian diet have the strongest evidence.

**Can I combine diets?**
Yes. Many people do a high-protein diet with intermittent fasting, or a vegetarian diet with calorie counting.

**Do I need a dietitian?**
If you have a medical condition like diabetes, kidney disease or PCOS, yes, get personal advice.
