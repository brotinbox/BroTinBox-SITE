> **Quick answer:** **In healthy people, creatine at the normal dose of 3–5 g a day has not been shown to cause kidney problems.** The confusion comes from **creatinine**, a waste product that blood tests use to check kidney function. Creatine can raise creatinine a little, which can **look** like a kidney problem on a report even when the kidneys are fine. If you **already have kidney disease**, diabetes with kidney involvement, or take medicines that affect the kidneys, do not take creatine without your doctor's advice.

"Creatine will damage your kidneys" is one of the most common warnings in Indian gyms and family WhatsApp groups. It sounds serious, so it deserves a clear answer.

# Where the fear comes from

There are three reasons people link creatine to kidney damage:

1. **The names sound alike.** Creatine (the supplement) and creatinine (the waste product) are different things.
2. **Blood reports.** People taking creatine sometimes see a higher creatinine level and get worried.
3. **A few case reports.** A small number of reports over the years described kidney problems in people taking creatine, often with very high doses, other supplements, or existing kidney disease.

# Creatine vs creatinine

- **Creatine:** stored in your muscles, used for quick energy.
- **Creatinine:** a waste product made when creatine breaks down. Your kidneys filter it out.

Doctors use **blood creatinine** to estimate how well your kidneys filter (eGFR). Normally, more creatinine in the blood means the kidneys are filtering less.

But when you take creatine, your body makes a bit more creatinine **simply because there is more creatine around**. The kidneys are working normally; there is just more to filter. A lot of muscle mass and a high-protein diet can do the same.

So the number can go up **without any kidney damage**.

# What the research shows

Many studies have looked at this directly:

- Studies in healthy adults taking 3–5 g a day for months to years have **not found kidney damage**.
- Studies using more accurate kidney tests (not just creatinine) have found **normal kidney function** in creatine users.
- The **International Society of Sports Nutrition** reviewed the evidence and concluded creatine monohydrate is safe for healthy people at recommended doses.

That said, research in people **with existing kidney disease** is limited. That is why they should avoid it unless a doctor says otherwise.

# Who should not take creatine without a doctor

- **Kidney disease** of any stage, or a single kidney
- **Diabetes**, especially if your kidneys are affected
- **High blood pressure** that is not controlled
- Regular use of **painkillers (NSAIDs)**, some blood pressure medicines, or other drugs that affect the kidneys
- **Pregnancy or breastfeeding**

# If your blood test shows high creatinine

Do not panic, and do not hide your supplement from your doctor.

- **Tell your doctor** you take creatine and train with weights.
- Your doctor may ask you to **stop creatine for 3–4 weeks** and retest.
- They may order a **cystatin C test**, which is not affected by creatine or muscle mass.
- A **urine test** for protein (albumin) helps check for real kidney damage.

If your kidneys are healthy, the numbers usually settle once you stop.

# How to take creatine safely

- Use plain **creatine monohydrate**
- Take **3–5 g a day**; there is no need for mega doses
- Drink water normally through the day
- Buy from trusted sellers with an **FSSAI licence** and batch number on the pack
- Avoid "stacks" with unknown ingredients
- Get a basic **kidney function test** before starting if you are over 40 or have any risk factors

# Other ways to protect your kidneys

The real risks for kidneys in India are not creatine. They are:

- **Uncontrolled diabetes**
- **High blood pressure**
- **Overuse of painkillers**
- **Dehydration**, especially in hot weather
- **Unverified supplements** and fat burners

Keep your sugar and blood pressure in check, and get regular health checks.

# Frequently asked questions

**Does creatine affect kidneys in the long term?**
Long-term studies in healthy people at normal doses have not found kidney damage.

**Can I take creatine with a high-protein diet?**
Yes. A high-protein diet is safe for healthy kidneys. If you have kidney disease, both need your doctor's guidance.

**Should I drink extra water on creatine?**
Drink normally and stay well hydrated, especially in Coimbatore's summer. You do not need litres extra.
