> **Quick answer:** Easy homemade protein meals with Indian food include **egg bhurji with chapati (about 25 g protein)**, **grilled chicken with rice and dal (45–55 g)**, **paneer tikka with chana (35–40 g)**, **soya chunk pulao (30–35 g)**, **fish curry meals (30–35 g)**, **sprouts and curd bowls (20–25 g)** and **dal-heavy thalis with curd (20–25 g)**. Aim for **25–40 g of protein per main meal**, and build every plate around one main protein plus a legume or dairy side.

You do not need protein powder or expensive "health food" to eat high protein. Normal Indian ingredients can easily give you 100+ g a day. Here are 15 simple homemade protein meals, with approximate protein for each.

# How to build a protein meal

Every meal below follows the same formula:

1. **One main protein:** eggs, chicken, fish, paneer, tofu or soya
2. **One protein side:** dal, chana, rajma, sprouts or curd
3. **Vegetables or salad**
4. **A measured carb:** rice, chapati, dosa, millets or oats

# Breakfast

## 1. Egg bhurji with chapati

3 eggs scrambled with onion, tomato and pepper, 2 chapatis.
**About 25 g protein.**

## 2. Moong dal chilla with curd

3 chillas made from soaked green gram, with a bowl of curd.
**About 20–25 g protein.**

## 3. Paneer stuffed dosa

2 dosas filled with 75 g grated paneer and vegetables, with sambar.
**About 20–22 g protein.**

## 4. Oats with milk, peanuts and banana

½ cup oats cooked in 300 ml milk, a spoon of peanut butter, banana.
**About 20 g protein.**

See more ideas in [high-protein Indian breakfast ideas](/blog/high-protein-indian-breakfast-ideas).

# Lunch

## 5. Grilled chicken, rice, dal and salad

150 g grilled chicken, 1 cup rice, dal, salad.
**About 45–55 g protein.**
Try our [mint grilled chicken recipe](/blog/mint-grilled-chicken-recipe).

## 6. Fish curry meals

150 g fish (mackerel, sardine or seer) curry, 1 cup rice, poriyal, curd.
**About 30–35 g protein.**
Or try [grilled mackerel](/blog/grilled-mackerel-recipe).

## 7. Soya chunk pulao with raita

50 g dry soya chunks cooked in a vegetable pulao, with a cup of curd raita.
**About 30–35 g protein.**

## 8. Rajma or chana with rice and curd

1.5 cups rajma or chana curry, 1 cup rice, curd.
**About 20–25 g protein.**

## 9. Egg curry with chapati and sprouts

3-egg curry, 2 chapatis, a cup of sprouts salad.
**About 28–30 g protein.**

# Dinner

## 10. Paneer tikka with chana and chapati

125 g paneer tikka, a bowl of chana, 2 chapatis.
**About 35–40 g protein.**
See our [tandoori paneer tikka recipe](/blog/tandoori-paneer-tikka-recipe).

## 11. Tofu bhurji with millet roti

150 g firm tofu scrambled with spices, 2 ragi or jowar rotis.
**About 25 g protein.**

## 12. Chicken and vegetable stir-fry with chapati

150 g chicken breast strips with capsicum, beans and onion, 2 chapatis.
**About 45 g protein.**

## 13. Pepper chicken with dosa

150 g dry pepper chicken with 2 dosas.
**About 40 g protein.**

# Snacks and mini meals

## 14. Sprouts and curd bowl

1 cup sprouted green gram with onion, tomato and lemon, a cup of curd.
**About 20–25 g protein.**

## 15. Kollu sundal with buttermilk

1 cup horse gram sundal with a glass of buttermilk.
**About 15–18 g protein.**
Read about [kollu benefits](/blog/kollu-horse-gram-benefits-weight-loss).

# A full high-protein day from this list

- **Breakfast:** egg bhurji with chapati (25 g)
- **Lunch:** grilled chicken, rice, dal and salad (50 g)
- **Evening:** sprouts and curd bowl (20 g)
- **Dinner:** paneer tikka with chana and chapati (35 g)

**Total: about 130 g protein.**

Vegetarian? Swap chicken for soya chunks or tofu, and eggs for moong dal chilla. See [protein in dal, chana and rajma](/blog/protein-in-dal-chana-rajma).

# Tips for homemade protein meals

- **Cook protein in bulk:** grill 500 g chicken or boil 10 eggs for the week
- **Keep easy proteins at home:** eggs, curd, paneer, soya chunks, roasted chana, peanuts
- **Measure oil:** grilling, roasting and pressure cooking keep calories in check
- **Weigh once:** weigh your usual portions a few times so you know what 150 g looks like

# Frequently asked questions

**How much protein should one homemade meal have?**
About 25–40 g for most adults. See [how much protein per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day).

**Can vegetarians get enough protein from home food?**
Yes, with paneer, tofu, soya, dal, chana, curd and milk at every meal.

**Is homemade protein better than protein powder?**
Food gives you more nutrients and fibre. Powder is just convenient. See [protein powder vs real food](/blog/protein-powder-vs-real-food-india).
