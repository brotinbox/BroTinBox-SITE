> **Quick answer:** Per 100 g, **peanuts** have about **25–26 g protein and 570 kcal**, while **almonds** have about **21 g protein and 580 kcal**. Peanuts give **more protein for much less money**. Almonds have **more fibre, vitamin E, calcium and magnesium**. Both are healthy, calorie-dense foods; a **30 g handful** (about 170 kcal) is a sensible daily portion. For **muscle gain on a budget**, peanuts win. For **micronutrients**, almonds edge ahead. Eating either is better than eating neither.

Almonds have a "premium health food" image in India, while peanuts (verkadalai) are the everyday snack. Here is how they really compare.

# Nutrition per 100 g (raw, approx.)

**Peanuts** (technically a legume)

- Calories: about 567 kcal
- Protein: about 25–26 g
- Fat: about 49 g (mostly unsaturated)
- Carbs: about 16 g
- Fibre: about 8–9 g
- Rich in: niacin, folate, magnesium

**Almonds** (a tree nut)

- Calories: about 579 kcal
- Protein: about 21 g
- Fat: about 50 g (mostly monounsaturated)
- Carbs: about 22 g
- Fibre: about 12 g
- Rich in: vitamin E, magnesium, calcium, riboflavin

# Per 30 g handful

**Peanuts:** about 170 kcal, 7.5–8 g protein, 2.5 g fibre
**Almonds:** about 175 kcal, 6 g protein, 3.5 g fibre

# Protein: peanuts win

Peanuts have about **20% more protein** than almonds. For vegetarians trying to add protein, they are one of the cheapest options. See [high-protein Indian snacks](/blog/high-protein-indian-snacks).

# Micronutrients: almonds win

Almonds are one of the best food sources of **vitamin E** (an antioxidant), and have more **calcium and fibre**.

# Cost: peanuts win easily

Almonds usually cost **several times more per kg** than peanuts. For the same budget, you get far more protein from peanuts.

# Heart health: both are good

Studies link regular nut and peanut intake with **better heart health**, partly due to their unsaturated fats, fibre and plant compounds. Both can fit a heart-healthy diet in moderate portions.

# Weight loss: portion is everything

Both are **calorie-dense**: a few extra handfuls a day can add 300–500 kcal without you noticing.

- **Measure a 25–30 g portion** instead of eating from the packet
- Choose **plain roasted or boiled** peanuts over fried, salted or masala versions
- **Boiled peanuts** (common in Tamil Nadu) have fewer calories per bowl because of the water

# Muscle gain: peanuts are great

- High protein and calories for the price
- **Peanut butter** makes it easy to add calories. See [peanut butter for muscle gain](/blog/peanut-butter-for-muscle-gain).

# Soaked almonds: are they better?

Soaking softens almonds and may make them easier to digest for some people. Claims that soaking dramatically increases nutrients are overstated. Eat them the way you like.

# Allergies

**Peanut and tree nut allergies** can be serious. If you have any nut allergy, avoid both unless your doctor says otherwise.

# Quality tips

- **Peanuts:** buy fresh; discard any that look mouldy or taste bitter (mould can produce aflatoxins)
- **Almonds:** buy from trusted shops; check for rancid smell
- **Store both** in airtight containers, in a cool place or the fridge

# The smart choice

- **Daily protein snack:** peanuts or boiled peanuts
- **Micronutrient boost:** a few almonds
- **Best:** a mixed handful of both, plus other nuts and seeds like walnuts and flaxseed

# Frequently asked questions

**How many almonds a day?**
About 20–25 almonds (about 30 g) is a common healthy portion.

**Are peanuts good for weight loss?**
Yes, in a measured handful. They are filling because of protein, fat and fibre.

**Are peanuts fattening?**
Only if you eat large amounts. Count them like any other food.
