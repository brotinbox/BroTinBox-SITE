> **Quick answer:** Adults over about **60–65** usually need **more protein than younger adults**, around **1.0–1.2 g per kg of body weight a day** for healthy older people, and sometimes more during illness or recovery (as advised by a doctor). Spread it as **about 25–30 g per meal** at breakfast, lunch and dinner, and combine it with **simple strength exercises 2–3 times a week**. This helps slow **age-related muscle loss (sarcopenia)**, protects strength and balance, and lowers the risk of falls. People with **kidney disease** should follow their doctor's protein advice.

As we age, we naturally lose muscle, often without noticing until climbing stairs, getting up from a chair or carrying shopping becomes hard. Protein and movement can slow this a lot. Here is a practical guide for older adults and families caring for them.

# Why protein matters more with age

- **Muscle loss (sarcopenia):** adults gradually lose muscle from middle age, faster after 60–65
- **Anabolic resistance:** older muscles respond less to protein, so they need a bit more per meal
- **Lower appetite:** many older people eat less overall, and protein drops first
- **Recovery:** illness, surgery and hospital stays cause rapid muscle loss

Keeping muscle means better **strength, balance, independence** and fewer **falls and fractures**.

# How much protein?

Expert groups on ageing commonly suggest:

- **Healthy older adults:** about **1.0–1.2 g per kg** a day
- **Older adults who are ill or recovering:** often **1.2–1.5 g per kg**, as advised by a doctor
- **Kidney disease:** **follow your doctor's advice;** protein may need to be limited

**Example:** a 60 kg older adult needs about **60–72 g protein a day**, compared with about 48 g at the basic adult level.

Many older Indians eat far less than this. See [how much protein per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day).

# Spread it through the day

Older muscles respond best to **about 25–30 g of protein per meal**. A typical Indian pattern puts most protein at lunch and very little at breakfast and dinner. Even it out.

# Easy, soft protein foods for older adults

- **Eggs:** boiled, scrambled, omelette (about 6 g each)
- **Curd and buttermilk:** easy to digest; hung curd is higher protein. See [curd vs Greek yoghurt](/blog/curd-vs-greek-yogurt).
- **Milk:** about 8 g per glass
- **Paneer:** soft, about 18–20 g per 100 g
- **Dal and khichdi:** cook dal thick and add more dal than rice
- **Fish:** soft, easy to chew; mackerel and sardines add omega-3
- **Smashed or shredded chicken:** easy to chew. See [smashed chicken bowl](/blog/smashed-chicken-bowl-recipe).
- **Tofu and soya granules:** soft and easy in curries
- **Sundal and sprouts** (cooked soft)

# A sample day (about 65–75 g protein)

- **Breakfast:** 2 idlis with sambar and 1 boiled egg, or ragi porridge made with milk (about 15–18 g)
- **Mid-morning:** a glass of buttermilk or milk (about 5–8 g)
- **Lunch:** rice with thick dal, 100 g fish or paneer, poriyal, curd (about 25–30 g)
- **Evening:** a cup of soft sundal (about 8 g)
- **Dinner:** 2 soft chapatis or dosa with egg curry or dal and vegetables (about 15–18 g)

# Pair protein with movement

Protein works best with exercise. Simple, safe options (check with a doctor first if you have heart, joint or balance problems):

- **Sit-to-stand** from a chair: 2–3 sets of 8–10
- **Wall push-ups:** 2 sets of 8–10
- **Step-ups** on the lowest stair, holding the rail
- **Light resistance bands** or water bottles for arms
- **Daily walking**
- **Balance practice:** standing on one leg near a wall

# Tips for families

- **Add protein to familiar foods:** extra dal, an egg, a cup of curd
- **Soft textures** if chewing is difficult
- **Smaller, more frequent meals** if appetite is low
- **Check for dental problems** that make eating hard
- **Get vitamin D and B12 checked.** See [vitamin D deficiency in India](/blog/vitamin-d-deficiency-india).

# When to see a doctor

- Unplanned weight loss
- Frequent falls or weakness
- Poor appetite for weeks
- Kidney, liver or heart disease (to set the right protein level)

# Frequently asked questions

**Is high protein safe for older people?**
For healthy kidneys, 1.0–1.2 g per kg is considered safe and helpful. With kidney disease, follow medical advice.

**Can older adults take protein powder?**
Yes, if they struggle to eat enough, but food first. Check with a doctor.

**Is it too late to build muscle after 60?**
No. Older adults can gain strength and muscle with protein and regular strength exercises.
