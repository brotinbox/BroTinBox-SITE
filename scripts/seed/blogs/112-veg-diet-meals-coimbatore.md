A typical veg "meals" plate or tiffin is filling, but often has:

- **Only 12–20 g of protein**
- **Large rice portions**
- **Fried sides** like appalam and vadai

# Option 2: Cook veg diet meals at home

**The veg protein plate**

- **Main protein:** paneer, tofu or soya (100–150 g paneer or tofu, or 50 g dry soya)
- **Legume side:** dal, chana, rajma or sprouts
- **Vegetables or salad:** half the plate
- **Carb:** 1 cup rice or 2 chapatis
- **Curd** on the side

**A sample veg day (about 1,700 kcal, 85–95 g protein)**

- **Breakfast:** moong dal chilla (2) with curd, or 2 idlis with sambar and a cup of sprouts
- **Lunch:** paneer tikka (100 g), 1 cup rice, dal, poriyal, curd
- **Evening:** buttermilk and kollu or chana sundal
- **Dinner:** smashed soya or tofu bhurji, 2 chapatis, vegetables

# Best veg protein sources

- **Soya chunks:** about 50 g protein per 100 g dry; the cheapest veg protein
- **Paneer:** about 18–20 g per 100 g
- **Tofu:** about 12–17 g per 100 g (firm)
- **Dal, chana, rajma:** about 7–9 g per 100 g cooked
- **Curd and milk:** about 3–4 g per 100 g; hung curd about 8–10 g
- **Sprouts and kollu:** about 8–9 g per 100 g sprouted

Compare them in [paneer vs tofu](/blog/paneer-vs-tofu) and [protein in dal, chana and rajma](/blog/protein-in-dal-chana-rajma).

# Nutrients vegetarians should watch

- **Vitamin B12:** often low; get tested
- **Iron:** greens and dal with vitamin C (lemon, amla)
- **Vitamin D:** common deficiency in India
- **Protein:** plan it into every meal
