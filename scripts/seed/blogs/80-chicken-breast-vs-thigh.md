> **Quick answer:** Per 100 g cooked and skinless, **chicken breast** has about **31 g protein, 3–4 g fat and 165 kcal**, while **chicken thigh** has about **25–26 g protein, 8–11 g fat and 180–210 kcal**. **Breast is better for weight loss** and maximum protein per calorie. **Thigh is juicier, more forgiving to cook** and fine for muscle gain or anyone not counting every calorie. Both are excellent protein.

Gym-goers swear by chicken breast. Most Indian home cooks prefer thigh and leg pieces. Who is right? Here is the full comparison.

# Nutrition per 100 g (cooked, skinless)

**Chicken breast**

- Calories: about 165 kcal
- Protein: about 31 g
- Fat: about 3–4 g
- Iron and zinc: lower

**Chicken thigh**

- Calories: about 180–210 kcal
- Protein: about 25–26 g
- Fat: about 8–11 g
- Iron and zinc: higher

**With skin**, both have more fat and calories. Thigh with skin can pass 230 kcal per 100 g.

Read the raw vs cooked numbers in [protein in chicken](/blog/protein-in-chicken).

# Protein per 100 kcal

- **Breast:** about 19 g protein per 100 kcal
- **Thigh:** about 12–14 g protein per 100 kcal

For the same calories, breast gives you about **40–50% more protein**.

# Which is better for weight loss?

**Breast**, if you are tracking closely. The lower fat makes it easier to fit into a calorie deficit.

But a 150 g thigh portion is only about 30–70 kcal more than breast. If thigh helps you enjoy your food and stick to your plan, that small difference is fine.

# Which is better for muscle gain?

**Either.** What matters is your total daily protein. Thigh's extra fat can help skinny people reach their calorie target. See [how to gain muscle in 30 days](/blog/how-to-gain-muscle-in-30-days).

# Taste and cooking

**Breast**

- Mild, lean, can dry out easily
- Best for: grilling, tikka, stir-fry, salads, sandwiches
- Tip: flatten to even thickness and do not overcook

**Thigh**

- Richer flavour, stays juicy
- Best for: curries, pepper chicken, biryani, roasting, chettinad dishes
- Tip: harder to overcook, so great for beginners

# Cost and availability

In most Coimbatore chicken shops, you can ask for boneless breast or boneless thigh. Prices vary by shop and season. Bone-in curry cut is usually the cheapest per kg, but you get less meat per rupee.

**Tip:** buy a whole chicken and ask the shop to separate breast, thighs and the rest. Use breast for grilling and the rest for curries.

# Which should you choose?

- **Cutting or weight loss:** breast
- **Bulking or maintenance:** either; thigh if you prefer the taste
- **Curries and slow cooking:** thigh
- **Grilling and tikka:** breast, or thigh if you want juicier results
- **Cooking for family:** mix both

# What about other parts?

- **Drumstick (leg):** similar to thigh, a little leaner
- **Wings:** more skin and fat; least protein per calorie
- **Liver:** rich in iron and vitamin A; eat occasionally

# Frequently asked questions

**Is chicken thigh unhealthy?**
No. It has a little more fat, but it is still a lean, high-protein food, especially without the skin.

**Does removing the skin make a big difference?**
Yes. The skin carries much of the fat. Removing it cuts calories noticeably.

**How much chicken should I eat a day?**
150–200 g of cooked chicken a day is common for active people, as part of a balanced diet.
