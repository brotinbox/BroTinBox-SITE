> **Quick answer:** **Yes, for most healthy adults creatine is good for health.** Creatine monohydrate at **3–5 g a day** is one of the most studied supplements in sports nutrition. It helps you **lift more, recover faster and build more muscle** with training, and research suggests possible benefits for **older adults and brain function** too. Large reviews have found it safe in healthy people. If you have kidney disease or take regular medicines, check with your doctor first.

Creatine has a strange reputation in India. Gym trainers swear by it, while relatives warn that it is a "steroid" or will "damage your kidneys". Here is what the evidence actually says.

# What creatine is

Creatine is **not a steroid** and not a hormone. It is a natural compound made from three amino acids.

- Your **liver and kidneys make about 1 g a day**.
- You get another **0.5–1 g a day** if you eat meat or fish.
- About **95% of it is stored in your muscles**.

Muscles use creatine to quickly recharge energy during short, hard efforts: a heavy set, a sprint, a jump. A supplement simply fills your muscle stores higher than food alone can.

# Proven benefits

## 1. More strength and muscle

This is the best-supported benefit. With regular training, creatine users typically:

- Get **1–2 extra reps** per set
- Gain **more strength** over weeks
- Gain **more lean mass** than training alone

It does not build muscle by itself. It helps you train harder, and the training builds muscle.

## 2. Better performance in short, intense exercise

Weight training, sprinting, football, cricket fast bowling, badminton: any sport with short bursts benefits. It does little for long, slow endurance like marathon running.

## 3. Helpful for vegetarians

Vegetarians get almost no creatine from food, so their muscle stores start lower. Studies often show **bigger gains in vegetarians** who supplement.

# Promising benefits (still being studied)

- **Older adults:** with strength training, creatine may help slow age-related muscle loss.
- **Brain:** some studies show small improvements in memory and mental fatigue, especially when sleep-deprived or in vegetarians.
- **Bone health:** early research, mixed results.

These are promising, but not as proven as the muscle benefits.

# Is creatine safe?

For healthy adults, the research is reassuring:

- The **International Society of Sports Nutrition** reviewed the evidence and concluded creatine monohydrate is **safe and well tolerated** in healthy people, including long-term use.
- Studies have **not shown kidney or liver damage** in healthy people at normal doses.
- The **water weight** gain of 1–2 kg is the most common side effect, and it is water inside the muscle, not fat.

Read the full breakdown in [creatine side effects](/blog/creatine-side-effects).

# Who should be careful

Talk to a doctor before taking creatine if you:

- Have **kidney disease** or reduced kidney function
- Have **diabetes with kidney involvement**
- Take medicines that affect the kidneys (some painkillers, some blood pressure medicines)
- Are **pregnant or breastfeeding**
- Are **under 18** (it is likely safe, but discuss it first)

# How to take creatine

- **Type:** creatine monohydrate. Other forms are more expensive and not better.
- **Dose:** 3–5 g a day, every day, including rest days.
- **Timing:** any time. Consistency matters more than timing.
- **Loading:** optional. 20 g a day for 5–7 days fills muscles faster, but 3–5 g a day reaches the same level in about 3–4 weeks.
- **Water:** drink normally; you do not need litres extra.

# Buying creatine in India

Fake supplements are a real problem. To stay safe:

- Buy from the brand's own site or an authorised seller
- Check for an **FSSAI licence number** and batch details on the label
- Prefer brands that show **third-party lab testing**
- Plain, unflavoured creatine monohydrate is usually the best value

# Do you need creatine?

No one *needs* it. Food, training and sleep matter far more. But if you train with weights 3+ days a week and want a small, proven edge, creatine is one of the few supplements worth the money.

# Frequently asked questions

**Is creatine a steroid?**
No. It is a natural compound found in meat and fish and made by your own body.

**Can women take creatine?**
Yes. The benefits and safety are similar for women.

**Do I need to cycle creatine?**
No. There is no evidence that cycling on and off is necessary.
