> **Quick answer:** To build muscle, most people should eat about **200–300 kcal above their maintenance calories** each day, with **1.6–2.2 g of protein per kg** of body weight. A rough starting point for maintenance is **body weight in kg × 30–33**. For a 65 kg active man, that means about **2,150 kcal maintenance** and about **2,350–2,450 kcal** to build muscle. Aim to gain about **0.25–0.5% of body weight per week**.

You are training hard, eating "a lot", and still not growing? Or gaining weight but mostly around the belly? The answer is usually in your calorie number.

# Step 1: Estimate your maintenance calories

Maintenance is the number of calories that keeps your weight stable.

A simple starting estimate:

- **Lightly active** (desk job, 3 workouts a week): body weight in kg × **30**
- **Active** (on your feet, 4–5 workouts a week): body weight in kg × **33**
- **Very active** (physical job plus daily training): body weight in kg × **35+**

Examples:

- 55 kg, lightly active: about **1,650 kcal**
- 65 kg, active: about **2,150 kcal**
- 75 kg, active: about **2,475 kcal**

These are estimates. The real test is your scale.

# Step 2: Add a small surplus

- **Beginners and lean people:** about **+250–350 kcal**
- **Intermediate lifters:** about **+200–300 kcal**
- **People with more body fat:** consider a recomp at maintenance instead. See [bulking vs cutting](/blog/bulking-vs-cutting).

A bigger surplus does not build muscle faster. It mostly adds fat. Read [dirty bulk vs clean bulk](/blog/dirty-bulk-vs-clean-bulk) to see why.

# Step 3: Set your protein

- **1.6 g per kg** a day covers most people
- **Up to 2.2 g per kg** can help some lifters

For a 65 kg person: about **105–145 g of protein a day**.

Our guide on [how much protein per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day) shows how to spread it across meals.

# Step 4: Fill the rest with carbs and fats

After protein:

- **Fat:** about 20–30% of your calories (about 55–80 g for 2,400 kcal)
- **Carbs:** the rest, from rice, chapati, oats, millets, fruit and potatoes

Carbs fuel your training. Do not cut them when trying to build muscle.

# Step 5: Track and adjust

1. **Weigh yourself 3–4 mornings a week**, after the toilet, before food.
2. **Look at the weekly average**, not single days.
3. **Target gain:** about 0.25–0.5% of body weight per week. For 65 kg, about **0.15–0.3 kg a week**.
4. **Adjust every 2–3 weeks.** Gaining faster than that? Cut 100–200 kcal. Not gaining? Add 100–200 kcal.

# What 2,400 kcal looks like (Indian, non-veg)

- **Breakfast:** 3-egg omelette, 2 chapatis, 1 banana (about 550 kcal, 25 g protein)
- **Lunch:** 150 g chicken, 1.5 cups rice, dal, vegetables, curd (about 800 kcal, 60 g protein)
- **Pre-workout:** peanut butter banana toast (about 350 kcal, 12 g protein)
- **Dinner:** fish or paneer, 3 chapatis, vegetables, a glass of milk (about 700 kcal, 40 g protein)

**Total: about 2,400 kcal and 135 g protein.**

# "I eat a lot but do not gain weight"

Common reasons:

- **You eat a lot at one meal, then little for the rest of the day.**
- **Liquid calories are missing.** Milk and shakes are easy calories. See [protein shake without protein powder](/blog/protein-shake-without-protein-powder).
- **Your activity is higher than you think**, such as a long commute, an active job or sport.
- **Low appetite.** Eat more often and choose calorie-dense foods: peanut butter, nuts, dates, milk, ghee in small amounts. See [peanut butter for muscle gain](/blog/peanut-butter-for-muscle-gain).

# "I am gaining, but mostly fat"

- **Your surplus is too big.** Reduce by 200 kcal.
- **Your training is not hard enough.** Follow a structured plan with progressive overload, like our [push pull legs workout plan](/blog/push-pull-legs-workout-plan).
- **Protein is too low.**

# Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to count calories to build muscle?**
Not forever, but tracking for a few weeks teaches you what your portions really contain.

**Can I build muscle in a calorie deficit?**
Beginners and people returning to training can, especially with high protein. Progress is slower.

**How much muscle can I gain in a year?**
Beginners who train and eat well can gain roughly 5–10 kg of lean mass in the first year. It slows down after that.

**Is 3,000 kcal too much?**
For a large, very active person, it may be right. For most people, it is a large surplus that adds fat.
