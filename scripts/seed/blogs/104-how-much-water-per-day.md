> **Quick answer:** Most healthy adults need about **2–3 litres of fluids a day** from drinks, with men usually needing a little more than women. In **Coimbatore's heat**, or if you **exercise, sweat a lot or work outdoors**, you may need **3–4 litres**. The simplest check: your **urine should be pale yellow**. People with **kidney, heart or liver disease** should follow their doctor's fluid advice, which may be lower.

"Drink 8 glasses of water" is the most common advice, but your needs depend on your body, the weather and your activity. Here is how to know how much is right for you.

# How much water do you need?

General guidance on total water from food and drinks is about:

- **Women:** about 2–2.7 litres a day
- **Men:** about 2.5–3.7 litres a day

About **20%** of that usually comes from food (rice, dal, vegetables, fruit, curd, rasam, buttermilk). The rest comes from drinks.

So for most people, **2–3 litres of drinks a day** is a good starting point.

# When you need more

- **Hot weather:** Coimbatore summers (March to May) and outdoor work
- **Exercise:** add about **400–800 ml per hour** of training, more if you sweat heavily
- **High-protein diets:** a little extra helps
- **Creatine users:** drink normally and stay well hydrated
- **Fever, vomiting or diarrhoea:** use ORS
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding:** needs go up; follow your doctor

# Signs you are drinking enough

- **Pale yellow urine** (not clear, not dark)
- Urinating every 3–4 hours during the day
- No headache, dizziness or dry mouth

# Signs you need more

- **Dark yellow urine**
- Headache, tiredness, dizziness
- Dry lips and mouth
- Constipation
- Muscle cramps

# Can you drink too much?

Yes. Drinking very large amounts quickly (many litres in a few hours) can dilute the sodium in your blood, which is dangerous. This is rare but happens with water challenges and extreme endurance events.

**Do not force** 5–6 litres a day "for weight loss" if you are not thirsty or sweating.

# Does water help weight loss?

A little, in indirect ways:

- **Drinking water before meals** can help some people eat less
- **Replacing sugary drinks** with water cuts calories
- **Thirst is sometimes mistaken for hunger**

It does not "flush fat" or "detox" your body. Fat loss comes from a calorie deficit. See [water weight vs fat](/blog/water-weight-vs-fat).

# What counts as water?

**Counts:**

- Water, buttermilk, coconut water
- Tea and coffee (yes, they still hydrate)
- Milk, rasam, soups
- Fruits and vegetables (watermelon, cucumber, oranges)

**Limit:**

- Sugary drinks and packaged juices
- Energy drinks
- Alcohol (it makes you lose more water)

# Easy ways to drink enough

- **Start the day with a glass of water**
- **Carry a 1-litre bottle** and refill it 2–3 times
- **Drink a glass with every meal**
- **Have buttermilk** with lunch; great in the heat
- **Drink during and after training**
- **Add lemon, mint or a few cucumber slices** if plain water bores you

# A simple daily water plan

- **Wake up:** 1 glass
- **Breakfast:** 1 glass
- **Mid-morning:** 1–2 glasses
- **Lunch:** 1 glass + buttermilk
- **Afternoon:** 1–2 glasses
- **Workout:** 500 ml–1 litre
- **Dinner:** 1 glass

That gives about **2.5–3.5 litres**.

# Frequently asked questions

**Is drinking water at night bad?**
No, but drinking a lot right before bed may wake you up to urinate.

**Is cold water bad for weight loss?**
No. Cold water burns a negligible number of extra calories, and it does not harm digestion in healthy people.

**Should I drink water during meals?**
Yes, if you are thirsty. It does not "dilute digestion".
