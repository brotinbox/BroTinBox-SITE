> **Quick answer:** **Zone 2 cardio** is steady, easy-to-moderate exercise where you can still **talk in full sentences but cannot sing**. It usually means about **60–70% of your maximum heart rate**. Brisk walking, incline walking, easy cycling or slow jogging all count. It improves heart health and endurance, and helps fat loss as part of a calorie deficit. Most guidelines suggest building towards **150 minutes a week** of moderate cardio.

Zone 2 has become one of the most talked-about training ideas on fitness podcasts and YouTube. It sounds technical, but it is really just "easy cardio done properly".

# What are heart rate zones?

Heart rate zones divide exercise intensity into levels, based on your **maximum heart rate**:

- **Zone 1:** about 50–60%. Very easy, like a slow walk.
- **Zone 2:** about 60–70%. Easy to moderate. You can talk but you are breathing harder.
- **Zone 3:** about 70–80%. Moderate to hard. Talking becomes difficult.
- **Zone 4:** about 80–90%. Hard. Only a few words at a time.
- **Zone 5:** about 90–100%. All-out sprints.

# How to find your zone 2

A simple estimate:

1. **Max heart rate ≈ 220 − your age.**
2. **Zone 2 ≈ 60–70% of that number.**

For a 30-year-old:
- Max heart rate ≈ 190 beats per minute
- Zone 2 ≈ **114–133** beats per minute

For a 45-year-old:
- Max heart rate ≈ 175
- Zone 2 ≈ **105–123** beats per minute

The 220 − age formula is only a rough guide and can be off by 10–15 beats for some people.

# The talk test: no watch needed

If you do not have a heart rate monitor:

- **You can speak in full sentences**, but you would not want to sing.
- **Breathing is deeper**, but not gasping.
- **You feel like you could keep going for 45–60 minutes.**

That is zone 2.

# Why zone 2 is popular

## 1. Better heart and endurance
Regular moderate cardio improves your heart's efficiency and your body's ability to use oxygen.

## 2. Helps your body use fat for fuel
At lower intensities, your body uses a larger share of fat for energy. Note: this does **not** automatically mean more fat loss. Fat loss still depends on your overall calorie balance.

## 3. Easy to recover from
Unlike hard intervals, zone 2 does not leave you exhausted. You can do it on most days and still lift weights.

## 4. Easy to stick with
It is comfortable enough that most people can keep it up for months.

# Is zone 2 good for fat loss?

It helps, but it is not magic. A 45-minute zone 2 session burns about 250–400 kcal depending on your weight and the activity. Combined with a calorie deficit and strength training, that adds up.

Higher-intensity cardio burns more calories per minute, but it is harder to recover from and harder to stick with. For most people, **a mix of daily walking, 2–3 zone 2 sessions a week and strength training** is the best fat-loss setup.

# Zone 2 workouts you can do

- **Brisk walking**, especially uphill
- **The 12-3-30 incline treadmill walk**. See [12-3-30 treadmill workout](/blog/12-3-30-treadmill-workout).
- **Easy cycling**, outdoor or stationary
- **Slow jogging** (for fitter people)
- **Swimming at an easy pace**
- **Elliptical or cross-trainer**

# How much zone 2 should you do?

- **Beginners:** 2–3 sessions of 20–30 minutes a week
- **Intermediate:** 3–4 sessions of 30–45 minutes
- **Endurance athletes:** much more

Most health guidelines recommend at least **150 minutes of moderate activity a week**, which zone 2 fits perfectly.

# Frequently asked questions

**Is walking zone 2?**
Brisk walking often is, especially for beginners or on an incline. Slow strolling is usually zone 1.

**Should I do zone 2 before or after weights?**
After weights, or on separate days, so your lifting is not affected.

**Can I do zone 2 every day?**
Yes, many people do. It is easy to recover from.

**Do I need a heart rate monitor?**
No. The talk test works well.
