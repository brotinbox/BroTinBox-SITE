> **Quick answer:** For a light **creamy mushroom** without cream, sauté **300 g sliced button mushrooms** on high heat until golden, cook **onion, garlic, pepper and herbs**, then stir in **thick hung curd** off the heat so it turns creamy without splitting. A **150 g serving** is about **155 kcal with about 8–9 g protein**. Mushrooms on their own are **low in protein**, so serve it with **paneer, eggs, chana or chicken** to make it a high-protein meal.

Mushrooms have a rich, meaty taste for very few calories. This light, creamy version uses hung curd instead of cream. It is a home-style take on the **Creamy Mushroom** on our BroTin Box veg menu.

# Ingredients (serves 2)

- 300 g button mushrooms, wiped clean and sliced
- 1½ tsp oil (or 1 tsp oil + ½ tsp butter)
- 1 medium onion, finely chopped
- 4 garlic cloves, finely chopped
- 1 green chilli, slit (optional)
- ½ tsp black pepper powder
- ¼ tsp turmeric
- ½ tsp dried oregano or mixed herbs (or 1 tsp kasuri methi)
- Salt to taste
- ½ cup thick hung curd, whisked smooth
- 2–3 tbsp milk or water, if needed
- Chopped coriander or spring onion to finish

# Method

## 1. Brown the mushrooms

- Heat 1 tsp oil in a wide pan on **high heat**.
- Add mushrooms in a single layer (cook in two batches if needed).
- **Do not stir for 2 minutes,** then toss and cook until golden and most of the water has evaporated, about 6–8 minutes.
- Remove to a plate.

**Why:** crowded mushrooms steam and turn rubbery. High heat and space give a golden, meaty flavour.

## 2. Cook the base

- In the same pan, add ½ tsp oil (or butter) and the onion. Cook until soft and light golden.
- Add garlic and green chilli; cook 1 minute.
- Add turmeric, pepper, herbs and salt.

## 3. Combine

Return the mushrooms to the pan and toss for 1 minute.

## 4. Make it creamy

- **Turn the heat off** and let the pan cool for 1 minute.
- Stir in the whisked hung curd.
- Return to **very low heat** for 1–2 minutes, just to warm through. Add a splash of milk if too thick.

**Tip:** adding curd to a very hot pan makes it split. Low heat keeps it smooth.

## 5. Finish

Top with coriander or spring onion and a pinch of crushed pepper.

# Nutrition per serving

For about **150 g** (half the recipe):

- **Calories:** about 155 kcal
- **Protein:** about 8–9 g
- **Carbs:** about 12 g
- **Fat:** about 8–9 g
- **Fibre:** about 3–4 g

These match our kitchen's Creamy Mushroom closely.

# Make it a high-protein meal

Mushrooms are filling and low in calories, but **not a big protein source** (about 3 g per 100 g). Pair them:

- **+ 100 g paneer cubes** stirred in at step 3: about +18–20 g protein
- **+ 2 boiled eggs** on the side: +12 g
- **+ a bowl of black chana or rajma**: +8–10 g
- **+ 150 g grilled chicken** (non-veg): +45 g

**Example plate (veg, about 30 g protein):** creamy mushroom + paneer cubes + chana + 2 chapatis + salad.

See [paneer vs tofu](/blog/paneer-vs-tofu) and [protein in dal, chana and rajma](/blog/protein-in-dal-chana-rajma).

# Why mushrooms are good for a diet

- **Very low in calories** (about 22–25 kcal per 100 g raw)
- **Rich, savoury taste** that makes meals satisfying
- **Some B vitamins, potassium and selenium**
- Sun-exposed mushrooms provide a little **vitamin D**. See [vitamin D deficiency in India](/blog/vitamin-d-deficiency-india).

# Tips

- **Do not wash mushrooms in a bowl of water;** wipe them or rinse quickly and dry
- **Use a wide pan** so they brown, not boil
- **Try a mix:** button with oyster mushrooms for more texture
- **Store** cooked creamy mushroom in the fridge for up to 1 day; curd-based sauces are best fresh

# Frequently asked questions

**Is mushroom high in protein?**
No. It has about 3 g per 100 g. Pair it with a protein food.

**Is this good for weight loss?**
Yes. It is low in calories and filling.

**Can I use cream instead of curd?**
You can, but it adds a lot more calories and fat.
