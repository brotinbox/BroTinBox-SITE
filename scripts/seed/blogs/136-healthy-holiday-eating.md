> **Quick answer:** To eat well over **Christmas and New Year** without undoing your progress: **don't diet hard before parties**, **eat a protein-rich meal first**, **fill half your plate with protein and vegetables**, **pick one or two treats you really want**, go easy on **alcohol, sweet drinks and fried snacks**, **keep walking every day**, and **go straight back to normal meals the next day**. One big dinner adds very little fat. A week of "holiday mode" is what adds up.

The last week of December is full of cake, biryani, parties and late nights. You do not need to skip any of it. You just need a plan so the holidays stay a few good meals, not a two-week slide.

# The holiday maths

- **1 kg of fat ≈ 7,700 extra kcal**
- A big party dinner might be 1,500–2,000 kcal, perhaps 800–1,200 above a normal meal
- **One party:** maybe 0.1–0.15 kg of real fat, plus 1–2 kg of water from salt and carbs that goes away in a few days
- **10 days of "it's the holidays":** this is where 1–2 kg of real fat comes from

So the goal is not a perfect day. It is **not letting one party turn into a whole week off**.

Read [water weight vs fat](/blog/water-weight-vs-fat) so the scale on 26 December does not scare you.

# Before the party

- **Do not starve all day.** You will overeat at night.
- **Eat a normal breakfast and a protein-rich lunch:** eggs, chicken, paneer, dal, curd
- **Have a small protein snack** (2 boiled eggs, a cup of curd or sundal) an hour before you leave
- **Drink a glass or two of water**

# At the party

## Build your plate smartly

1. **Start with protein:** tandoori chicken, grilled fish, paneer tikka, kebabs
2. **Add salad and vegetables**
3. **Then a measured portion** of biryani, fried rice or naan
4. **Pick one dessert you really love,** not a bite of everything

## Watch the hidden calories

- **Fried starters:** chicken 65, cutlets, bajji, samosa
- **Creamy gravies:** butter chicken, paneer butter masala, korma
- **Sweet drinks and mocktails:** often 150–250 kcal a glass
- **Plum cake and sweets:** a slice of rich plum cake can be 300+ kcal

## If you drink alcohol

- Alcohol adds calories and lowers your willpower around food
- **Alternate each drink with water**
- **Avoid sugary mixers;** choose soda or water
- **Set a limit before you go**
- Never drink and drive

# The day after

- **Do not skip meals to "make up for it".** That often leads to the next binge.
- **Eat your normal, protein-rich meals**
- **Drink water,** have buttermilk
- **Go for a long walk**
- **Weigh in a few days later,** not the next morning

# Keep moving through the holidays

- **Aim for 8,000–10,000 steps** a day, even on holidays
- **Family walks** after big meals
- **Short home workouts** if the gym is closed. See [how to lose weight without a gym](/blog/lose-weight-without-gym).

# Christmas and New Year food swaps

- **Plum cake:** one thin slice instead of two thick ones
- **Biryani:** more chicken, less rice, raita instead of extra gravy
- **Fried chicken:** swap for tandoori or grilled
- **Sweet drinks:** soda with lime, buttermilk or water
- **Late-night snacks:** a protein snack or nothing; see [eating late at night and weight loss](/blog/eating-late-at-night-weight-loss)

# What if you overdo it?

Everyone does sometimes. Follow the **"never miss twice" rule**: make the very next meal a normal one. Read [cheat day or cheat meal](/blog/cheat-day) and [how to stay consistent with a diet](/blog/how-to-stay-consistent-with-diet).

# Frequently asked questions

**Will I gain weight over Christmas and New Year?**
A little water weight is likely. Real fat gain is small if you go back to normal eating between celebrations.

**Should I diet before a party?**
Eat normally and keep protein high. Starving first usually backfires.

**How do I say no to food pushers?**
"I'm full, it was lovely" works. Or take a small portion and eat slowly.
