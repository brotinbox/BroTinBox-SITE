> **Quick answer:** For **black chana sundal**, soak **1 cup kala chana** overnight, **pressure cook for 5–6 whistles** until soft, drain, then temper **mustard seeds, urad dal, curry leaves, green chilli and a pinch of hing** in **1 tsp oil**, add the chana with salt and finish with **grated coconut and lemon**. **100 g of seasoned black chana** has about **140 kcal, 8 g protein and 7.5 g fibre**, our kitchen's exact numbers. It is one of the easiest high-protein, high-fibre snacks or sides in South Indian cooking.

Sundal is the temple prasadam and evening snack everyone grew up with. It also happens to be one of the healthiest snacks you can make. This is a home-style version of the **Black Chana Bowl** side in our boxes.

# Ingredients (serves 3–4)

- 1 cup dry black chana (kala chana), about 200 g
- Water for soaking and cooking
- ½ tsp salt (for cooking)

**Tempering**

- 1 tsp oil (coconut oil for authentic taste)
- ½ tsp mustard seeds
- ½ tsp urad dal
- 1 dry red chilli, broken
- 1 green chilli, slit
- A sprig of curry leaves
- A pinch of hing (asafoetida)

**To finish**

- 2 tbsp freshly grated coconut
- 1 tbsp chopped coriander
- A squeeze of lemon (optional)
- Salt to taste

# Method

## 1. Soak

Wash the chana and soak in plenty of water for **8–10 hours** or overnight. It will roughly double in size.

## 2. Cook

- Drain, add fresh water to cover by 2–3 cm and ½ tsp salt.
- **Pressure cook 5–6 whistles** on medium heat, then let the pressure release naturally.
- The chana should be **soft but not mushy**. Press one between your fingers to check.
- Drain well. Save the cooking water for rasam or soup.

## 3. Temper

Heat oil. Add mustard seeds; when they splutter, add urad dal and fry until light golden. Add red chilli, green chilli, curry leaves and hing.

## 4. Combine

Add the drained chana and salt. Toss on medium heat for **2–3 minutes**.

## 5. Finish

Turn off the heat. Add grated coconut, coriander and lemon. Mix well.

# Ways to eat it

- **Evening snack** with tea (no sugar) or buttermilk
- **Side with lunch** to add protein and fibre to a rice meal
- **With grilled chicken or paneer** to build a high-protein plate
- **In a salad** with onion, tomato, cucumber and lemon

# Variations

- **Mango sundal:** add grated raw mango in summer
- **Kollu sundal:** same method with horse gram. See [kollu sprout salad](/blog/kollu-sprout-salad-recipe).
- **White chana or green gram** sundal
- **Spicy version:** add a spoon of sundal podi (roasted chana dal, red chilli, coriander seeds)

# Why sundal is great for diets

- **High in fibre and protein,** so it keeps you full
- **Low in fat** with just 1 tsp oil
- **Cheap and easy to make in bulk**
- **Traditional,** so families eat it happily

# Tips

- **Do not skip soaking;** it shortens cooking and helps digestion
- **Add a pinch of hing and cook well** to reduce gas
- **Store** in the fridge for 2 days; add coconut just before eating

# Frequently asked questions

**Is black chana better than white chana?**
Black chana has a little more fibre; protein is similar.

**Can I eat sundal every day?**
Yes, a bowl a day is a great addition for most people.

**Does chana cause gas?**
It can. Soaking, cooking well and adding hing and ginger help.
