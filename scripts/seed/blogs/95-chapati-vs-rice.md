> **Quick answer:** **One plain chapati** (about 35–40 g, no ghee) has about **95–120 kcal, 3 g protein and 2–3 g fibre**. **One cup of cooked white rice** (about 150 g) has about **180–200 kcal, 3.5–4 g protein and under 1 g fibre**. So **2 chapatis ≈ 1 cup of rice** in calories. Chapati has a little more fibre and keeps some people fuller, but **for weight loss the portion matters more than the choice**. Eat whichever you enjoy, in a measured amount.

In South India, the dinner question is often "rice or chapati?" Many people switch to chapati when they start a diet. Here is whether it really helps.

# Nutrition compared

**1 plain whole wheat chapati** (about 35–40 g, no ghee or oil)

- Calories: about 95–120 kcal
- Carbs: about 18–20 g
- Protein: about 3 g
- Fibre: about 2–3 g

**1 cup cooked white rice** (about 150 g)

- Calories: about 180–200 kcal
- Carbs: about 40–42 g
- Protein: about 3.5–4 g
- Fibre: about 0.5 g

**Add ghee or oil?** 1 tsp ghee on a chapati adds about 45 kcal. Phulka (no oil) is the lightest.

# Which is better for weight loss?

**Neither is magic.** Calories are similar for typical portions:

- 2 chapatis ≈ 200–240 kcal
- 1 cup rice ≈ 180–200 kcal

Chapati's advantages:

- **More fibre,** so you may feel fuller
- **Easier to count:** "2 chapatis" is clearer than "some rice"
- **Slightly slower** effect on blood sugar than white rice

Rice's advantages:

- **Easy to digest**
- **Goes with sambar, rasam, kootu** and most South Indian dishes
- **Gluten-free** (for people who must avoid gluten)

The real problem is usually **3–4 cups of rice** or **5–6 chapatis with ghee** in one meal.

# Which is better for diabetes?

Whole wheat chapati and less polished rice (parboiled, red or brown) usually raise blood sugar more slowly than polished white rice. Smaller portions, plus protein and vegetables at the same meal, matter even more. Follow your doctor's advice. See [brown rice vs white rice](/blog/brown-rice-vs-white-rice).

# Which is better for muscle gain?

Both. Rice is easy to eat in larger amounts and digests quickly around training. Chapati adds a little fibre. Many athletes eat rice for lunch and chapati for dinner.

# What matters more than rice vs chapati

- **Portion:** about 1 cup rice or 2 chapatis per meal for weight loss
- **Protein on the plate:** dal, eggs, chicken, fish, paneer
- **Vegetables:** half the plate
- **Oil and ghee:** measured, not poured
- **What comes with it:** chapati with paneer butter masala can be heavier than rice with sambar

# Can you eat both in one meal?

Yes. Just count them together. For example: **1 chapati + ½ cup rice** is roughly the same as 2 chapatis or 1 cup rice.

# Healthier options

- **Phulka** instead of oily chapati or parotta
- **Multigrain or ragi chapati** for more fibre
- **Parboiled or red rice** instead of polished raw rice
- **Millets** like ragi, kambu and thinai occasionally. See [millets for weight loss](/blog/millets-for-weight-loss-tamil-nadu).

# Frequently asked questions

**Is chapati better than rice at night?**
Not necessarily. Total calories for the day matter more than timing. See [eating late at night and weight loss](/blog/eating-late-at-night-weight-loss).

**Is parotta the same as chapati?**
No. Parotta is made with maida and lots of oil; one parotta can have 250–300 kcal.

**How many chapatis should I eat a day?**
For weight loss, about 2 per meal for most people. More if you are active and training.
