> **Quick answer:** The best high-protein Indian snacks are **boiled eggs (6 g each)**, **roasted chana (about 18–20 g per 100 g)**, **peanuts (about 25 g per 100 g)**, **sundal (8–10 g per cup)**, **sprouts salad (8–12 g per cup)**, **paneer cubes (18–20 g per 100 g)**, **curd or hung curd (5–10 g per bowl)** and **soya chunk fry (about 25 g per 50 g dry)**. Aim for **10–20 g of protein per snack** instead of bajji, biscuits or bakery puffs.

Evening snack time in India usually means tea with biscuits, bajji, vadai or a puff. Tasty, but low in protein and high in oil. Here are 15 Indian snacks that fill you up and add real protein.

# 15 high-protein Indian snacks

## Quick, no cooking

**1. Boiled eggs**
2 eggs: **about 12 g protein**, 140 kcal. Add pepper and salt.

**2. Roasted chana (pottukadalai or kala chana)**
A 40 g handful: **about 7–8 g protein**, about 150 kcal.

**3. Peanuts (roasted, unsalted)**
A 30 g handful: **about 7–8 g protein**, about 170 kcal. Calorie-dense, so measure.

**4. Curd or hung curd**
1 cup curd: **about 5–6 g protein**. Hung curd: **about 10 g per cup**.

**5. Paneer cubes with pepper**
75 g paneer: **about 14 g protein**, about 200–230 kcal.

**6. Glass of milk**
250 ml: **about 8 g protein**.

## South Indian favourites

**7. Chana sundal**
1 cup: **about 8–10 g protein**.

**8. Kollu (horse gram) sundal**
1 cup: **about 8–10 g protein**. See [kollu benefits](/blog/kollu-horse-gram-benefits-weight-loss).

**9. Green gram (pachai payaru) sprouts salad**
1 cup with onion, tomato and lemon: **about 8–12 g protein**.

**10. Pesarattu (moong dal dosa)**
1 medium: **about 7–8 g protein**.

## Cook-ahead options

**11. Soya chunk pepper fry**
50 g dry soya: **about 25 g protein**. See [soya chunks protein](/blog/soya-chunks-protein).

**12. Egg bhurji (2 eggs) in a chapati roll**
**About 15 g protein**.

**13. Paneer or tofu tikka bites**
100 g: **about 15–18 g protein**.

**14. Moong dal chilla**
2 chillas: **about 12–14 g protein**.

**15. Homemade protein shake**
Milk, banana, peanut butter and oats: **about 15–20 g protein**. See [protein shakes without protein powder](/blog/protein-shake-without-protein-powder).

# Snacks that look healthy but are low in protein

- **Makhana:** about 9–10 g protein per 100 g, but a typical bowl (25–30 g) gives only about 3 g
- **Fruit:** great for fibre and vitamins, little protein
- **"Protein" biscuits and bars:** often high in sugar; check the label. See [how to read nutrition labels](/blog/how-to-read-nutrition-labels-india).
- **Mixture, murukku, chips:** mostly oil and carbs

# Best snacks by goal

**Weight loss**

- Boiled eggs, sundal, sprouts, hung curd, buttermilk
- Keep snacks around 100–200 kcal

**Muscle gain**

- Paneer, peanuts, soya fry, milk shakes, egg rolls
- Snacks can be 300–400 kcal

**Office or college**

- Roasted chana, peanuts, boiled eggs, a box of sundal

# A simple snack plan

- **11 AM:** a fruit + 2 boiled eggs or a handful of roasted chana
- **4–5 PM:** tea without sugar + a cup of sundal or sprouts
- **Post-workout:** milk or a homemade shake

That adds about **20–30 g of protein** to your day from snacks alone.

# Frequently asked questions

**What is the cheapest high-protein snack in India?**
Roasted chana, peanuts and boiled eggs give the most protein per rupee.

**Are protein bars good snacks?**
Some are fine, but many are expensive and sugary. Real food is usually better.

**Can I eat peanuts on a diet?**
Yes, in a measured handful (about 30 g). They are healthy but calorie-dense.
