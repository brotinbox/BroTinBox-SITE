> **Quick answer:** One large **whole egg** has about **6–6.5 g protein and 70–78 kcal**. One **egg white** has about **3.5 g protein and 17 kcal**. The **yolk** adds about **2.7 g protein**, the fat, and most of the vitamins: **vitamin D, B12, choline, selenium, lutein**. For most healthy people, **whole eggs are the better choice**. Egg whites help when you need extra protein with very few calories, so many people combine **1–2 whole eggs with 2–3 extra whites**.

The gym habit of throwing away yolks comes from old fears about cholesterol. Here is what egg whites and whole eggs actually give you, and when each makes sense.

# Nutrition: whole egg vs egg white vs yolk

For one large egg (about 50 g edible):

**Whole egg**

- Calories: about 70–78 kcal
- Protein: about 6–6.5 g
- Fat: about 5 g
- Cholesterol: about 185 mg
- Vitamins and minerals: vitamin D, B12, A, choline, selenium, iron, lutein

**Egg white only**

- Calories: about 17 kcal
- Protein: about 3.5 g
- Fat: almost none
- Vitamins: some riboflavin and potassium, little else

**Yolk only**

- Calories: about 55 kcal
- Protein: about 2.7 g
- Fat: about 4.5 g
- Most of the egg's vitamins, minerals and healthy fats

So nearly **half the protein** and almost all the micronutrients are in the yolk.

# What about cholesterol?

Older advice limited eggs because yolks contain cholesterol. Newer research shows that for most people, **dietary cholesterol has a smaller effect on blood cholesterol** than saturated fat, trans fat and overall diet.

A large 2020 analysis found that **about one egg a day was not linked to higher heart disease risk** in most people.

If you have **diabetes, heart disease or high LDL cholesterol**, ask your doctor how many yolks fit your diet. Read [how many eggs a day is safe](/blog/how-many-eggs-a-day-is-safe).

# Which is better for weight loss?

Both can work.

- **Egg whites** give you protein with very few calories, so they help when your calorie budget is tight.
- **Whole eggs** keep you fuller for longer, thanks to the fat.

A good balance: **2 whole eggs + 2 egg whites** gives about 20 g protein for about 185 kcal.

# Which is better for muscle gain?

**Whole eggs.** Research suggests eating whole eggs after training may support muscle building at least as well as, and in some studies better than, the same protein from egg whites alone. The extra calories also help when you are bulking.

See [how many eggs a day](/blog/how-many-eggs-a-day-is-safe) and [protein in egg](/blog/protein-in-egg).

# When egg whites make sense

- You need lots of protein with very few calories (a strict cut)
- Your doctor has asked you to limit yolks
- You already eat several whole eggs and want more protein without more fat

# How to use egg whites without waste

- Use the yolks in a family dish, or give them to someone who needs the calories
- Buy **pasteurised liquid egg whites** if you use a lot
- Never eat raw egg whites; cook them fully to avoid salmonella and to absorb the protein properly

# Easy Indian ideas

- **Egg white bhurji** with onion, tomato, green chilli and coriander, plus 1 whole egg for taste
- **Masala omelette** with 2 whole eggs and 2 whites
- **Egg curry** with 3 whole eggs for a high-protein lunch
- **Boiled eggs** for snacks: keep a batch in the fridge for 3–4 days

# Frequently asked questions

**Is it bad to eat 2 whole eggs a day?**
No. For most healthy people, 1–2 whole eggs a day is fine, and active people often eat 2–3.

**Do egg whites have all the essential amino acids?**
Yes, egg white protein is complete. But the yolk adds nutrients the white lacks.

**Are brown eggs healthier than white eggs?**
No. Shell colour depends on the hen breed, not nutrition.
