> **Quick answer:** A calorie deficit means eating less energy than your body uses. Estimate your daily maintenance calories with the formula below, then eat 300–500 kcal less. This usually leads to about 0.25–0.5 kg of fat loss per week. Keep protein high, weigh yourself regularly, and adjust based on your results over several weeks.

Keto, intermittent fasting, millet diets and "no rice after 7 PM" all look very different. When they work for fat loss, they share one thing: they help you eat less energy than you burn.

That does not mean all calories affect your hunger and health in the same way. But it does mean you need a rough number to work from, and it only takes 5 minutes to find it.

# Step 1: Calculate your resting calories

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation is a widely used way to estimate how much energy your body uses at rest.

- **Men:** (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) + 5
- **Women:** (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) − 161

# Step 2: Multiply by your activity level

- **Mostly sitting, little exercise:** × 1.2
- **Light exercise, 1–3 days a week:** × 1.375
- **Moderate exercise, 3–5 days a week:** × 1.55
- **Hard exercise, 6–7 days a week:** × 1.725

The result is your estimated maintenance calories. Be honest here, as most people overestimate how active they are. An hour at the gym does not cancel out a day of sitting. If you are unsure, choose the lower option.

# Step 3: Subtract 300–500 kcal

Start with the smaller end of the range if you are smaller, or if you have less weight to lose. Faster is not always better. Very low intakes can increase hunger, lower your energy and make it harder to keep muscle.

# Two worked examples

## Karthik, 30, 80 kg, 175 cm, gym twice a week

- **Resting:** 800 + 1,094 − 150 + 5 = about 1,750 kcal
- **Maintenance:** 1,750 × 1.375 = about 2,400 kcal
- **Target:** about 2,000 kcal a day
- **Expected loss:** a 400 kcal daily deficit is about 0.35 kg a week

## Priya, 28, 65 kg, 160 cm, walks and does yoga 3 days a week

- **Resting:** 650 + 1,000 − 140 − 161 = about 1,350 kcal
- **Maintenance:** 1,350 × 1.375 = about 1,850 kcal
- **Target:** about 1,550 kcal a day
- **Expected loss:** a 300 kcal daily deficit is about 0.27 kg a week

These are estimates based on a rough figure of about 7,700 kcal per kg of body fat. Your real result will vary, which is why the next step matters most.

# Step 4: Check your results and adjust

Weigh yourself in the morning, 3 or more times a week, and compare the weekly averages. Daily weight jumps around with salt, water, carbs and hormones, so a single reading tells you very little.

After 3–4 weeks:

- **Losing about 0.25–0.75 kg a week:** Keep going.
- **No change on average:** Check your tracking first, then reduce by 100–200 kcal or add more walking.
- **Losing very fast and feeling terrible:** Eat a bit more.

If you get stuck later on, read [how to break a weight loss plateau](/blog/weight-loss-plateau-why-scale-stopped).

# How to make a deficit easier

## Keep protein high

Protein helps you stay full and protects muscle during fat loss. See [how much protein you need](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day).

## Cut the calories you do not notice

These often make the biggest difference:

- **Cooking oil:** 1 tablespoon is about 120 kcal
- **Sweet tea or coffee:** two or three a day add up quickly
- **Refills:** a second cup of rice is about another 200 kcal
- **Snacks:** a small plate of mixture or a couple of bajjis can match a small meal

Our [South Indian calorie guide](/blog/calories-in-south-indian-food) gives figures for common dishes.

## Do not ban the foods you love

You can fit biryani or dessert into your week if you plan for them. Rigid rules often end in a binge.

## Walk more

Daily walking adds to your energy use, without the hunger that often follows hard training.

# Who should not do this alone?

Talk to a doctor or registered dietitian before dieting if you are pregnant or breastfeeding, have diabetes or another medical condition, take regular medication, or have a history of eating disorders. This guide is for healthy adults. Those under 18 should not follow calorie targets without professional guidance.

# Frequently asked questions

**How low should my calories go?**
Most adults should avoid very low targets, unless they are under medical supervision. A moderate 300–500 kcal deficit is a sensible starting point.

**Do I have to count calories forever?**
No. Tracking for a few weeks teaches you what portions look like. After that, many people can manage by habit.

**Why am I not losing weight even in a deficit?**
You may be retaining water, or you may be eating more than you think. Oil and portion sizes are the most common blind spots. If nothing changes after careful tracking, or you have other symptoms, see your doctor.

**Can I build muscle in a calorie deficit?**
Beginners and people with more weight to lose often can, if they lift weights and eat enough protein. For most people, gaining muscle is easier with more food. Here is our [muscle gain guide](/blog/indian-gym-diet-muscle-gain).
