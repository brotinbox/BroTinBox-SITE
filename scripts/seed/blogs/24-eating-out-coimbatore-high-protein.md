> **Quick answer:** You can eat out in Coimbatore and stay on track. Choose grilled, tandoori or dry-roasted protein, limit rice or parotta to one measured serving, swap fried sides for salad or curd, and watch refills. Coimbatore is a hard city to diet in, and that is a compliment. There is Kongu-style chicken, fresh parotta, biryani, arisi paruppu sadam and strong filter coffee around every corner.

You do not have to give any of it up. Here is what to order at the places you are most likely to eat, with calorie figures taken from our [South Indian calorie guide](/blog/calories-in-south-indian-food).

# 3 habits that save the most calories

## 1. Decide before you are hungry

It is hard to make a good choice when you are starving and everyone at the table is ordering. Have a rough plan on the way there, and eat a small protein snack first if you will be eating late.

## 2. Order protein first

Build your meal around chicken, fish, eggs, paneer or dal, and then decide on the carbs. It helps you to stay full and to eat less of everything else.

## 3. Control the extras

Refills, fried sides and sugary drinks are often the difference between a 600 kcal meal and a 1,200 kcal meal. Sweet lime soda, fried appalam and an extra ladle of gravy all look small, but they can add up fast.

# What to order, place by place

## At a mess or a banana-leaf meals restaurant

A full unlimited South Indian meal can come to **800–1,200 kcal**, depending on refills.

**Order this:**
- One serving of rice, then stop
- Extra poriyal and kootu
- Rasam, and sambar with plenty of dal and vegetables
- Grilled fish, or a chicken or egg side

**Go easy on:** extra rice, fried sides, sweets and thick oily gravies.

## At a biryani place

A restaurant plate of chicken biryani is about **600–900 kcal**.

**Order this:**
- Share a plate, or ask for a half portion if they offer one
- Add a chicken kebab or grilled chicken for more protein
- Have raita and onion salad on the side
- Take the leg or breast piece, and leave some of the rice

**Go easy on:** dalcha or brinjal gravy with extra oil, 65-style starters and a sweet to finish.

## At a parotta stall

A parotta has about **250–320 kcal**, and a plate of kothu parotta has **600–800 kcal**.

**Order this:**
- One parotta, or swap for chapati if it is available
- A dry-roasted chicken or an egg dish, instead of more parotta
- A boiled egg or omelette on the side

**Go easy on:** extra salna, which is often oily, and a second or third parotta.

## At a tiffin centre

Tiffin can be the easiest meal to manage, although it is often low in protein.

**Order this:**
- Idli (about 55–65 kcal each), or plain dosa (about 120–170 kcal), rather than a ghee roast
- Pesarattu or ragi dosa, if they serve them
- Sambar, plus an egg or a side of sundal if they are available

**Go easy on:** vadas, a ghee roast (about 300–400 kcal) and several helpings of coconut chutney.

## At a Kongu or Chettinad restaurant

Regional food can be high in protein, if you choose dry dishes.

**Order this:**
- Dry-style chicken dishes such as pallipalayam chicken, or a grilled or tawa fish
- Pepper chicken, with a measured serving of rice
- Rasam and a vegetable side

**Go easy on:** deep-fried starters and very oily gravies. Ask for less oil if the kitchen can do that. Many will.

## At a tandoori or North Indian restaurant

**Order this:**
- Tandoori chicken or fish, or paneer tikka
- Dal without too much butter or cream
- Plain roti instead of butter naan
- Salad and curd on the side

**Go easy on:** butter chicken, malai dishes, stuffed naans and paneer butter masala.

## At a cafe or bakery

Keep an eye out for sweet coffee, cream, and flaky pastries like puffs, which contain a lot of fat.

**Order this:**
- Filter coffee with less sugar, or black coffee
- An egg puff instead of two pastries, as an occasional choice
- A sandwich with grilled chicken, egg or paneer, and no extra mayo

# Ordering on a delivery app

Delivery apps make it easy to add things you did not plan on. Decide what you want first. Choose a "grilled" or "tandoori" dish over one described as "crispy", "loaded" or "butter". Skip the combo that adds fries and a drink. If there are no calorie figures, assume the meal has more oil than you would use at home.

# What if you overdo it?

It happens. Do not skip your next meal or punish yourself at the gym. Go back to your normal plan at the next meal, and keep your protein up. One restaurant meal will not undo weeks of work. # Frequently asked questions

**How many times a week can I eat out and still lose weight?**
There is no fixed limit. Many people do well with one or two planned meals out per week. Your total calories over the week matter most.

**Is restaurant grilled chicken always healthy?**
It is usually a good choice, but it may be brushed with butter or oil. Ask for less, and go easy on sauces and dips.

**Is biryani bad for weight loss?**
No food is off limits. A shared or smaller portion with extra protein and raita can fit into a plan if you account for it.

**How can I estimate calories at a restaurant?**
Use our [calorie guide](/blog/calories-in-south-indian-food) as a starting point, and add a margin for oil. Most people underestimate restaurant meals.
