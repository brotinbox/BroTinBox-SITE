> **Quick answer:** A true plateau means your weekly average weight and waist size have not changed for 3–4 weeks. Before you cut more food, check for water retention, re-measure your portions, and look at your daily activity. If you really are stuck, reduce intake by 100–200 kcal or walk more. If you have been dieting for months, take a 1–2 week break at maintenance.

The first few weeks felt great. The scale moved every week. Then it stopped, and now it feels as if your body has decided to ignore everything you are doing.

Plateaus are normal, and they rarely mean that your metabolism is broken. Here is how to work out what is really going on.

# First: is it really a plateau?

Your weight can swing by a kilo or more from one day to the next. Common reasons include:

- A salty meal or restaurant dinner
- More carbs than usual
- Poor sleep or high stress
- Sore muscles after a hard workout
- The menstrual cycle, which can cause water retention for days at a time
- Digestion and bowel movements

**How to check:**

1. Weigh yourself 3 or more mornings a week, and compare the weekly averages.
2. Measure your waist every 2 weeks, in the same place, first thing in the morning.
3. Compare photos taken a month apart, if you are comfortable doing that.

If your weight is flat but your waist is shrinking, you are probably still making progress. If nothing has changed for **3–4 weeks**, you can call it a plateau.

# The 5 most common causes

## 1. Your portions have crept up

This is the most common reason. In week one, you measured the rice and oil. By week eight, you are guessing. A free-poured extra spoon of oil is about 120 kcal, and a slightly bigger rice serving can add 50–100 more. Two or three of these a day can wipe out your whole deficit.

**Fix:** Go back to measuring everything for 7 days. You do not have to do it forever. It is simply a way to recalibrate your eyes.

## 2. You weigh less, so you burn less

A smaller body uses less energy, for both basic functions and movement. A target that worked at 85 kg may be close to maintenance at 75 kg.

**Fix:** Recalculate your numbers using our [calorie deficit guide](/blog/calorie-deficit-how-to-calculate).

## 3. You are moving less without noticing

When dieting, many people unconsciously take fewer steps and sit more. This everyday activity can make a surprisingly big difference to the energy you burn.

**Fix:** Track your steps for a week, then try adding about 2,000 a day. A 20-minute walk after dinner is an easy way to start.

## 4. Weekends are undoing your week

Eating 400 kcal under your target from Monday to Friday saves 2,000 kcal. A big weekend with biryani, dessert and drinks can easily use all of that up.

**Fix:** Keep your weekends enjoyable, but plan them. Have one planned meal out, instead of two whole days off. Our [eating out guide](/blog/eating-out-high-protein-coimbatore) can help.

## 5. You are exhausted

Long periods of dieting, too little sleep and too much stress can increase hunger and make you less active. Eventually, your plan becomes harder to stick to.

**Fix:** Prioritise sleep, and consider a break from dieting (see below).

# What to do when you are really stuck

Try these in order. Change only one thing at a time, then give it 2–3 weeks.

1. **Tighten your tracking** for one week, particularly oil, snacks, drinks and weekend meals.
2. **Add movement,** starting with daily steps.
3. **Check your protein,** because it helps with fullness and with protecting muscle. Here is [how much you need](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day).
4. **Reduce intake by 100–200 kcal,** provided you are not already eating very little.
5. **Lift weights** 2–3 times a week to help you keep muscle while you lose fat.

# When to take a diet break

If you have been in a deficit for 3 months or more, and you are hungry, tired and fed up, it may be time for a break. Eat at your new maintenance level for 1–2 weeks, keeping your protein and your training going.

It may feel like going backwards, but this is not giving up. You may gain a small amount of water weight at first, and that is normal. Many people return feeling more able to stick to their plan.

# When to see a doctor

Ask your doctor to take a look if the plateau comes with any of the following:

- Unusual tiredness, hair loss or feeling cold all the time
- Irregular periods
- Very low calorie intake for a long time, with no weight change
- New medication that may affect your weight
- Signs of low mood or an unhealthy relationship with food

Conditions such as an underactive thyroid or PCOS can make weight loss harder, and they need proper medical advice.

# What not to do

- **Do not crash diet.** Cutting calories drastically usually backfires. It is also harder to keep your muscle.
- **Do not buy fat burners.** They do not fix the problem, and some can be unsafe.
- **Do not change everything at once.** You will not know what worked.

# Frequently asked questions

**How long does a weight loss plateau last?**
It depends on what is causing it. Many apparent plateaus are water weight and pass within a couple of weeks. A real one lasts until something changes, in your intake or your activity.

**Does starvation mode cause plateaus?**
Your body does use a little less energy when you diet, but it does not stop you from losing fat completely. More often, the cause is a smaller body, reduced movement, and portions that have drifted upwards.

**Should I do more cardio?**
Walking is a good first step. Extra cardio can help, but hard sessions may make you hungrier, so keep an eye on your food.

**Should I stop weighing myself?**
If the scale makes you anxious, weigh less often, and use your waist measurements and photos too.
