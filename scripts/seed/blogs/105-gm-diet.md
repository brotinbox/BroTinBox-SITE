> **Quick answer:** The **GM diet** is a 7-day plan where each day allows only certain foods: **fruits only (day 1)**, **vegetables only (day 2)**, **fruits and vegetables (day 3)**, **bananas and milk (day 4)**, **a protein food with tomatoes (days 5–6)** and **brown rice, vegetables and juice (day 7)**. People often lose **2–4 kg in a week**, but most of it is **water and glycogen**, and it usually comes back. It is **low in protein**, not backed by research, and **not linked to General Motors** despite the name.

The GM diet has been circulating in India on WhatsApp and Instagram for years. Here is what it involves, why the scale drops, and whether it is worth doing.

# Where did it come from?

The story says it was created for **General Motors** employees in the 1980s. There is **no evidence** that General Motors made or endorsed it. It is a popular internet diet, not a scientifically tested plan.

# The 7 days

**Day 1: Fruits only**
Any fruit except bananas. Watermelon and other high-water fruits are encouraged.

**Day 2: Vegetables only**
Raw or cooked vegetables. Often starts with a boiled potato for breakfast.

**Day 3: Fruits and vegetables**
Any fruits (except bananas) and vegetables. No potatoes.

**Day 4: Bananas and milk**
Usually up to **8 bananas and 3 glasses of milk**, plus a vegetable soup.

**Day 5: Protein and tomatoes**
Originally beef or chicken with tomatoes. Indian versions use **paneer, chicken or brown rice** with 6 tomatoes.

**Day 6: Protein and vegetables**
Chicken, fish or paneer with vegetables (no potatoes).

**Day 7: Brown rice, vegetables and fruit juice**

The plan also promotes a "GM wonder soup" (cabbage, onion, tomato, capsicum, celery) and **lots of water**.

# Why people lose weight on it

- **Very low calories** on most days (often 800–1,200 kcal)
- **Low carbs** on days 1–3 drain glycogen, and each gram of glycogen holds about 3 g of water
- **Less food in your gut**
- **Lots of water and fibre**

Result: **2–4 kg on the scale** in 7 days. But fat loss in a week at this deficit is usually **only about 0.5–1 kg**. The rest is water.

# The problems

- **Very low protein** on days 1–4, so you lose muscle
- **Hunger, tiredness, headaches**, especially on days 1–3
- **No long-term habit change:** you go back to normal eating on day 8
- **Fast regain:** the water weight returns within days
- **Not suitable** for people with diabetes (fruit-only and banana days can disturb blood sugar), kidney disease, pregnancy, or a history of eating disorders
- **No research** supporting it

# Is there anything good about it?

- It **increases fruits and vegetables**, which many Indians under-eat
- It shows people how **sugary drinks and snacks** add calories
- **Wonder soup** is a decent low-calorie starter

You can keep these good parts without the extreme rules.

# A better 7-day plan

Instead of the GM diet, try this for a week:

- **Every meal:** protein + vegetables + a measured carb
- **Breakfast:** 2 idlis with sambar and 2 boiled eggs, or moong dal chilla with curd
- **Lunch:** 1 cup rice, 150 g chicken, fish or paneer, poriyal, salad, curd
- **Evening:** buttermilk and sundal or a fruit
- **Dinner:** a bowl of vegetable soup, 2 chapatis, dal, vegetables
- **Walk:** 8,000–10,000 steps a day

You will likely lose **1–2 kg in the first week** (some water, some fat), and keep going at 0.5–1 kg a week after that. See our [7-day Indian weight loss meal plan](/blog/indian-weight-loss-meal-plan-7-days).

# Frequently asked questions

**How much weight can you lose on the GM diet?**
Usually 2–4 kg on the scale in 7 days, mostly water.

**Can I repeat the GM diet every week?**
It is not recommended. Repeating it means long periods of low protein and muscle loss.

**Is the GM diet good for diabetics?**
No. The fruit-only and banana days can raise blood sugar, and it is not designed for medical conditions.
