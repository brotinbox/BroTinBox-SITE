Deepavali means new clothes, early morning oil baths, crackers, and boxes of sweets arriving from everyone you know. Nobody should spend it staring at a calorie table.

But it does help to know the numbers, so that you can choose what you really want. The real risk is not one festival day. It is the tin of murukku on the counter for the next ten days.

# Approximate calories in Deepavali sweets

Recipes and piece sizes vary widely, particularly in home kitchens, so treat these as estimates:

- **Mysore pak (1 piece, about 40 g):** 180–220 kcal
- **Boondi laddu (1 piece, 40–50 g):** 180–220 kcal
- **Gulab jamun (1 piece with syrup, about 40 g):** 140–170 kcal
- **Kaju katli (1 piece, about 20 g):** 80–100 kcal
- **Adhirasam (1 piece, about 50 g):** 200–230 kcal
- **Jangiri (1 medium):** 150–200 kcal
- **Rava laddu (1 piece, about 40 g):** 160–190 kcal

# Approximate calories in savoury snacks

- **Murukku (1 medium, about 25 g):** 120–140 kcal
- **Mixture (½ cup, about 50 g):** 250–280 kcal
- **Ribbon pakoda (½ cup, about 50 g):** 250–280 kcal
- **Thattai (1 piece, about 15 g):** 70–85 kcal
- **Banana chips (½ cup, about 40 g):** 200–220 kcal

To put it in perspective, a 70 kg person burns about 130 kcal in a 30-minute brisk walk. That is not a reason to feel guilty. It is simply a reason to enjoy each sweet on purpose, instead of eating it because it happens to be in front of you.

# A simple plan for Deepavali

## Before the festival: keep your routine

The days leading up to Deepavali are often the hardest, because sweets arrive early. Keep your normal meals as long as you can. Keep gift boxes in a closed cupboard, not on the dining table.

## On the day: enjoy it properly

- **Have a protein breakfast if you can.** Eggs, sundal or curd help take the edge off your appetite.
- **Choose your top two or three sweets** and skip the ones you do not love.
- **Serve yourself a piece on a plate.** You will eat less than when you are picking from a box.
- **Drink water between snacks**, and do not fill up on sugary drinks.
- **Take a walk** after lunch with family, if the day allows it.

There is no need to fast beforehand or to punish yourself afterwards. Skipping meals usually makes you hungrier when the sweets come out.

## After the festival: stop the leftovers

This is where most of the damage happens. Try these ideas:

- Share sweets with neighbours, colleagues or anyone who will enjoy them.
- Keep one small box and pass on the rest.
- Go straight back to your normal meals the next morning, with no "detox" and no starving.

# Smart swaps that still taste like a festival

- **Choose smaller pieces**, or cut large sweets in half and share.
- **Roasted snacks:** try roasted makhana, roasted chana or a spiced sundal alongside the fried ones.
- **Fresh fruit with a small sweet:** this makes the portion feel bigger and more satisfying.
- **Home baking and frying:** measure out sugar, ghee and oil, and you may find you can reduce them a little without anyone noticing.

Be careful with "sugar-free" sweets. They can still be high in calories from nuts, ghee and flour, so check the label if there is one. Here is [how to read one](/blog/how-to-read-nutrition-labels-india).

# If you have diabetes

Festival days can be harder to manage. Agree your plan with your doctor, keep up your medicines and monitoring, and spread out any sweets rather than having them all at once. Remember that "no added sugar" sweets can still raise your blood sugar.

# What if you gain weight after Deepavali?

A jump of a kilo or more in a couple of days is usually mainly water, salt and extra food in your system, not all fat. To gain 1 kg of actual fat, you would need to eat roughly 7,700 kcal more than you burn.

Go back to your normal meals and activity, and it should settle over the next week or so. If you want a structure to come back to, try our [7-day weight loss meal plan](/blog/indian-weight-loss-meal-plan-7-days), or learn to [set your calorie target](/blog/calorie-deficit-how-to-calculate).

# Frequently asked questions

**Which Deepavali sweet has the fewest calories?**
Per piece, smaller sweets such as kaju katli tend to be lower, although they are still rich. Portion size matters more than the type.

**Are ghee sweets healthier than sweets made with oil?**
Both are concentrated fats with about the same calories. Choose the one you enjoy more, and eat it in moderation.

**Can I eat sweets and still lose weight?**
Yes, if your overall calories stay in check. A planned sweet is much easier to manage than a week of unplanned snacking.

**Should I do a detox after Deepavali?**
No. Your liver and kidneys already do that job. Regular meals, water, sleep and movement are all you need.
