> **Quick answer:** For **caramel onion paneer**, slowly cook **2 large sliced onions** in **2 tsp oil** on low heat for **15–20 minutes** until deep golden and sweet, add **ginger-garlic, pepper, a little chilli and garam masala**, then toss in **lightly seared paneer cubes** and finish with coriander and lemon. The sweetness comes from the onions themselves, with no sugar needed. A serving with **100 g paneer** gives about **19–21 g protein and 380–430 kcal**.

Caramelised onions turn simple paneer into something special: sweet, savoury and rich without cream or butter. This is a home-style version of the **Caramel Onion Paneer** on our BroTin Box veg menu.

# Ingredients (serves 2)

- 200 g paneer, cut into 2 cm cubes
- 2 large onions, thinly sliced
- 2 tsp oil (or 1 tsp oil + 1 tsp ghee)
- ½ tsp cumin seeds
- 1 tsp ginger-garlic paste
- 1 green chilli, slit
- ½ tsp black pepper powder
- ½ tsp Kashmiri chilli powder
- ¼ tsp turmeric
- ½ tsp garam masala
- Salt to taste
- 2–3 tbsp water
- Fresh coriander and a squeeze of lemon to finish

# Method

## 1. Caramelise the onions (the key step)

- Heat 1½ tsp oil in a heavy pan on **low to medium-low heat**.
- Add cumin seeds, then the sliced onions and a pinch of salt (it helps them release moisture).
- Cook slowly for **15–20 minutes**, stirring every couple of minutes.
- If they stick, add a tablespoon of water and scrape the bottom.
- They are done when they are **soft, sticky and deep golden brown**.

Do not rush this with high heat; the onions will burn before they turn sweet.

## 2. Sear the paneer

While the onions cook, heat the remaining ½ tsp oil in another pan. Sear the paneer cubes for **1–2 minutes per side** until lightly golden. Keep aside.

**Tip:** if your paneer is very firm, soak the cubes in warm salted water for 10 minutes first to soften them.

## 3. Build the masala

To the caramelised onions, add ginger-garlic paste and green chilli. Cook 1 minute. Add pepper, chilli powder, turmeric and salt. Cook 30 seconds.

## 4. Combine

Add the paneer and 2–3 tbsp water. Toss gently for **2 minutes** so the onions coat every cube. Sprinkle garam masala.

## 5. Finish

Turn off the heat. Add coriander and a squeeze of lemon to balance the sweetness.

# Nutrition per serving

For 100 g full-fat paneer with onions and oil:

- **Calories:** about 380–430 kcal
- **Protein:** about 19–21 g
- **Fat:** about 26–30 g
- **Carbs:** about 14–16 g

**Lighter version:** use **low-fat paneer** and 1 tsp oil in total. That brings it to about **280–320 kcal and 22–25 g protein**.

# Tips

- **Thin, even onion slices** caramelise evenly
- **Heavy pan** prevents burning
- **Patience:** the flavour comes from time, not sugar
- **Make a big batch of caramelised onions** and keep them in the fridge for 4–5 days; they also work in omelettes, dal and sandwiches

# Variations

- **Tofu version:** use pressed firm tofu for fewer calories. See [tandoori tofu tikka](/blog/tandoori-tofu-tikka-recipe) for pressing tips.
- **Add capsicum** in step 4 for crunch and colour
- **Spicier:** add ½ tsp crushed pepper and an extra green chilli

# Frequently asked questions

**Is caramelised onion high in sugar?**
Onions have natural sugars, and caramelising concentrates the flavour, but 2 onions shared between 2 people add only about 40–50 kcal each.

**Is this good for weight loss?**
Yes, in a measured portion, especially with low-fat paneer. It is cooked with little oil and no cream.

**How long does it keep?**
Up to 2 days in the fridge. Reheat gently in a pan.
