> **Quick answer:** For most people, **push pull legs (PPL) builds more muscle than a bro split** because it trains each muscle **twice a week** instead of once. Research shows training a muscle at least twice a week gives better growth than once, when the total number of sets is similar. A bro split can still work for advanced lifters who already train with very high volume, but for beginners and intermediates PPL is the better choice.

Walk into any gym in Coimbatore and you will hear both: "Today is chest day" and "Today is push day." Both are popular ways to organise your week. But which one actually builds more muscle?

# What is push pull legs?

PPL splits your training by **movement**:

- **Push day:** chest, shoulders, triceps (bench press, overhead press, dips, push-ups)
- **Pull day:** back, biceps, rear shoulders (rows, pull-ups, lat pulldowns, curls)
- **Legs day:** quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves (squats, deadlifts, lunges, leg press)

You can run it in two ways:

- **3 days a week:** Push, Pull, Legs once each (each muscle trained once a week)
- **6 days a week:** Push, Pull, Legs, Push, Pull, Legs, rest (each muscle trained twice a week)

# What is a bro split?

A bro split trains **one or two muscle groups per day**, usually five days a week:

- **Monday:** Chest
- **Tuesday:** Back
- **Wednesday:** Shoulders
- **Thursday:** Arms
- **Friday:** Legs

Each muscle gets one big session a week, with lots of sets on that day.

# What the research says

The key factor is **training frequency**: how many times a week each muscle gets worked.

A widely cited 2016 review by Schoenfeld and colleagues found that training each muscle **at least twice a week** gave better muscle growth than training it once a week. Later research showed that **total weekly volume** (the number of hard sets per muscle per week) matters most, and frequency is mainly a way to fit that volume in without junk sets.

Here is why that favours PPL:

- On a bro split, you might do 15–20 sets of chest on Monday. The last 8–10 sets are often low quality because you are already tired.
- On a 6-day PPL, you do about 8–10 sets of chest on Monday and again on Thursday. Every set is done fresh.
- Muscle protein synthesis (the "building" signal after training) stays raised for roughly 24–48 hours. Hitting a muscle twice a week gives you two growth windows instead of one.

# PPL vs bro split: side by side

**Push pull legs**
- Frequency: each muscle twice a week (6-day version)
- Sets per session per muscle: moderate, higher quality
- Best for: beginners to intermediates, anyone who wants faster progress
- Downside: 6 days a week is a big time commitment

**Bro split**
- Frequency: each muscle once a week
- Sets per session per muscle: high, quality drops late in the session
- Best for: advanced lifters who enjoy one big "pump" session per muscle
- Downside: miss one day and that muscle goes two weeks without training

# What if you can only train 3–4 days a week?

Then a 6-day PPL is not realistic, and that is fine. Options:

- **3 days a week:** full body, three times a week. Each muscle still gets trained three times.
- **4 days a week:** upper/lower split (Upper, Lower, rest, Upper, Lower). Each muscle is trained twice.
- **3 days of PPL:** works, but frequency drops to once a week, similar to a bro split.

For most working people and students, a 4-day upper/lower or 3-day full body gives results very close to a 6-day PPL.

# Which split should you choose?

Choose **PPL (6 days)** if:
- You can reliably train 6 days a week
- You want the most muscle growth in the least time
- You recover well and sleep 7+ hours

Choose a **bro split** if:
- You are an experienced lifter and enjoy it
- You train with very high volume per session
- It keeps you motivated (enjoyment matters; the best split is the one you stick to)

Choose **full body or upper/lower** if:
- You can only train 3–4 days a week
- You are a complete beginner

# The part that matters more than your split

The split decides how you organise your week. Results come from:

- **Progressive overload:** adding a little weight or a rep over time
- **Enough hard sets:** about 10–20 sets per muscle per week for most people
- **Protein:** about 1.6 g per kg of body weight every day. See [how much protein per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day).
- **Calories:** a small surplus to build muscle, or a small deficit to lose fat. Our [Indian gym diet for muscle gain](/blog/indian-gym-diet-muscle-gain) shows a sample day.
- **Sleep:** muscle is built during recovery, not during the workout

A perfect PPL with poor food and four hours of sleep will lose to a "bro split" done with great food and great sleep.

# Frequently asked questions

**Is PPL good for beginners?**
Yes, but a 3-day full body routine is often easier for the first 2–3 months while you learn the lifts. Then move to PPL.

**Is a bro split bad?**
No. It is just less efficient for most people. If you love it and you are progressing, keep going.

**How long should a PPL workout take?**
About 60–75 minutes, including warm-up.

**Can I do PPL for fat loss?**
Yes. Lifting weights while in a calorie deficit helps you keep muscle while losing fat. Diet does most of the fat loss work.
