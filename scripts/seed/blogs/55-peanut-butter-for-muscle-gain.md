> **Quick answer:** **2 tablespoons (about 32 g) of peanut butter has about 7–8 g of protein, 16 g of fat and about 190 kcal.** It is **good for muscle gain** because it adds a lot of calories and some protein in a small, tasty serving. But it is **not a high-protein food**: most of its calories come from fat. Use it as a calorie booster, not as your main protein source.

Peanut butter is the gym-bro favourite: on toast, in shakes, straight from the jar. But is it actually good for building muscle? And how much protein does it really have?

# Peanut butter nutrition

For **2 tablespoons (about 32 g)** of regular peanut butter:

- **Calories:** about 190 kcal
- **Protein:** about 7–8 g
- **Fat:** about 16 g (mostly unsaturated)
- **Carbs:** about 6–7 g
- **Fibre:** about 2–3 g

Per **100 g**:

- **Calories:** about 590 kcal
- **Protein:** about 25 g
- **Fat:** about 50 g

So **about 15–17% of its calories come from protein** and about **75% from fat**.

# Is peanut butter good for muscle gain?

**Yes, as a calorie booster.**

To build muscle, most people need a small calorie surplus. If you are skinny and struggle to eat enough, peanut butter is one of the easiest ways to add calories:

- 2 tablespoons adds about 190 kcal in seconds
- It fits into breakfast, snacks and shakes
- It is cheap compared with mass gainers

**No, as a main protein source.**

To get 30 g of protein from peanut butter, you would need about 8 tablespoons, which is about **760 kcal**. Compare that with 100 g of cooked chicken breast: about 31 g of protein for 165 kcal.

Use peanut butter alongside proper protein sources like eggs, chicken, fish, paneer, curd, soya and dal.

# Is peanut butter good for weight loss?

It can fit, but **portion control is everything**. It is very easy to eat 4–5 tablespoons without noticing, which is 400–500 kcal.

For weight loss:

- Measure 1 tablespoon (about 95 kcal) with an actual spoon
- Spread it thinly
- Count it as your fat for that meal

# Regular vs high-protein peanut butter

Some Indian brands sell "high protein" peanut butter with added whey or pea protein. These can have **about 30–35 g of protein per 100 g** instead of 25 g. That is better, but still mostly fat. Read the label and check:

- **Protein per serving**
- **Added sugar** (many flavoured versions contain a lot)
- **Added oils** like palm oil

Our [nutrition label guide](/blog/how-to-read-nutrition-labels-india) shows what to look for.

# Which peanut butter is best?

Look for:

- **Ingredients:** ideally just peanuts and maybe salt
- **No added sugar or very little**
- **No hydrogenated oils**

Natural peanut butter separates, with oil on top. Just stir it.

# Easy ways to use peanut butter for muscle gain

1. **Peanut butter banana toast:** 2 slices of brown bread, 1 tablespoon of peanut butter, 1 sliced banana. About 400 kcal, 12–14 g protein.
2. **Homemade mass shake:** 300 ml milk, 1 banana, 1 tablespoon of peanut butter, 30 g oats. About 550 kcal, 20 g protein.
3. **Peanut butter oats:** stir a spoon into your morning oats.
4. **Chapati roll:** spread on a chapati with banana slices as a pre-workout snack.
5. **Apple slices with peanut butter** as an evening snack.

# Peanuts vs peanut butter

Plain roasted peanuts have very similar nutrition and are often cheaper. A handful (about 30 g) gives about 170 kcal and 7–8 g of protein. Boiled peanuts are a great, lower-calorie Coimbatore snack.

# Frequently asked questions

**How much peanut butter per day for muscle gain?**
1–3 tablespoons a day is common, depending on your calorie needs.

**Is peanut butter better than whey?**
No. Whey is mostly protein; peanut butter is mostly fat. They do different jobs. See [is whey protein enough to build muscle](/blog/is-whey-protein-enough-to-build-muscle).

**Can I eat peanut butter at night?**
Yes. Calories at night are not different from calories in the day. Just count them.

**Is peanut butter bad for cholesterol?**
Peanut butter fat is mostly unsaturated, which is generally considered heart-friendly in moderate amounts. Avoid versions with hydrogenated oils.
