> **Quick answer:** The best **vegan protein sources in India** are **soya chunks (about 50 g protein per 100 g dry)**, **tofu (12–17 g per 100 g firm)**, **sattu (about 20 g per 100 g)**, **peanuts (about 25 g per 100 g)**, **pumpkin seeds (about 30 g per 100 g)**, and **dal, chana, rajma and kollu (about 7–9 g per 100 g cooked)**. Combine several across the day to reach **1.2–1.6 g per kg** (more if you lift), and take a **vitamin B12 supplement**, since B12 is not reliably available from plant foods.

A vegan diet means no meat, fish, eggs or dairy, so paneer, curd, milk and whey are out too. That makes protein planning more important. Here are the best Indian vegan sources and how to use them.

# Top vegan protein sources (Indian kitchen)

**Soya chunks and granules**

- About **50–52 g protein per 100 g dry**
- 50 g dry = about 25 g protein
- Cheapest protein per rupee. See [soya chunks protein](/blog/soya-chunks-protein).

**Tofu (firm)**

- About **12–17 g per 100 g**
- Use for tikka, bhurji, curries. See [tandoori tofu tikka](/blog/tandoori-tofu-tikka-recipe).

**Sattu (roasted chana flour)**

- About **20 g per 100 g**
- Mix 3–4 tbsp in water with lemon, salt and cumin as a drink

**Peanuts and peanut butter**

- About **25 g per 100 g**
- Calorie-dense; measure portions. See [peanuts vs almonds](/blog/peanuts-vs-almonds).

**Seeds**

- **Pumpkin seeds:** about 30 g per 100 g
- **Sunflower seeds:** about 20 g per 100 g
- **Flaxseed and chia:** about 17–18 g per 100 g, plus omega-3 (ALA)

**Legumes (cooked, per 100 g)**

- **Chana, rajma, black chana:** about 7–9 g
- **Moong and masoor dal:** about 7–9 g
- **Kollu (horse gram) sprouts:** about 9 g. See [kollu sprout salad](/blog/kollu-sprout-salad-recipe).

**Whole grains (per 100 g cooked)**

- **Quinoa:** about 4–4.5 g
- **Millets and oats:** about 3–5 g

**Plant protein powders**

- Pea, soy or rice protein: about 20–25 g per scoop. Useful if you struggle to reach your target.

# How much protein do vegans need?

- **General health:** at least 1 g per kg
- **Active or losing weight:** 1.2–1.6 g per kg
- **Lifting for muscle:** 1.6–2.2 g per kg

Plant proteins are slightly less digestible and some are low in certain amino acids, so aiming at the **higher end** is sensible. See [how much protein per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day).

# Complete protein: do you need to combine?

Most plant foods are low in at least one essential amino acid. You do not need to combine them at every meal; just **eat a variety through the day**. Traditional pairings already work well:

- **Rice + dal**
- **Chapati + rajma or chana**
- **Idli or dosa (rice + urad dal)**

Soya and quinoa are complete proteins on their own.

# A vegan high-protein day (about 100 g protein)

- **Breakfast:** moong dal chilla (3) with peanut chutney (about 20 g)
- **Mid-morning:** sattu drink (about 8–10 g)
- **Lunch:** soya pulao (50 g dry soya), dal, salad (about 35 g)
- **Evening:** roasted chana and pumpkin seeds (about 12 g)
- **Dinner:** tofu bhurji (150 g tofu), 2 chapatis, rajma (about 30 g)

# Nutrients vegans must watch

- **Vitamin B12:** take a supplement; deficiency is common and serious
- **Vitamin D:** see [vitamin D deficiency in India](/blog/vitamin-d-deficiency-india)
- **Iron:** greens, dal, chana with vitamin C (lemon, amla)
- **Calcium:** calcium-set tofu, ragi, sesame, fortified plant milks
- **Omega-3:** flax, chia, walnuts, or algae-based supplements
- **Zinc and iodine:** legumes, seeds, iodised salt

Get blood tests once a year.

# Is creatine vegan?

Yes, supplement creatine is made synthetically. Vegans often benefit more. See [creatine for vegetarians](/blog/creatine-for-vegetarians).

# Frequently asked questions

**Can vegans build muscle?**
Yes, with enough total protein, a variety of sources and progressive strength training.

**Is soya safe to eat daily?**
For most people, about 30–50 g dry a day is a safe amount.

**What is the cheapest vegan protein in India?**
Soya chunks, followed by chana, peanuts and dal.
