> **Quick answer:** Before a workout, eat a carb-based meal 2–3 hours ahead, or a small snack like a banana 30–60 minutes before. Afterwards, have a meal with 20–40 g of protein and some carbs within a couple of hours. Your total protein and calories for the day matter more than exact timing, so do not panic if you cannot drink a shake within 30 minutes.

Gym advice about timing can sound very strict: eat this at exactly this minute, or your workout is wasted. In reality, timing is a fine-tuning tool. It helps, but it comes after getting the daily basics right.

Here is a simple approach, using normal Indian food.

# Before your workout

The goal is simple: enough fuel to train well, without feeling heavy.

## 2–3 hours before: a proper meal

Have carbs, moderate protein and not too much fat. For example:

- 2 chapatis with dal or egg curry
- 1 cup rice with fish or chicken and poriyal
- Oats pongal with curd
- 3 idlis with sambar and 1–2 boiled eggs

## 30–60 minutes before: a small snack

Keep it light and low in fat and fibre, as both slow digestion:

- A banana
- 2 dates and a small handful of puffed rice
- A small idli with a little sambar
- A slice of bread with a little honey or peanut butter

## Early morning gym, no time to eat?

Many people train comfortably without eating, particularly for shorter sessions. If you feel weak, dizzy or perform poorly, try a banana or a couple of dates before you leave. Hydrate either way. If you have diabetes or take medicines that can lower your blood sugar, ask your doctor before training on an empty stomach.

## What about coffee?

Black coffee 30–60 minutes before training can help some people feel more alert and perform a little better. Do not use it to cover up for poor sleep, avoid it late in the day, and skip it if it makes you jittery or upsets your stomach.

# After your workout

The goal is to give your muscles protein, replace some of the energy you used, and rehydrate.

**A good target is 20–40 g of protein**, with bigger people and harder sessions towards the top of that range. Add carbs depending on your goal.

Good post-workout meals include:

- **Grilled chicken, 2 chapatis and corn salad**
- **3 eggs in a light curry with 1 cup of rice**
- **Paneer bhurji with chapatis and a cup of curd**
- **Soya chunk pulao with raita**
- **Grilled fish, rice, dal and vegetables**

# How real is the "anabolic window"?

The idea was that you had about 30 minutes after training to eat protein, or you would lose your gains. Later research shows that this window is much wider than once believed, particularly if you ate a meal a few hours before training.

**In practice:**

- If you ate 2–3 hours before training, a meal within a couple of hours afterwards is fine.
- If you trained fasted, it makes more sense to eat sooner.
- If you train twice in a day, timing and carbs matter more, because you have less time to recover.

For most gym-goers, the far bigger question is whether you hit your protein across the day. A common target for muscle gain is **1.6–2.2 g per kg of body weight**, split across 3–4 meals. See our [Indian gym diet guide](/blog/indian-gym-diet-muscle-gain).

# Sample days

## 6:30 AM gym session

- **6:00 AM:** Banana and water
- **8:00 AM:** Protein breakfast, such as egg bhurji, chapati and curd
- **1:00 PM:** Rice, chicken or paneer, dal and vegetables
- **8:00 PM:** A dinner with protein and salad

## 7:00 PM gym session

- **8:00 AM:** Protein breakfast
- **1:00 PM:** Balanced lunch
- **5:30 PM:** Boiled eggs or sundal, and a banana
- **8:30 PM:** Post-workout dinner with protein and carbs

# Does this change for fat loss?

The timing principles stay the same, but your total calories should be lower. Keep the post-workout protein and be careful with extras: post-workout juice, large shakes and "I earned this" treats can undo a calorie deficit quickly. If you need help working out a target, read our [calorie deficit guide](/blog/calorie-deficit-how-to-calculate).

# Do not forget water

Drink through the day, and sip during training. In Coimbatore's warmer months, you can sweat a lot during an intense session. For most gym workouts under an hour, water is enough. For long, sweaty sessions, you may also need some electrolytes.

# Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a protein shake after the gym?**
No. A shake is convenient, but food works too. Read our guide on [protein powder vs real food](/blog/protein-powder-vs-real-food-india).

**Should I eat carbs after a workout if I want to lose fat?**
You can. Carbs help recovery and do not automatically turn into fat. Just count them as part of your daily calories.

**Is it bad to work out on an empty stomach?**
Not for most healthy people, if you feel good and perform well. Some people do better with a small snack first, so test it and see.

**How long after eating should I wait before training?**
Allow about 2–3 hours after a full meal and 30–60 minutes after a small snack. Adjust according to how your stomach feels.
