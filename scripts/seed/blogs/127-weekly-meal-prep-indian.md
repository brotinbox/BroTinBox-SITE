> **Quick answer:** For **Indian weekly meal prep**, spend **2–3 hours on Sunday** cooking **proteins** (grilled or boiled chicken, boiled eggs, chana, rajma, soya), **bases** (chapati dough, cooked rice or millets), and **prep vegetables** (chopped onions, tomatoes, cut salad veg). Store in airtight boxes in the fridge: cooked meat and dal for **2–3 days**, boiled eggs for **up to a week**, and **cook a second, smaller batch on Wednesday**. Each day, you just assemble and heat.

Cooking healthy food every single day is exhausting. Meal prep turns 7 cooking sessions into 2. Here is a simple Indian meal prep plan that works with rice, chapati, dal and curries.

# Why meal prep works

- **Saves time:** 2 sessions a week instead of 7–14
- **Controls portions:** you measure once
- **Stops impulse orders** when you are tired and hungry
- **Keeps protein high** because it is always ready

# Food safety first

- **Cool cooked food within 1–2 hours** and refrigerate
- **Cooked meat, fish and dal:** use within **2–3 days**
- **Cooked rice:** cool quickly, refrigerate, use within **1–2 days**, and reheat until steaming hot
- **Boiled eggs (in shell):** up to **7 days** in the fridge
- **Freeze** anything you will eat after day 3
- **Fish:** cook fresh or within a day; it does not keep as well

# The Sunday session (2–3 hours)

## Proteins

- **Chicken:** boil or grill 750 g–1 kg. Shred half for [smashed chicken](/blog/smashed-chicken-bowl-recipe); slice the rest.
- **Eggs:** boil 10–12
- **Legumes:** pressure cook 2 cups of chana or rajma (soaked overnight)
- **Soya:** boil and squeeze 150 g soya chunks; store plain, season when you cook
- **Paneer or tofu:** buy fresh; marinate a batch to grill on the day

## Bases

- **Chapati dough:** knead for 2–3 days; keep covered in the fridge
- **Rice or millets:** cook for 1–2 days only
- **Oats:** keep dry; quick to cook daily

## Vegetables

- **Chop onions, tomatoes, capsicum, beans and carrots**; store in boxes lined with paper towel
- **Wash and dry salad vegetables**; cut cucumber on the day
- **Make one vegetable dish** (poriyal or kootu) for 2 days

## Extras

- **Mint-coriander chutney** (3–4 days)
- **Hung curd** for marinades and dips. See [curd vs Greek yoghurt](/blog/curd-vs-greek-yogurt).
- **Roasted chana and peanuts** portioned into small boxes for snacks

# The Wednesday top-up (45–60 minutes)

- Cook a fresh batch of chicken or soya
- Boil a fresh pot of dal or chana
- Cook rice for Thursday–Friday
- Chop fresh vegetables

# A sample week of lunches

- **Mon:** smashed chicken, rice, chana, salad
- **Tue:** paneer tikka, chapati, dal, salad
- **Wed:** egg curry, rice, poriyal
- **Thu:** grilled chicken wrap with chapati and mint chutney
- **Fri:** soya pulao with raita
- **Sat:** chana masala, chapati, sprouts salad

All around **30–50 g protein** each. For more ideas, see [homemade protein meals](/blog/homemade-protein-meals) and [high-protein veg lunch box ideas](/blog/high-protein-veg-lunch-box-ideas).

# Containers that help

- **Steel or glass boxes** with tight lids
- **Divided lunch boxes** to keep wet and dry items apart
- **Small 100–150 ml boxes** for curd, chutney and dressings
- **Label with the date**

# Daily routine (10–15 minutes)

1. Take out the protein and base for the day
2. Heat the protein; make 2 fresh chapatis or heat rice
3. Add salad and curd
4. Pack and go

# When meal prep is not enough

Meal prep fails when life gets busy: late meetings, travel, family events. Have a backup plan: a few frozen portions, or a meal service for the busiest days.

# Frequently asked questions

**Can I meal prep for the whole week?**
For proteins and rice, cook twice a week. Freezing helps for longer.

**Does reheated chicken lose protein?**
No. Protein stays the same; only texture changes.

**Is it safe to reheat rice?**
Yes, if it was cooled quickly, refrigerated, and reheated until steaming hot.
