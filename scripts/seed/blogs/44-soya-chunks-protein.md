> **Quick answer:** **100 g of dry soya chunks has about 52 g of protein** and about 345 kcal. Once soaked and cooked, they absorb water and roughly triple in weight, so **100 g of cooked soya chunks has about 15–18 g of protein**. A normal serving of **30–50 g dry** gives about **15–26 g of protein**. Research shows soya in normal amounts does **not lower testosterone** or cause "man boobs" in men.

Soya chunks (also called meal maker or nutri nuggets) are the cheapest high-protein food most Indians can buy. But they come with a lot of myths. Here are the real numbers, and the truth about soya and men's hormones.

# Soya chunks nutrition per 100 g (dry)

- **Protein:** about 52 g
- **Carbs:** about 33 g
- **Fibre:** about 13 g
- **Fat:** about 0.5–1 g
- **Calories:** about 345 kcal

That is more protein per 100 g than chicken breast (about 31 g cooked). It is also very low in fat and high in fibre.

# Dry vs cooked: the key difference

Soya chunks absorb a lot of water. As a rough guide:

- **30 g dry** becomes about 90–100 g cooked
- **50 g dry** becomes about 150 g cooked

So:

- **30 g dry soya:** about 15–16 g protein, about 105 kcal
- **50 g dry soya:** about 26 g protein, about 170 kcal
- **100 g cooked soya:** about 15–18 g protein

When you see "52 g protein" on the pack, remember it refers to the dry weight.

# Is soya protein good quality?

Yes. Soya is one of the few plant proteins that is a **complete protein**, meaning it has all nine essential amino acids. Its quality score is close to animal proteins. That makes it one of the best options for vegetarians trying to hit a high protein target.

# Soya chunks and men: the testosterone question

This is the biggest worry. The concern comes from **isoflavones**, plant compounds in soya that have a weak estrogen-like effect.

What the research says:

- A large 2021 review that pooled dozens of clinical studies found that soya protein and isoflavones **did not significantly change testosterone or estrogen levels in men**.
- Most reported problems come from rare case reports of men eating extremely large amounts of soya every day for long periods.

At normal amounts, like **25–50 g of dry soya chunks a day**, soya is considered safe for men.

# Are there any side effects?

Some people notice:

- **Gas or bloating**, because of the high fibre. Start with a small portion and soak well.
- **Allergy**, in people with a soya allergy (rare in adults, but real).

If you have **thyroid problems** and take thyroid medicine, take your medicine at least 4 hours apart from soya-heavy meals, and follow your doctor's advice.

# How much soya should you eat a day?

A sensible range for most people: **25–50 g of dry soya chunks a day**. It is fine to eat soya several times a week alongside other proteins like dal, paneer, curd, eggs or chicken.

# How to cook soya chunks so they taste good

The secret is preparation:

1. **Boil** the chunks in salted water for 5–7 minutes.
2. **Squeeze out the water** completely. This removes the "raw soya" smell.
3. **Rinse and squeeze again.**
4. **Marinate** in curd, ginger-garlic paste, chilli powder and salt for 15–30 minutes.

Easy dishes:

- **Soya chunks pepper fry**, Chettinad style
- **Soya chunks curry** with onion and tomato
- **Soya biryani or pulao**, with more soya and less rice
- **Smashed soya**: lightly mashed and spiced, great with chapati or oats
- **Soya and vegetable stir-fry**

Smashed soya chunks show up regularly in our veg boxes. They are one of the easiest ways to get a high-protein vegetarian lunch.

# Soya chunks vs paneer vs chicken

Per typical serving:

- **50 g dry soya (about 150 g cooked):** about 26 g protein, about 170 kcal
- **100 g paneer:** about 18–20 g protein, about 260–320 kcal
- **100 g cooked chicken breast:** about 31 g protein, about 165 kcal

For vegetarians, soya gives the most protein for the fewest calories and the lowest cost. See our full [chicken vs eggs vs paneer vs soya](/blog/chicken-eggs-paneer-soya-protein-comparison) comparison and [how much protein in paneer](/blog/protein-in-paneer).

# Frequently asked questions

**Can I eat soya chunks every day?**
Yes, in moderate amounts (25–50 g dry). Mix them with other protein sources over the week.

**Are soya chunks good for weight loss?**
Yes. They are high in protein and fibre and low in fat, which helps keep you full on fewer calories.

**Are soya chunks good for muscle gain?**
Yes. They are a complete protein and work well alongside other protein sources.

**Do soya chunks cause hair fall?**
There is no good evidence for this.
