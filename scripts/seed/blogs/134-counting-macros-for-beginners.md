> **Quick answer:** **Counting macros** means tracking your daily **protein, carbs and fat** (and therefore calories), instead of just "eating clean". To start: **(1)** set your **calories**, **(2)** set **protein at 1.6–2.2 g per kg** for training or **1.2–1.6 g per kg** for weight loss, **(3)** set **fat at about 0.6–1 g per kg**, **(4)** fill the rest with **carbs**. Use a food tracking app and a **kitchen scale for 2–4 weeks** to learn portions. After that, many people can estimate well without weighing everything.

Macro counting, often called **IIFYM** ("if it fits your macros"), is one of the most flexible ways to diet. No food is banned; you just fit it into your numbers. Here is a beginner's guide with Indian food.

# The three macros

**Protein** (4 kcal per gram)

- Builds and repairs muscle, keeps you full
- Sources: eggs, chicken, fish, paneer, tofu, soya, dal, curd, milk

**Carbohydrates** (4 kcal per gram)

- Main energy source, fuels training
- Sources: rice, chapati, idli, dosa, oats, millets, fruit, vegetables

**Fat** (9 kcal per gram)

- Hormones, vitamin absorption, flavour
- Sources: oil, ghee, nuts, seeds, egg yolks, fish, paneer

**Calories = (protein × 4) + (carbs × 4) + (fat × 9)**

# Step 1: Set your calories

Use [how many calories should I eat](/blog/how-many-calories-should-i-eat). Then:

- **Fat loss:** maintenance − 300 to 500
- **Muscle gain:** maintenance + 200 to 400

# Step 2: Set protein

- **Fat loss:** 1.2–1.6 g per kg (up to 2 g if training hard)
- **Muscle gain:** 1.6–2.2 g per kg

# Step 3: Set fat

About **0.6–1 g per kg** (roughly 20–35% of calories). Do not go too low; fat is needed for hormones.

# Step 4: Fill the rest with carbs

**Carbs (g) = (calories − protein kcal − fat kcal) ÷ 4**

# Worked example

**75 kg man, fat loss, target 2,000 kcal**

- **Protein:** 1.8 g × 75 = **135 g** → 540 kcal
- **Fat:** 0.8 g × 75 = **60 g** → 540 kcal
- **Carbs:** (2,000 − 540 − 540) ÷ 4 = **230 g**

So: **135 g protein, 60 g fat, 230 g carbs**.

# How to track

1. **Download a tracking app** (for example HealthifyMe or MyFitnessPal); both have many Indian foods
2. **Buy a kitchen scale** (₹300–700)
3. **Weigh food raw** where possible, or use consistent cooked weights
4. **Log as you eat,** not at the end of the day
5. **Save common meals** to log quickly

# Tricky parts with Indian food

- **Oil and ghee:** the biggest hidden source. Measure with a spoon; 1 tbsp = about 120 kcal and 14 g fat.
- **Home curries:** log ingredients for the whole pot, then divide by servings.
- **Restaurant food:** estimate generously; it often has more oil than you think.
- **Rice:** weigh cooked rice; 1 cup (about 150 g) = about 180–200 kcal.
- **Dal:** a mix of protein and carbs; count both.

See [how to read nutrition labels](/blog/how-to-read-nutrition-labels-india) and [calories in South Indian food](/blog/calories-in-south-indian-food).

# Do you need to hit macros exactly?

No. Aim for:

- **Calories:** within about ±100 kcal
- **Protein:** within about ±10 g
- **Fat and carbs:** roughly; they are flexible

Consistency over weeks matters more than perfect days.

# Flexible, not unlimited

IIFYM allows treats, but:

- **Most food should still be nutritious** (vegetables, protein, whole grains)
- **Fibre** matters: aim for 25–35 g a day
- **Micronutrients** come from variety

# When to stop counting

Many people count for **4–8 weeks**, learn their portions, then switch to the plate method and occasional check-ins.

# Frequently asked questions

**Is macro counting better than calorie counting?**
It adds protein focus, which helps body composition. Calories still matter most for weight change.

**Is counting macros hard with Indian food?**
It takes a couple of weeks to learn, mainly because of oil and mixed dishes.

**Can I eat sweets on IIFYM?**
Yes, if they fit your calories and you still hit your protein.
