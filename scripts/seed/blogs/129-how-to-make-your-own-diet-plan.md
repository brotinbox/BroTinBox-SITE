> **Quick answer:** To make your own diet plan: **(1)** set a goal (lose, gain or maintain), **(2)** estimate your **calories**, **(3)** set your **protein** (1.2–2.2 g per kg), **(4)** pick **3–4 meal times** that fit your day, **(5)** build each meal with the **plate method** (protein + vegetables + measured carb), **(6)** write a simple **7-day menu** using foods you already eat, and **(7)** **track and adjust every 2 weeks** based on your weight average and waist. Keep it simple enough to follow for months.

You do not need an expensive dietitian plan or an app subscription to eat well. With a few numbers and Indian food you already eat, you can build a plan that works. Here is how.

# Step 1: Choose your goal

- **Lose fat**
- **Gain muscle**
- **Maintain and eat healthier**

One goal at a time. If you have a medical condition (diabetes, kidney disease, PCOS, thyroid), check with your doctor first.

# Step 2: Estimate your calories

Use [how many calories should I eat](/blog/how-many-calories-should-i-eat) to find your maintenance. Then:

- **Lose fat:** maintenance − 300 to 500 kcal
- **Gain muscle:** maintenance + 200 to 400 kcal
- **Maintain:** maintenance

# Step 3: Set protein

- **Weight loss:** 1.2–1.6 g per kg
- **Muscle gain:** 1.6–2.2 g per kg
- **General health:** at least 1 g per kg

**Example:** 70 kg, weight loss = about 85–110 g protein a day.

# Step 4: Pick your meal times

Fit your plan to your life, not the other way around.

- **3 meals + 1 snack** works for most people
- **Night shift?** Shift the times, keep the structure
- **Intermittent fasting?** See [intermittent fasting on an Indian diet](/blog/intermittent-fasting-indian-diet)

Divide protein roughly evenly: 25–40 g per meal.

# Step 5: Build each meal with the plate method

- **Half the plate:** vegetables, salad, poriyal, keerai
- **A quarter:** protein (eggs, chicken, fish, paneer, tofu, soya, dal)
- **A quarter:** carbs (1 cup rice, 2 chapatis, 2–3 idlis, oats or millets)
- **Side:** curd or buttermilk
- **Oil:** 1–2 tsp per person per meal

See [what is a balanced diet](/blog/what-is-a-balanced-diet).

# Step 6: Write a 7-day menu

Use foods you already like. Repeat breakfasts and lunches; vary dinners.

**Example (about 1,600 kcal, weight loss, non-veg)**

**Breakfast (rotate 2–3 options)**

- 2 idlis + sambar + 2 boiled eggs
- Moong dal chilla (2) + curd
- Oats with milk + banana + 2 boiled eggs

**Lunch**

- 1 cup rice + 150 g chicken or fish + dal + poriyal + salad + curd

**Snack**

- Buttermilk + sundal or sprouts

**Dinner (rotate)**

- 2 chapatis + paneer bhurji + vegetables
- 2 chapatis + egg curry + salad
- 2 dosas + chicken pepper fry + sambar
- Millet roti + dal + vegetables

Vegetarian? Swap chicken and fish for paneer, tofu or soya.

# Step 7: Shop and prep

- Make a **shopping list** from your menu
- **Prep proteins** twice a week. See [weekly meal prep, Indian style](/blog/weekly-meal-prep-indian).
- Keep **easy protein snacks** at home

# Step 8: Track and adjust

Every 2 weeks, look at:

- **Weekly average weight**
- **Waist measurement**
- **Energy, hunger and training**

Then adjust:

- **Weight loss stalled for 2+ weeks?** Cut 100–200 kcal or add 2,000 steps
- **Gaining too fast on a bulk?** Cut 100–150 kcal
- **Always hungry?** Add protein and vegetables, not more rice

# Rules that make any plan work

- **Make it boring on weekdays,** flexible on weekends
- **Protein first** at every meal
- **Do not drink your calories**
- **Plan for functions and eating out**
- **Never miss twice.** See [how to stay consistent with a diet](/blog/how-to-stay-consistent-with-diet).

# Frequently asked questions

**Do I need an app?**
It helps for the first few weeks to learn portions. After that, the plate method is enough for many people.

**Should I cut rice completely?**
No. Measure it instead.

**How often should I change my plan?**
Only when progress stops for 2+ weeks, or your goal changes.
