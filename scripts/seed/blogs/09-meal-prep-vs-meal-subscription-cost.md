> **Quick answer:** Cooking at home is usually cheaper in raw ingredient cost per meal. A meal subscription often comes out close once you count **time (5–7 hours a week), gas, wasted groceries, and the meals you end up ordering** when you skip a prep day. If your time is valuable or your home cooking is inconsistent, a measured subscription is often the better deal for results.

"Why pay for a meal subscription when I can cook it myself?" It is a fair question, and the answer depends on what you count.

This guide walks through the real costs of meal prepping a high-protein diet at home versus subscribing, so you can decide with clear numbers instead of guesses.

# What a high-protein meal actually requires

To compare fairly, we need to compare the same meal. A typical BroTin Box contains:

- **150 g cooked chicken** (or soya, paneer, or 130 g mackerel)
- **2 boiled eggs**
- **100 g legumes** (chana, green gram, rajma, peanuts or kollu)
- **A salad** (corn, cabbage, cucumber, coleslaw or broccoli)
- **A measured carb** (2 chapatis, oats upma or oats pongal)
- **About 58–81 g protein** in the non-veg boxes

That is a lot of food, and a lot of components to cook separately.

# The cost of cooking it yourself

## 1. Ingredients
This is the obvious part. Chicken, eggs, legumes, vegetables, oats or atta, spices and oil. Prices vary by shop and season, so use your own local prices with this simple formula:

**Cost per meal = (raw chicken per meal + 2 eggs + legumes + vegetables + carb + oil and spices)**

Two things people often get wrong:

- **Raw vs cooked weight.** Chicken loses roughly 25% of its weight when cooked. To get 150 g cooked, you need to buy about **200 g raw**.
- **Bone and trim.** If you buy bone-in chicken, the usable meat is even less.

## 2. Time
This is the cost almost everyone ignores. A realistic weekly prep for six high-protein meals:

- Shopping: **1–1.5 hours**
- Cleaning, chopping, marinating: **1.5–2 hours**
- Cooking several components: **2–2.5 hours**
- Washing up and packing: **1 hour**

That is **5–7 hours a week**. Put any value on your hour and the numbers change quickly.

## 3. Gas, electricity and containers
Cooking several components every week adds to your gas and electricity bill. Storage containers wear out.

## 4. Waste
Vegetables wilt, legumes get forgotten, and half a packet of paneer expires. Most households throw away some of what they buy.

## 5. The "skip day" cost
This is the big one. Life gets busy, you skip a prep session, and you order a ₹250–₹400 restaurant meal that is higher in oil and lower in protein. Two skipped days a week can wipe out all of the savings, and your progress too.

# Side-by-side: what you are really comparing

## Cooking at home
- **Lower** ingredient cost per meal
- **5–7 hours** of your week
- Portions and oil are **estimated**, unless you weigh everything yourself
- Same 2–3 dishes most weeks (variety takes even more time)
- Easy to fall off when life gets busy

## Measured meal subscription
- **Higher** price per meal than raw ingredients
- **Zero** prep time, nothing to wash
- Every component **weighed**, macros printed on the box
- A **different box every day** of the week
- Consistency is built in: food arrives whether you feel motivated or not

# Who should cook at home?

- You **enjoy cooking** and see it as relaxation.
- You have **reliable time** every week to prep.
- Your budget is **very tight** and every rupee matters.
- You are happy to **weigh food** to keep portions accurate.

# Who should subscribe?

- You work **long hours** or travel for work.
- You have **tried meal prep before** and fell off after two weeks.
- You want **exact macros** without weighing and tracking.
- You value **5–7 free hours** a week more than the price difference.
