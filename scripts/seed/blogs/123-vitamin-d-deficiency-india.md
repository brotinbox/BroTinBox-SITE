> **Quick answer:** **Vitamin D deficiency is very common in India**, even with plenty of sunshine, because many people stay indoors, cover up, have darker skin and eat few vitamin D foods. It can cause **tiredness, muscle weakness, aches, low mood and weaker bones**, and may affect training and recovery. A **blood test (25-hydroxy vitamin D)** confirms it. Get **sunlight on your arms and legs for 15–30 minutes** several days a week, eat **fish, eggs and fortified foods**, and take supplements **only as advised by your doctor**, since very high doses can be harmful.

Many gym-goers in India eat well and train hard, yet feel tired, sore and weak. A common hidden reason is low vitamin D. Here is what you need to know.

# Why vitamin D matters

- **Bones:** helps absorb calcium and keep bones strong
- **Muscles:** supports muscle function and strength
- **Immunity:** plays a role in immune health
- **Mood and energy:** low levels are linked with fatigue and low mood

For people who train, low vitamin D may mean more aches, slower recovery and a higher risk of stress injuries.

# Why it is so common in India

Despite the sunshine, studies across India have found low vitamin D in a large share of adults. Reasons include:

- **Indoor jobs and lifestyles:** office, college, home
- **Covered clothing** and avoiding the sun
- **Darker skin,** which needs more sun to make vitamin D
- **Pollution** reducing UVB rays
- **Few vitamin D foods** in typical vegetarian diets
- **Sunscreen use** (good for skin, but reduces vitamin D production)

# Symptoms

Many people have **no clear symptoms**. When present, they can include:

- Tiredness and low energy
- Muscle weakness and aches
- Bone or lower back pain
- Frequent infections
- Low mood
- Slow recovery after training

These overlap with many other problems, so do not self-diagnose.

# How to test

Ask your doctor for a **25-hydroxy vitamin D** blood test. It is widely available in Coimbatore labs.

Commonly used ranges (labs vary):

- **Deficient:** below about 20 ng/mL
- **Insufficient:** about 20–29 ng/mL
- **Sufficient:** about 30 ng/mL and above

Your doctor will interpret your result along with your health.

# How to raise vitamin D

## 1. Sunlight

- **15–30 minutes** of sun on arms and legs, several days a week
- **Late morning to early afternoon** sun has more UVB, but be careful of sunburn and heat in Coimbatore summers
- **Through a window does not work;** glass blocks UVB

## 2. Food

Few foods are rich in vitamin D, but these help:

- **Oily fish:** mackerel, sardines, salmon. See [mackerel benefits](/blog/mackerel-benefits-omega-3-indian-diet).
- **Egg yolks.** See [egg white vs whole egg](/blog/egg-white-vs-whole-egg).
- **Fortified milk and oils** (check labels)
- **Mushrooms exposed to sunlight** (small amounts)

Vegetarians have fewer food options and often need sunlight or supplements.

## 3. Supplements (with your doctor)

- Doctors often prescribe **weekly or daily vitamin D3** for a set period, then a maintenance dose
- **Do not take high-dose sachets or injections on your own.** Too much vitamin D can raise calcium levels and harm the kidneys and heart
- Recheck levels after a few months, as advised

# Vitamin D and the gym

- Strength training and good nutrition still matter most
- If your levels are low, correcting them may help **energy, muscle function and bone health**
- Pair with enough **calcium** (curd, milk, paneer, ragi) and **protein**. See [how much protein per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day).

# Other common deficiencies to check

- **Vitamin B12,** especially in vegetarians
- **Iron,** especially in women
- **Thyroid function** if you feel very tired

A basic health check once a year is worth it.

# Frequently asked questions

**Can I get enough vitamin D from food alone?**
It is hard, especially for vegetarians. Sunlight is the main natural source.

**Does vitamin D help muscle growth?**
If you are deficient, correcting it supports muscle function. If your levels are normal, extra vitamin D does not build more muscle.

**Is it safe to take vitamin D every day?**
At the dose your doctor recommends, yes. Avoid very high doses without supervision.
