> **Quick answer:** Per 100 g, **soya chunks (dry)** have the most protein (about 52 g), followed by **cooked chicken breast** (about 31 g), **paneer** (about 18–20 g) and **eggs** (about 13 g, or 6 g per egg). Chicken and soya give the most protein per calorie, paneer is the most calorie-dense because of its fat, and eggs are the most versatile. The best plan uses more than one.

Chicken, eggs, paneer and soya are the four protein sources most Indians rely on. Gym-goers argue about which is "best". The honest answer: each one wins on something different.

Here is a clear comparison so you can choose based on your goal, diet and budget. All values are approximate.

# Nutrition per 100 g

## Chicken breast (cooked, skinless)
- **Protein:** about 31 g
- **Calories:** about 165 kcal
- **Fat:** about 3–4 g
- **Protein per 100 kcal:** about 19 g

## Whole eggs (about 2 large eggs)
- **Protein:** about 13 g (about 6 g per egg)
- **Calories:** about 145 kcal
- **Fat:** about 10 g
- **Protein per 100 kcal:** about 9 g

## Paneer (full fat)
- **Protein:** about 18–20 g
- **Calories:** about 265–320 kcal
- **Fat:** about 20–25 g
- **Protein per 100 kcal:** about 6–7 g

## Soya chunks (dry weight)
- **Protein:** about 52 g
- **Calories:** about 345 kcal
- **Fat:** under 1 g
- **Protein per 100 kcal:** about 15 g

Remember: soya chunks soak up water and grow in size, so 100 g dry becomes much more on the plate. In our Tuesday veg box, **110 g of cooked soya chunks** gives **26 g of protein**.

# Which one wins for your goal?

## For fat loss: chicken breast and soya
They give the most protein for the fewest calories. That means you can eat a filling portion and stay in your calorie budget.

## For muscle gain: all four, combined
When you are eating in a surplus, paneer and whole eggs help you reach your calories more easily. Mix them with chicken or soya to hit your protein number.

## For vegetarians: soya + paneer + legumes
Soya is the most protein-dense vegetarian option. Paneer adds variety and richness. Legumes like chana, rajma and kollu fill the gaps and add fibre.

## For convenience: eggs
Boiled, scrambled, omelette, in a dosa: eggs take five minutes and need no planning.

# Protein quality: are they all "complete"?

All four are **complete proteins**, meaning they contain all nine essential amino acids:

- **Eggs and dairy (paneer)** are often used as reference standards for protein quality.
- **Chicken** is a high-quality animal protein.
- **Soya** is one of the few plant proteins that is complete on its own, and its quality score is close to animal proteins.

# Common worries, answered

## "Does soya affect hormones in men?"
At normal food amounts, research has not shown that soya lowers testosterone or affects male hormones in a meaningful way. A serving of soya chunks a day is fine for most people.

## "Are eggs bad for cholesterol?"
For most healthy people, 1–3 eggs a day as part of a balanced diet is fine. If you have high cholesterol, diabetes or heart disease, ask your doctor what suits you.

## "Is paneer fattening?"
Paneer is high in fat, so portions matter. 100 g of full-fat paneer can carry 265–320 kcal. It is a great food, just measure it.

# Frequently asked questions

**Which is cheapest per gram of protein?**
Soya chunks and eggs are usually the cheapest sources of protein in Indian markets, followed by chicken. Paneer is usually the most expensive per gram of protein.

**Can I eat soya every day?**
Yes, one serving a day is fine for most people. Variety across the week is still a good idea.

**Is fish better than chicken?**
Fish like mackerel gives a little less protein per 100 g than chicken breast but adds omega-3 fats. Both are excellent; rotating them is ideal.
