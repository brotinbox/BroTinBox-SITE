> **Quick answer:** One medium idli has about **55–65 kcal**, a plain dosa **120–170 kcal**, a masala dosa **250–350 kcal**, one parotta **250–320 kcal**, a cup of ven pongal **280–350 kcal**, and a restaurant plate of chicken biryani **600–900 kcal**. The biggest swing in every dish comes from oil and ghee: each tablespoon adds about **120 kcal**.

South Indian food has a reputation for being light. Some of it is: idli, rasam and sundal are genuinely low in calories. Some of it is not: parotta, ghee roast, biryani and anything deep-fried can quietly add up to a full day's calories in one meal.

Use this guide to estimate what you eat. All numbers are approximate. They change with portion size, recipe, and especially how much oil or ghee goes in.

# Why the same dish can vary by 300 calories

Two plates of ven pongal can look the same and differ hugely. The reasons:

1. **Oil and ghee.** One tablespoon of oil is about 120 kcal. One tablespoon of ghee is about 115–120 kcal. Restaurants often use two or three times what a home kitchen uses.
2. **Portion size.** A "cup" at a hotel can be 150 g or 300 g.
3. **Batter and stuffing.** Masala, cheese, or extra potato change everything.
4. **Refills.** Unlimited meals make tracking almost impossible.

This is why counting calories from a generic chart only gets you close. If your goal is fat loss, being 300 kcal off every day can erase your entire deficit.

# Smarter swaps that keep the taste

- Choose **pesarattu or ragi dosa** over ghee roast for more fibre and protein.
- Ask for **chutney on the side** and use half.
- Pair idli or dosa with a **protein side**: boiled eggs, sundal or a small bowl of curd.
- Swap **parotta for chapati**: two chapatis are about 190 kcal, versus 500+ for two parottas.
- Fill half your plate with **vegetables or salad** before adding rice.

# Frequently asked questions

**Is idli good for weight loss?**
Idli is low in calories and fat, but also low in protein. On its own it will not keep you full for long. Pair it with a protein source and go easy on the chutney.

**Which South Indian breakfast has the most protein?**
Pesarattu, egg dosa, and anything served with sundal or eggs. See our list of [15 high-protein Indian breakfasts](/blog/high-protein-indian-breakfast-ideas).

**How many calories should I eat in a day?**
It depends on your size, activity and goal. Most moderately active adults maintain weight somewhere between 1,800 and 2,600 kcal. For fat loss, a deficit of 300–500 kcal per day is a sustainable starting point. See our [7-day Indian weight loss meal plan](/blog/indian-weight-loss-meal-plan-7-days).
