> **Quick answer:** **Brown rice and white rice have almost the same calories** (about **110–130 kcal per 100 g cooked**). Brown rice has **more fibre** (about 1.5–2 g vs 0.3–0.5 g per 100 g), more magnesium and B vitamins, and raises blood sugar a little more slowly. For **weight loss, portion size matters far more than the colour of your rice**. Good middle options in South India are **parboiled (puzhungal) rice** and **red or matta rice**.

Many people switch to brown rice the moment they start a diet. Is it really better? Here is a clear comparison, plus local rice options that are often easier to enjoy.

# Nutrition per 100 g cooked

**White rice** (raw, polished, like ponni)

- Calories: about 120–130 kcal
- Carbs: about 28 g
- Protein: about 2.5 g
- Fibre: about 0.3–0.5 g

**Brown rice**

- Calories: about 110–125 kcal
- Carbs: about 25 g
- Protein: about 2.5–3 g
- Fibre: about 1.5–2 g
- More magnesium, B vitamins and some antioxidants

Exact numbers vary by variety and how much water it is cooked in.

# Blood sugar

- **White rice** has a higher glycaemic index (GI), so it raises blood sugar faster.
- **Brown rice** has a moderately lower GI because of the bran layer.
- **Parboiled rice** also has a lower GI than raw white rice.

What you eat **with** rice matters a lot. Rice with dal, vegetables, curd and protein raises blood sugar more slowly than plain rice.

People with diabetes benefit from smaller portions, more fibre and protein at the meal, and choosing less polished rice. Discuss your plan with your doctor.

# Which is better for weight loss?

**The portion.** One cup of brown rice and one cup of white rice have nearly the same calories.

Brown rice may help a little because its fibre keeps you fuller. But two cups of brown rice will still have more calories than one cup of white rice.

Read [is rice bad for weight loss](/blog/is-rice-bad-for-weight-loss) for how much rice fits a weight loss plan.

# Downsides of brown rice

- **Taste and texture:** chewier and nuttier; many South Indian families find it hard to switch
- **Cooking time:** longer, needs soaking
- **Digestion:** some people find it heavy
- **Arsenic:** rice can absorb arsenic from soil and water, and the bran holds more. Washing well and cooking in extra water that you drain off reduces it. This is a reason to vary your grains, not to panic.
- **Shelf life:** goes rancid faster because of the oils in the bran

# Local options worth trying

- **Parboiled rice (puzhungal arisi):** common in Tamil Nadu, lower GI than raw rice, keeps some nutrients
- **Red rice / matta rice:** more fibre, earthy taste, great with sambar and fish curry
- **Hand-pounded rice:** less polished, more fibre
- **Millets:** ragi, kambu, thinai and samai as a partial swap. See [millets for weight loss](/blog/millets-for-weight-loss-tamil-nadu).

# How to make any rice meal healthier

- **Measure:** about 1 cup cooked rice per meal (about 150 g) for weight loss
- **Fill half the plate with vegetables**
- **Add protein:** dal, chana, eggs, chicken or fish
- **Add curd:** it slows digestion
- **Cool and reheat:** cooked and cooled rice forms some resistant starch, which may slightly reduce its blood sugar impact

# So which should you eat?

- **Weight loss:** either, in a measured portion with protein and vegetables
- **Diabetes:** less polished rice (brown, red, parboiled), smaller portions, guided by your doctor
- **Muscle gain or athletes:** white rice is fine and easy to digest around training
- **Taste:** if you hate brown rice, do not force it; fix the portion and the plate instead

# Frequently asked questions

**Does brown rice reduce belly fat?**
No single food reduces belly fat. A calorie deficit does. See [how to lose belly fat](/blog/how-to-lose-belly-fat-what-works).

**Is white rice unhealthy?**
No. It is a clean energy source. The problem is large portions with little protein or vegetables.

**Can I eat rice at night?**
Yes. Timing matters less than total calories. See [eating late at night and weight loss](/blog/eating-late-at-night-weight-loss).
