> **Quick answer:** For **beetroot curd raita**, grate **1 small raw beetroot** (about 75 g), squeeze out a little juice if very wet, and stir into **1½ cups thick fresh curd** with **roasted cumin powder, salt, a pinch of pepper and chopped coriander or mint**. Add an optional **mustard and curry leaf tempering**. A **130 g serving** has about **115 kcal and 5.5 g protein**, our kitchen's numbers for **Pink Beetroot Curd**. Use **hung curd** for about double the protein.

Beetroot turns plain curd a bright pink that makes any plate look special. It is cooling, quick, and a good way to add vegetables and protein to a meal. This is a home-style version of the **Pink Beetroot Curd** side in our boxes.

# Ingredients (serves 3)

- 1 small beetroot (about 75 g), peeled
- 1½ cups thick fresh curd (about 375 g)
- ½ tsp roasted cumin powder
- A pinch of black pepper
- Salt to taste
- 1 tbsp chopped coriander or mint
- ½ green chilli, finely chopped (optional)

**Optional tempering (South Indian style)**

- ½ tsp oil
- ¼ tsp mustard seeds
- ¼ tsp urad dal
- A few curry leaves

# Method

## 1. Grate the beetroot

Grate the raw beetroot finely. If it is very juicy, squeeze it gently in your palm to avoid a watery raita.

**Raw or cooked?** Raw beetroot gives crunch and a fresh taste. If you prefer it softer, steam the grated beetroot for 3–4 minutes and cool it.

## 2. Whisk the curd

Whisk the curd until smooth. Add salt, cumin and pepper.

## 3. Mix

Stir in the beetroot, green chilli and herbs. The curd will turn pink.

## 4. Temper (optional)

Heat oil, add mustard seeds and urad dal; when they splutter and turn golden, add curry leaves. Pour over the raita.

## 5. Chill

Serve cold. Best within a few hours.

# Nutrition

For a **130 g serving** (like our kitchen's Pink Beetroot Curd):

- **Calories:** about 115 kcal
- **Protein:** about 5.5 g
- **Carbs:** about 11.5 g
- **Fat:** about 5 g
- **Fibre:** about 1.5 g

**Higher protein version:** use **hung curd** (see [curd vs Greek yoghurt](/blog/curd-vs-greek-yogurt)). This raises it to about **10–12 g protein** per serving.

# Why it is good for your plate

- **Curd:** protein, calcium and probiotics
- **Beetroot:** fibre, folate and natural nitrates
- **Cooling:** perfect with spicy grilled chicken, biryani or pulao
- **Low in calories** compared with creamy dips

# What to serve it with

- Grilled chicken or paneer tikka. See [mint grilled chicken](/blog/mint-grilled-chicken-recipe).
- Soya pulao or vegetable pulao
- Chapati and dal
- As a dip for cucumber and carrot sticks

# Variations

- **Cucumber curd:** grated cucumber, cumin and mint
- **Carrot curd:** grated carrot with a mustard tempering
- **Mint curd:** blended mint, coriander and green chilli in curd

Our boxes rotate **Pink Beetroot Curd, Cool Mint Curd, Crunchy Carrot Curd and Fresh Cucumber Curd** as protein sides.

# Pink urine? Do not panic

Eating beetroot can turn urine or stool pink or red for a day in some people. This is called **beeturia** and is usually harmless. If you see red without eating beetroot, see a doctor.

# Frequently asked questions

**Can I eat curd raita at night?**
For most people, yes. See [is curd good at night](/blog/is-curd-good-at-night).

**Is beetroot raita good for weight loss?**
Yes. It adds protein and fibre for few calories.

**How long does it keep?**
Best the same day. Keep it in the fridge and eat within 12 hours.
