> **Quick answer:** A beginner-friendly **push pull legs (PPL) plan** runs **Push, Pull, Legs, Push, Pull, Legs, Rest**, with **4–5 exercises per day**, **3 sets of 6–12 reps** each, and about **1–2 reps left in the tank** on every set. If you can only train 3 days, do Push, Pull, Legs once each and rest in between. Add a little weight or one rep every week.

Earlier this week we explained [why push pull legs beats a bro split](/blog/push-pull-legs-vs-bro-split) for most people. Here is the actual plan you can take to the gym tomorrow.

# The weekly schedule

**6-day version (best results)**
- Monday: Push
- Tuesday: Pull
- Wednesday: Legs
- Thursday: Push
- Friday: Pull
- Saturday: Legs
- Sunday: Rest

**3-day version (busy weeks or beginners)**
- Monday: Push
- Wednesday: Pull
- Friday: Legs

Start with the 3-day version for the first 4–6 weeks if you are new to lifting. Move to 6 days when the lifts feel familiar and you recover well.

# Push day (chest, shoulders, triceps)

1. **Bench press (barbell or dumbbell):** 3 sets of 6–10
2. **Overhead press (seated dumbbell or standing barbell):** 3 sets of 8–10
3. **Incline dumbbell press:** 3 sets of 8–12
4. **Lateral raises:** 3 sets of 12–15
5. **Triceps pushdown or overhead extension:** 3 sets of 10–12

No bench? Swap with push-ups (feet raised to make it harder), pike push-ups and bench dips.

# Pull day (back, biceps, rear shoulders)

1. **Lat pulldown or pull-ups (assisted if needed):** 3 sets of 6–10
2. **Barbell or dumbbell row:** 3 sets of 8–10
3. **Seated cable row:** 3 sets of 10–12
4. **Face pulls or rear delt flyes:** 3 sets of 12–15
5. **Dumbbell or barbell curls:** 3 sets of 10–12

# Legs day (quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves)

1. **Squats (barbell, goblet or leg press):** 3 sets of 6–10
2. **Romanian deadlift:** 3 sets of 8–10
3. **Walking lunges or Bulgarian split squats:** 3 sets of 10 per leg
4. **Leg curl:** 3 sets of 10–12
5. **Calf raises:** 3 sets of 12–15

# On the second Push/Pull/Legs of the week

To keep things fresh and cover more angles, you can swap the first exercise:

- **Push 2:** start with incline press instead of flat bench
- **Pull 2:** start with rows instead of pulldowns
- **Legs 2:** start with Romanian deadlift or leg press instead of squats

# How heavy should you go?

Pick a weight where you finish the target reps with about **1–2 reps left** in you. Your form should stay clean on the last rep.

- If you hit the **top of the rep range** on all sets (for example 3 × 10 when the range is 6–10), add a small amount of weight next time.
- If you cannot hit the **bottom** of the range, reduce the weight.

This simple rule is progressive overload. Write your weights down in a notebook or phone app.

# Rest between sets

- **Big lifts (bench, squat, rows, deadlifts):** 2–3 minutes
- **Smaller exercises (curls, raises, pushdowns):** 60–90 seconds

Resting too little on heavy lifts makes you weaker on the next set, which slows progress.

# Warm-up (5–10 minutes)

- 5 minutes of light cardio or brisk walking
- Arm circles, leg swings, hip openers
- 1–2 light sets of your first exercise before your working sets

# Progress check: what to expect

- **Weeks 1–4:** learning the movements, strength goes up quickly
- **Weeks 4–12:** steady strength gains, clothes start fitting differently
- **3–6 months:** clear visible changes if your diet supports it

# Diet makes or breaks PPL

Six training days a week needs fuel:

- **Protein:** about 1.6 g per kg of body weight a day. Read [how much protein per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day).
- **Carbs:** rice, chapati, oats, fruit, especially before training. See [what to eat before and after a workout](/blog/what-to-eat-before-and-after-workout).
- **Calories:** a small surplus to build muscle, or a small deficit to lose fat.

# Frequently asked questions

**Can I add abs and cardio to PPL?**
Yes. Add 2–3 sets of planks or hanging knee raises at the end of 2–3 sessions, and walk daily. Keep hard cardio away from legs day.

**How long should I run this plan?**
As long as you are progressing. Many people run PPL for a year or more, only changing exercises occasionally.

**I missed a day. What now?**
Just continue with the next workout in order. Do not try to double up.

**Can women follow this plan?**
Yes. The same plan works for everyone. Adjust the weights to your own strength.
