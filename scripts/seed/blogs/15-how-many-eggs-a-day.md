For years, eggs were treated as a cholesterol bomb. Gym culture then went the other way, with people eating a dozen egg whites a day and throwing away the yolks. Neither extreme makes much sense.

Here is what we know.

# What is in an egg?

A medium to large egg has approximately:

- **Calories:** 70–78 kcal
- **Protein:** about 6 g
- **Fat:** about 5 g
- **Cholesterol:** about 185 mg, all in the yolk

Two boiled eggs in our boxes come to **140 kcal, 12.5 g protein and 9.5 g fat**.

# Should you throw away the yolk?

Usually, no. The white is almost pure protein, and it is very low in calories. The yolk adds fat and most of the egg's calories, but it also contains:

- Vitamin A and vitamin D
- Vitamin B12 and folate
- Choline, which many diets fall short on
- Lutein and zeaxanthin, which are found in the eye

**When egg whites make sense:** If you are eating many eggs a day and need to control calories, keep one or two whole eggs and add extra whites. That gives you high protein and some of the nutrition of the yolk.

# Do eggs raise your cholesterol?

For many people, cholesterol in food has a smaller effect on blood cholesterol than saturated fat, trans fat and overall diet. That said, people respond differently. Some see their LDL ("bad") cholesterol go up more than others when they eat a lot of eggs.

The way you eat your eggs matters too. Boiled eggs with vegetables are very different from an omelette fried in butter and served with processed meat.

# What does the research say?

A large analysis published in **The BMJ in 2020** followed participants from three long-running US studies, then combined the results with other studies. It found that moderate egg consumption, up to about one egg a day, was not associated with a higher risk of cardiovascular disease overall. In Asian populations, the combined analysis even suggested a possibly lower risk.

Three important caveats:

- These studies show associations. They cannot prove cause and effect.
- The authors noted a possible higher risk among people with type 2 diabetes, although results for this group are not settled.
- The research says less about very high intakes, such as 6 or more eggs every day over many years.

# So how many eggs should you eat?

There is no single limit for everyone. Here is a sensible starting point, not medical advice:

- **Healthy adults:** About 1 a day is well supported by research. Many people eat 2 as part of a healthy diet.
- **Active people or those trying to build muscle:** 2–3 whole eggs can fit, particularly if the rest of the diet is low in saturated fat. Check your cholesterol during your regular health tests.
- **People with diabetes, heart disease or high LDL:** Ask your doctor or dietitian for a personal target.
- **People with an egg allergy:** Avoid eggs completely.

If you eat a lot of eggs, a routine lipid profile is the simplest way to see how your body is responding.

# The healthiest ways to eat eggs

- **Boiled:** Easy to carry, with no added fat
- **Poached:** Light and fast
- **Bhurji with vegetables:** Add onion, tomato, capsicum and spinach, and measure the oil
- **Egg curry:** Choose a tomato-onion base over a heavy coconut or cream gravy
- **Omelette:** Use a teaspoon of oil, plus plenty of vegetables

Cook eggs until the whites are firm, particularly for anyone who is pregnant, elderly or has a weakened immune system. Refrigerate boiled eggs if you are not eating them soon.

# Are eggs better than other protein foods?

Eggs are a great source of protein and very convenient, but they are not the only option. They have less protein per serving than chicken, and soya chunks have far more per gram when dry. # Are brown eggs better than white?

Not in any way that matters much. Shell colour depends mainly on the breed of hen. Nutrition is broadly similar, so buy whichever you prefer and can afford. Check for clean, uncracked shells.

# Frequently asked questions

**Is it safe to eat 2 eggs every day?**
For many healthy adults, yes, as part of a balanced diet. If you have diabetes, heart disease or high LDL, talk to your doctor first.

**Do eggs help with weight loss?**
They can. Eggs are filling and high in protein for their calories, which helps you control hunger. They only help if your total calories stay in check.

**Is it OK to eat eggs at night?**
Yes. Timing does not make eggs more or less healthy. Eat them whenever they suit your day.

**Can I replace eggs if I do not eat them?**
Yes. Paneer, tofu, curd, soya chunks and legumes can all fill the gap. Our vegetarian boxes do not include eggs.
