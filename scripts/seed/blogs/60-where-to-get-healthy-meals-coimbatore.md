# What makes a meal "healthy"?

Before you pick a place, know what you are looking for. A healthy main meal usually has:

- **Enough protein:** about 25–50 g per meal (chicken, fish, eggs, paneer, soya, dal)
- **Controlled carbs:** a measured portion of rice, chapati or millets, not an unlimited refill
- **Vegetables or salad:** for fibre and to keep you full
- **Less oil:** grilled, steamed or tawa-cooked instead of deep fried
- **Known calories:** so you can actually plan your day

Most "healthy" labels fail on the last point. If nobody can tell you the calories, you are guessing. Our guide to [calories in South Indian food](/blog/calories-in-south-indian-food) shows how quickly a normal meals plate adds up.

# Option 2: Salad and bowl kitchens

Coimbatore has a growing number of cloud kitchens and cafes selling salads, grain bowls and "diet" lunches. They are good for a **light lunch** and for people who like raw vegetables.

**Watch out for:**

- **Low protein.** A big bowl of vegetables and dressing can have under 15 g of protein.
- **Hidden calories** in dressings, mayo, cheese and fried toppings.
- **Hunger by 4 PM,** which often ends in a snack you did not plan.

Ask for the protein and calorie count. If they cannot tell you, treat it as a side dish, not a full meal.

# Option 3: Smart picks at a mess or tiffin centre

You do not have to give up local food to eat healthy. At a regular meals hotel or tiffin centre:

- **Take one serving of rice** and skip the refill. Ask for extra sambar, rasam or kootu instead.
- **Add a protein:** egg, fish curry, chicken (not fried), or extra dal.
- **At breakfast,** idli or pesarattu with sambar beats poori or vada.
- **Skip the appalam and fried sides** if you are cutting calories.

This works, but it is hard to stay consistent because the portions change every day. Read [is rice bad for weight loss](/blog/is-rice-bad-for-weight-loss) to see how much rice actually fits your goal.
