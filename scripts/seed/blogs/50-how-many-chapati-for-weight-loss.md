> **Quick answer:** For most adults trying to lose weight, **2 chapatis per meal** (about 4 a day across lunch and dinner) is a good starting point, as long as the plate also has protein and vegetables. One plain chapati (about 30–35 g atta, 6-inch) has roughly **100–120 kcal** and **3 g of protein**. Add ghee and it goes up to about 140–160 kcal. Very active people or men with higher calorie needs can often eat 3 per meal.

Chapati is often seen as the "healthy" alternative to rice, so many people switch to chapati and then eat 5–6 at dinner. How many should you really eat to lose weight?

# Calories in one chapati

For a medium chapati (about 6 inches, made from about 30–35 g of whole wheat atta):

- **Plain, no oil:** about 100–120 kcal
- **With half a teaspoon of ghee or oil:** about 120–140 kcal
- **With a teaspoon of ghee on top:** about 140–160 kcal
- **Protein:** about 3 g
- **Fibre:** about 3–4 g
- **Carbs:** about 20–22 g

A thin phulka has fewer calories (about 70–80 kcal). A thick, large homemade chapati can have 150+ kcal before ghee.

# Chapati vs other breads

- **Phulka (thin, no oil):** about 70–80 kcal
- **Chapati (medium):** about 100–120 kcal
- **Parotta (Kerala/Tamil style):** about 250–300 kcal, made from maida with a lot of oil
- **Naan:** about 250–300 kcal
- **Ragi roti:** about 100–120 kcal, with more fibre and calcium

# How many chapatis a day for weight loss?

It depends on your calorie target and what else is on your plate. As a rough guide:

**For a woman eating about 1,400–1,600 kcal a day**
- 1–2 chapatis at lunch and 1–2 at dinner
- Total: about 3–4 a day

**For a man eating about 1,800–2,000 kcal a day**
- 2–3 chapatis at lunch and 2 at dinner
- Total: about 4–5 a day

**If you also eat rice in the same meal**
- Reduce to 1 chapati, or choose either rice or chapati, not both

These are starting points. If your weight is not dropping after 2–3 weeks, reduce by one chapati a day. Our [calorie deficit guide](/blog/calorie-deficit-how-to-calculate) shows how to find your own target.

# Is chapati better than rice for weight loss?

Not by much. Calorie for calorie, they are similar:

- **2 medium chapatis:** about 200–240 kcal
- **1 cup cooked rice (about 150 g):** about 190–200 kcal

Chapati has slightly more fibre and protein, which can help you feel full. But what really matters is **how much** you eat. Read [is rice bad for weight loss](/blog/is-rice-bad-for-weight-loss) for a full comparison.

# The plate matters more than the chapati count

Four chapatis with a big bowl of potato curry and pickle will not help weight loss. Two chapatis with a good protein source and vegetables will.

A good weight-loss plate:

- **Half the plate:** vegetables (poriyal, salad, kootu)
- **A quarter:** protein (chicken, fish, eggs, paneer, dal, soya)
- **A quarter:** chapati or rice

# Tips to make chapati weight-loss friendly

1. **Skip the ghee on top**, or use just a few drops.
2. **Make them thinner and smaller.**
3. **Add besan or ragi** to the atta for more protein and fibre.
4. **Eat protein first**, then chapati. You will often stop sooner.
5. **Avoid stuffed parathas** with butter for daily meals.
6. **Count your chapatis** before you start eating, instead of "one more" at a time.

# Should you stop eating chapati at night?

No. Eating carbs at night does not make you gain fat by itself. Total calories through the day matter more. If you feel lighter with a smaller dinner, have 1–2 chapatis with more protein and vegetables. See [is eating late at night bad for weight loss](/blog/eating-late-at-night-weight-loss).

# Frequently asked questions

**Is 2 chapatis a day enough?**
For some people on a lower calorie diet, yes, if the rest of their food gives enough energy and protein. Very low carb intake can make training harder.

**Are multigrain chapatis better?**
They may have slightly more fibre and protein. The calorie difference is small.

**Is chapati with ghee bad for weight loss?**
A little ghee is fine. A teaspoon on every chapati adds up quickly: 4 chapatis could carry 160+ extra calories.

**Can diabetics eat chapati?**
Yes, in measured portions with protein and vegetables. Follow your doctor's guidance on carbs.
