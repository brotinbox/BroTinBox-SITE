> **Quick answer:** For **pepper pongal oats**, dry roast **¼ cup moong dal**, pressure cook it with **½ cup rolled oats** and about **2 cups water** (or cook oats separately after the dal is soft), then mash lightly and temper **1 tsp ghee or oil** with **crushed black pepper, cumin, ginger, curry leaves and a few cashews (optional)**. A **130 g bowl** has about **200 kcal, 7.5 g protein and 6 g fibre**, our kitchen's numbers. It tastes like ven pongal with **more fibre and less rice**.

Ven pongal is one of the most comforting South Indian breakfasts, but restaurant pongal can be heavy with ghee and rice. This oats version keeps the pepper-cumin flavour and the soft texture, with more fibre. It is a home-style take on the **Pepper Pongal Oats** in our boxes.

# Ingredients (serves 2)

- ½ cup rolled oats
- ¼ cup split yellow moong dal
- 2–2½ cups water
- ½ tsp salt (adjust)
- A pinch of turmeric (optional)

**Tempering**

- 1 tsp ghee (or oil)
- 1 tsp whole black pepper, coarsely crushed
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 1 tsp grated ginger
- A sprig of curry leaves
- A pinch of hing
- 5–6 cashews (optional; adds about 50 kcal)

# Method

## 1. Roast the dal

Dry roast the moong dal in a pan on low heat for **2–3 minutes** until fragrant and lightly golden. This gives pongal its nutty flavour.

## 2. Cook the dal

Wash the roasted dal and pressure cook with **1½ cups water**, salt and turmeric for **2–3 whistles** until soft.

## 3. Add the oats

- Open the cooker, add the oats and **¾–1 cup hot water**.
- Simmer on low heat for **4–5 minutes**, stirring, until the oats are soft and the mixture is thick but creamy.
- Mash lightly with the back of a ladle.

**Tip:** pongal thickens as it cools. Keep it slightly loose.

## 4. Temper

Heat ghee in a small pan. Add cumin and crushed pepper; let them sizzle for a few seconds. Add ginger, curry leaves, hing and cashews (if using). Fry until the cashews are light golden.

## 5. Combine

Pour the tempering over the pongal and mix well. Taste and adjust salt.

# Nutrition

For a **130 g bowl** (like our kitchen's Pepper Pongal Oats):

- **Calories:** about 200 kcal
- **Protein:** about 7.5 g
- **Carbs:** about 33 g
- **Fibre:** about 6 g
- **Fat:** about 4 g

Restaurant ven pongal with rice and extra ghee is often **280–350 kcal per cup**. See [calories in South Indian food](/blog/calories-in-south-indian-food).

# Make it a high-protein meal

- **+ 2 boiled eggs:** +12 g protein
- **+ a cup of sambar:** +4–5 g
- **+ a cup of curd:** +5–6 g
- **+ 50 g paneer cubes** stirred in: +9–10 g

**Example:** pepper pongal oats + sambar + 2 boiled eggs = about 380 kcal and 24 g protein.

# Why this works for diets

- **Oats + moong dal** give more fibre and protein than rice pongal
- **Pepper and ginger** give big flavour with little fat
- **Soft and comforting,** great for breakfast or a light dinner

Compare oats with idli in [oats vs idli](/blog/oats-vs-idli).

# Tips

- **Use rolled oats,** not instant, for better texture
- **Crush the pepper fresh** for the best aroma
- **Do not skip roasting the dal**
- **Make it ahead:** keeps 1 day in the fridge; add hot water when reheating

# Frequently asked questions

**Is oats pongal good for weight loss?**
Yes. It is filling and lower in calories than rice pongal. Add protein on the side.

**Can diabetics eat oats pongal?**
It is usually a better choice than rice pongal; keep the portion moderate and follow your doctor's advice.

**Is it gluten-free?**
Not reliably, since oats are often processed with wheat. Use certified gluten-free oats if needed.
