# What counts as a "high-protein meal"?

A good benchmark: **30–50 g of protein per main meal**. That is the amount most adults need at lunch and dinner to hit a daily target of about 1.6 g per kg.

For context:

- A typical restaurant "meals" plate: about 15–20 g protein
- A chicken biryani plate: about 25–35 g protein, but often 700–1,000 kcal
- 150 g of grilled chicken with rice and dal: about 50–55 g protein

# Option 2: Eat out smart

If you eat out, choose dishes where the protein is visible and the oil is not:

- **Tandoori or grilled chicken** instead of chicken 65 or fried chicken
- **Fish fry (tawa, not deep fried) or fish curry** with less rice
- **Egg dishes:** omelette, boiled eggs, egg curry
- **Paneer tikka** instead of paneer butter masala
- **Extra dal or sambar**, less rice

We wrote a full guide on [eating out in Coimbatore without wrecking your diet](/blog/eating-out-high-protein-coimbatore), with what to order at different kinds of restaurants.

# Option 3: Cook at home

Cooking is the cheapest way to eat high protein. Easy, local high-protein foods:

- **Chicken breast** (about 31 g protein per 100 g cooked)
- **Eggs** (about 6 g each)
- **Mackerel (kaanaangeluthi)** and other local fish
- **Paneer and curd**
- **Soya chunks, chana, rajma, green gram, horse gram (kollu)**

The challenge is time. Weighing, planning and cooking every day is what most people give up on. Read [meal prep vs meal subscription](/blog/meal-prep-vs-meal-subscription-cost) to compare the real cost of each.

# A high-protein day in Coimbatore

For a 70 kg person aiming for about 110 g of protein:

- **Breakfast at home:** 3 eggs and 2 idlis, about 22 g
- **Lunch (BroTin Box or home):** a measured chicken or paneer meal, about 45–60 g
- **Evening:** sundal or a bowl of curd and peanuts, about 10 g
- **Dinner:** fish or paneer with chapati, about 25–30 g

That gets you to target without protein powder.
