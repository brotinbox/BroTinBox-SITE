> **Quick answer:** A good beginner gym diet chart has **protein in every meal** (about 1.6 g per kg of body weight a day), **carbs around your workout**, plenty of vegetables, and calories set to your goal: about **300–500 kcal below maintenance** to lose fat, or **200–300 kcal above** to build muscle. Below are simple Indian veg and non-veg charts for a 70 kg beginner.

Just joined a gym? Your trainer probably told you, "Diet is 70%." Then nobody told you what to actually eat.

This guide gives you a simple Indian gym diet chart you can follow from tomorrow, with both vegetarian and non-vegetarian versions.

# Step 1: Know your targets

Before the chart, three numbers:

- **Protein:** about **1.6 g per kg** of body weight. For 70 kg, that is about 110 g a day.
- **Calories:** start with your weight in kg × 30 as a rough maintenance number. For 70 kg, about 2,100 kcal. Eat about 1,700–1,800 kcal for fat loss, or about 2,300–2,400 kcal for muscle gain.
- **Water:** about 2.5–3.5 litres a day, more on hot days and training days.

These are starting points. Weigh yourself weekly and adjust by 100–200 kcal if you are not moving in the right direction. Our [calorie deficit guide](/blog/calorie-deficit-how-to-calculate) explains this in more detail.

# Non-veg gym diet chart (about 2,100 kcal, 115 g protein)

**Morning (6:30–7:00 AM)**
- Black coffee or tea with less sugar
- 1 banana if you train early

**Breakfast (8:00 AM)**
- 3 boiled eggs or an omelette with onion and tomato
- 2 idlis or 1 dosa with sambar
- Protein: about 25 g

**Lunch (1:00 PM)**
- 150 g grilled or curry chicken (less oil)
- 1 cup rice or 2 chapatis
- Dal or sambar, and a big portion of vegetables or salad
- Protein: about 50 g

**Pre-workout snack (4:30–5:00 PM)**
- 1 banana and a handful of peanuts, or 2 slices of bread with peanut butter
- Protein: about 7 g

**Post-workout / dinner (8:00 PM)**
- 100–150 g fish, chicken or 2–3 eggs
- 2 chapatis or a small bowl of rice
- Vegetable poriyal and a bowl of curd
- Protein: about 35 g

# Veg gym diet chart (about 2,100 kcal, 100–110 g protein)

**Breakfast**
- Besan chilla or 3 idlis with sambar
- 1 glass of milk
- Protein: about 20 g

**Mid-morning**
- Sprouted green gram or chana salad (1 cup)
- Protein: about 10 g

**Lunch**
- 100 g paneer or soya chunks curry
- 1 cup rice or 2 chapatis, a bowl of dal
- Salad and a bowl of curd
- Protein: about 40 g

**Pre-workout**
- Banana and peanut butter toast
- Protein: about 8 g

**Dinner**
- Soya chunks or rajma, or tofu bhurji
- 2 chapatis, vegetables
- Protein: about 25 g

Hitting 110 g on a vegetarian diet is possible, but it takes planning. Paneer, soya chunks, curd, milk, dal and chana do most of the work. If you struggle, a scoop of whey is a convenient top-up; read [protein powder vs real food](/blog/protein-powder-vs-real-food-india) first.

# Fat loss vs muscle gain: what changes?

The meal structure stays the same. Only the portions change:

**For fat loss**
- Keep protein the same
- Reduce rice, chapati and oil
- Add more vegetables to stay full
- Walk 7,000–10,000 steps a day

**For muscle gain**
- Keep protein the same
- Add one more chapati or half a cup more rice at lunch and dinner
- Add a snack like milk with peanuts, or curd with fruit

# What to eat before and after the gym

- **Before (60–90 minutes):** something light with carbs: banana, idli, poha, toast.
- **After (within a couple of hours):** a real meal with protein and carbs.

The "30-minute anabolic window" is not as strict as gym talk suggests. What matters most is your total protein for the day. Our [pre and post-workout guide](/blog/what-to-eat-before-and-after-workout) covers timing in detail.

# Beginner diet mistakes

1. **Only eating chicken and boiled vegetables.** It burns you out in two weeks. Normal Indian food works fine with the right portions.
2. **Skipping carbs before workouts.** You will feel weak. Carbs are fuel.
3. **Buying supplements before fixing food.** Food first, supplements later.
4. **Drinking your calories.** Juice, sweet tea, shakes from outside and soft drinks add up fast.
5. **Changing everything at once.** Fix protein first, then portions, then timing.

# Frequently asked questions

**Is rice okay on a gym diet?**
Yes. Rice is a great carb for training. Control the portion. See [is rice bad for weight loss](/blog/is-rice-bad-for-weight-loss).

**How many eggs should I eat a day?**
Most healthy people can eat 2–3 whole eggs a day comfortably. Read [how many eggs a day is safe](/blog/how-many-eggs-a-day-is-safe).

**Do I need protein powder as a beginner?**
No. It is convenient, not necessary. Real food can easily get you to your target.
