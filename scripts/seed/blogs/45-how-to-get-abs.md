> **Quick answer:** **Abs are made in the kitchen and revealed by fat loss.** Everyone already has abdominal muscles. They become visible when body fat drops to roughly **10–14% for men** and **16–20% for women** (it varies by person). Ab exercises make the muscles stronger and slightly thicker, but they **do not burn belly fat**. The formula is: a calorie deficit, high protein, strength training, daily walking, and 2–3 short ab sessions a week.

"How to get abs" is one of the most searched fitness questions in the world. And it gets the most wrong answers: 1,000 crunches a day, belly fat burner drinks, sauna belts. Here is what actually works.

# Why you cannot see your abs yet

You already have a six-pack. The rectus abdominis (your "six-pack muscle") is there in everyone. It is just covered by a layer of fat.

Rough body fat ranges for visible abs:

- **Men:** outlines start to show around 12–15%, clearly visible around 10–12%
- **Women:** outlines start to show around 18–22%, clearly visible around 16–19%

Genetics decide where you store fat first and lose it last. Many men store fat around the lower belly; many women around hips and thighs. That is why lower abs are usually the last to show.

# Step 1: Lose the fat (diet does most of the work)

You cannot "spot reduce" belly fat. Crunches do not burn the fat sitting on top of your abs. Your body decides where fat comes off.

What does work:

- **A calorie deficit** of about **300–500 kcal a day**. See our [calorie deficit guide](/blog/calorie-deficit-how-to-calculate).
- **High protein**, about **1.6–2.2 g per kg** of body weight, to keep muscle and stay full.
- **Less liquid sugar and fried food.** These are the easiest calories to cut.
- **Patience.** At 0.5–1% of body weight a week, getting from 20% to 12% body fat can take 4–6 months.

Our guide on [how to lose belly fat](/blog/how-to-lose-belly-fat-what-works) explains what works and the myths to ignore.

# Step 2: Train your whole body

Lifting weights helps in two ways:

- It **keeps your muscle** while you are in a deficit, so you lose fat, not muscle.
- Heavy compound lifts like **squats, deadlifts, rows and overhead presses** train your core hard as a stabiliser.

Aim for 3–5 strength sessions a week. A [push pull legs plan](/blog/push-pull-legs-workout-plan) works well.

# Step 3: Train your abs directly (2–3 times a week)

Ab training makes the muscles stronger and a little thicker, so they "pop" more once you are lean. You do not need to do it daily.

A simple ab routine (10–15 minutes):

1. **Hanging knee raises or lying leg raises:** 3 sets of 10–15
2. **Cable crunches or weighted crunches:** 3 sets of 10–15
3. **Plank:** 3 sets of 30–60 seconds
4. **Side plank or Russian twists:** 2–3 sets per side

Treat abs like any other muscle: add reps, time or weight over weeks.

# Step 4: Walk every day

Daily walking adds steady calorie burn without making you hungry. **7,000–10,000 steps a day** is a great target. See [walking for weight loss](/blog/walking-for-weight-loss-steps).

# Step 5: Sleep and manage stress

Poor sleep increases hunger and cravings, and makes it harder to stick to your diet. Aim for 7–8 hours.

# A sample "abs" day of eating (about 1,800 kcal, 130 g protein)

- **Breakfast:** 3 eggs plus 2 egg whites, 1 dosa, black coffee
- **Lunch:** 150 g grilled chicken, half a cup of rice, dal, a big salad
- **Snack:** curd with a fruit, a handful of roasted chana
- **Dinner:** fish or paneer, 2 chapatis, vegetables

Adjust portions to your own size and goal.

# Myths about getting abs

- **"Do 100 crunches a day."** Builds the muscle a little, does nothing for the fat on top.
- **"Fat-burner drinks melt belly fat."** No drink targets belly fat.
- **"Never eat rice."** Rice in a measured portion is fine. See [is rice bad for weight loss](/blog/is-rice-bad-for-weight-loss).
- **"Sweat belts burn fat."** You lose water, not fat.
- **"Women should do only light ab work."** Women benefit from strength training just like men.

# How long does it take to get abs?

It depends on where you start:

- **Already lean (about 15% men / 22% women):** about 6–12 weeks
- **Average (about 20–25% men / 28–32% women):** about 4–8 months
- **Higher body fat:** a year or more, and that is perfectly normal

Track your waist measurement and take photos every 2 weeks. The scale alone can be misleading.

# Frequently asked questions

**Can I get abs in 30 days?**
Only if you are already quite lean. For most people, it takes several months.

**Do I need a gym to get abs?**
No. Diet, walking and bodyweight training can get you there. A gym makes progressive overload easier.

**Why can I see my upper abs but not my lower abs?**
The lower belly is usually the last place men lose fat. Keep going with the deficit.

**Are abs genetic?**
The shape and symmetry of your abs are genetic. Whether you can see them depends on body fat.
