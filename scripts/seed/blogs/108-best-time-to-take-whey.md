> **Quick answer:** **The best time to take whey is whenever it helps you reach your daily protein target.** Total protein per day matters far more than timing. That said, **within a few hours after training** is a convenient and sensible time, and **in the morning** works well if your breakfast is low in protein. Aim for about **20–40 g of protein per serving** (usually 1 scoop), and spread your protein across 3–5 meals.

Fitness influencers argue about the "anabolic window", morning shakes and bedtime protein. Here is what the research actually supports.

# What matters most: total daily protein

For muscle gain, studies consistently show that **total protein per day** is the biggest factor. Aim for:

- **1.6–2.2 g per kg** of body weight if you train for muscle
- **1.2–1.6 g per kg** if you are losing weight

Whey is just a convenient way to fill the gap. See [how much protein per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day).

# The "anabolic window"

The old idea: you must drink your shake **within 30 minutes** of training or you lose your gains.

The newer view: the window is **much wider**, several hours around your workout. If you ate a protein meal 1–3 hours before training, there is no rush afterwards.

**Bottom line:** having protein within a couple of hours after training is good practice, but you do not need to sprint to the shaker.

# Good times to take whey

## After training

- Convenient: many people are hungry and have their shaker with them
- Helps start recovery
- Pair with a banana or a meal if it has been a while since you ate

## In the morning

- Many Indian breakfasts (idli, dosa, upma, poha) are low in protein
- A scoop with milk or alongside breakfast adds 20–25 g

## Between meals

- If your meals are 5–6 hours apart, a shake fills the gap
- Helps spread protein through the day

## Before bed

- A slow-digesting protein (casein, or milk, curd or paneer) is often used before bed
- Whey works too, but digests faster

# How much per serving?

- About **20–40 g of protein** per serving
- Roughly **0.3–0.4 g per kg** of body weight per meal
- For most people, that is **1 scoop** (check your label)

Larger servings are fine; the extra protein is still used, just not all for muscle.

# Whey on rest days?

**Yes,** if you need it to hit your daily protein. Muscles recover and grow on rest days too.

# What to mix it with

- **Water:** fewer calories, good for weight loss
- **Milk:** more protein and calories, good for gaining
- **With oats or banana:** a quick meal replacement
- **With creatine:** fine to mix together. See [creatine vs whey](/blog/creatine-vs-whey).

# Do you even need whey?

Not if you get enough protein from food. Whey is helpful if:

- You are vegetarian and struggle to reach your target
- You are busy and skip meals
- You need a quick option after training

Real food works just as well: eggs, chicken, fish, paneer, curd, dal and soya. See [protein powder vs real food](/blog/protein-powder-vs-real-food-india).

# Buying tips in India

- Buy from **authorised sellers**; fake whey is common
- Check the **FSSAI licence** and batch number
- Look for **third-party lab testing**
- Avoid "amino spiking" brands where the protein per scoop is lower than claimed
- Lactose sensitive? Try **whey isolate**

# Frequently asked questions

**Should I take whey before or after a workout?**
Either works. After is more common and convenient.

**Can I take whey on an empty stomach?**
Yes, if it suits your stomach.

**Is 2 scoops a day okay?**
Yes, if you need it to reach your protein target and you are healthy.
