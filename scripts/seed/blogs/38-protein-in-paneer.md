Paneer is the most popular protein food for vegetarians in India. But is it really a "high protein" food, or a high-fat food with some protein? The answer is a bit of both.

# Paneer nutrition per 100 g

Typical full-fat paneer (made from whole buffalo or cow milk):

- **Protein:** about 18–20 g
- **Fat:** about 20–25 g
- **Carbs:** about 1–4 g
- **Calories:** about 260–320 kcal
- **Calcium:** high, around 200–480 mg depending on the source

Low-fat or "lite" paneer (made from toned or skimmed milk):

- **Protein:** about 20–25 g
- **Fat:** about 5–12 g
- **Calories:** about 150–220 kcal

Packaged brands print these numbers on the pack. Homemade and loose paneer from the shop can vary widely, so treat the numbers as estimates.

# Is paneer a good protein source?

**Yes, with a catch.** The protein is high quality: it is mostly casein, a slow-digesting milk protein that keeps you full for longer and supports muscle repair.

The catch is that full-fat paneer has **more calories from fat than from protein**. For every 1 g of protein, you get more than 1 g of fat. Compare that with chicken breast, which has about 31 g of protein and just 3–4 g of fat per 100 g cooked.

This does not make paneer bad. It just means **portion size matters** if you are trying to lose weight.

# How much protein in common paneer servings?

- **50 g paneer (a few cubes):** about 9–10 g protein, 130–160 kcal
- **100 g paneer:** about 18–20 g protein, 260–320 kcal
- **150 g paneer:** about 27–30 g protein, 400–480 kcal
- **Paneer butter masala, one restaurant bowl:** about 15–20 g protein, often 400–600 kcal
- **Paneer tikka, 6 pieces (about 120 g):** about 20–24 g protein, about 300–380 kcal
- **Paneer bhurji, one bowl (100 g paneer):** about 20 g protein, about 330–400 kcal with oil

Gravies with butter and cream double the calories without adding much protein.

# Paneer for weight loss

You can eat paneer while losing weight. Some tips:

- **Stick to 75–100 g per meal.**
- **Choose low-fat paneer** if your goal is fat loss.
- **Grill or sauté** with minimal oil instead of deep-frying.
- **Pair it with vegetables**, not with naan and butter gravy.
- **Count it as your protein and some of your fat** for that meal.

# Paneer for muscle gain

Paneer is great for vegetarians building muscle because it is calorie-dense and protein-rich. 150 g of paneer plus dal, curd and milk can make a vegetarian high-protein day much easier.

Casein's slow digestion is one reason some lifters like a paneer or curd-based meal before bed.

# Paneer vs tofu vs soya chunks

Per 100 g:

- **Paneer:** about 18–20 g protein, 260–320 kcal
- **Tofu (firm):** about 12–17 g protein, about 120–150 kcal
- **Soya chunks (dry):** about 52 g protein, about 345 kcal
- **Soya chunks (cooked, from about 35 g dry):** about 17–18 g protein, about 120 kcal

So if you want the most protein for the fewest calories as a vegetarian, **soya chunks win**, then tofu, then paneer. Paneer wins on taste and how easily it fits into Indian cooking. # Frequently asked questions

**How much paneer should I eat a day?**
75–150 g a day fits most diets. Account for the calories if you are losing weight.

**Is homemade paneer healthier?**
It depends on the milk. Paneer from toned milk has less fat than paneer from full-cream milk.

**Is paneer good at night?**
Yes, for most people. Its slow-digesting protein can be a good part of dinner.

**Does paneer increase cholesterol?**
Full-fat paneer is high in saturated fat. If you have high cholesterol, choose low-fat paneer and keep portions moderate. Follow your doctor's advice.
