> **Quick answer:** **For most healthy people, eating curd at night is fine.** There is no good scientific evidence that curd at night causes harm. Curd gives protein, calcium and probiotics. Traditional advice, including Ayurveda, suggests avoiding it at night, and some people do notice **more mucus, a sore throat or acidity** after curd at dinner. If that happens to you, have it at lunch instead, or switch to buttermilk.

"Do not eat curd at night" is one of the most common pieces of food advice in Indian homes. But is it a real health rule or a tradition? Here is what we know.

# Curd nutrition (per 100 g)

Plain curd made from toned milk:

- **Protein:** about 3–4 g
- **Calories:** about 60–70 kcal
- **Calcium:** about 120–150 mg
- **Fat:** about 3–4 g
- **Live cultures (probiotics):** if fresh and not heated

**Thick hung curd or Greek-style yoghurt** has much more protein, about **8–10 g per 100 g**, because the whey is drained off.

A normal bowl (about 150 g) of regular curd gives about **5–6 g of protein**.

# Why do people say "no curd at night"?

The idea mainly comes from **Ayurveda**, which describes curd as heavy and mucus-forming, and says it should not be eaten at night.

There is limited scientific research specifically on "curd at night". What we do know:

- Dairy does not increase mucus production in most people, according to the studies done on milk.
- Some people feel their throat or nose is "thicker" after dairy. This is personal and real for them, even if it is not universal.
- People with acid reflux may notice more discomfort from any food eaten close to bedtime, including curd.

# Benefits of curd at night

## 1. Protein before sleep
Curd contains casein, a slow-digesting protein. A bowl at dinner adds to your daily protein. Hung curd is even better.

## 2. Good for digestion
The live cultures in fresh curd support gut health. Curd rice is a classic, light end to a South Indian meal for good reason.

## 3. Calcium
Curd is an easy way to add calcium to your diet.

## 4. Helps you feel full
A bowl of curd with dinner can make a lighter dinner more satisfying.

# When to avoid curd at night

You may want to skip it at night if:

- You notice a **cold, sore throat or blocked nose** after eating it
- You have **acid reflux** that is worse at night
- You are **lactose intolerant** (though curd is easier to digest than milk for many people)
- Your doctor has given you specific dietary advice

# Better options if curd at night bothers you

- **Buttermilk (moru)** with ginger, curry leaves and a pinch of salt: lighter and easier
- **Curd at lunch** instead of dinner
- **Room-temperature curd** instead of cold curd from the fridge
- **Add pepper, ginger or jeera** to curd, which many people find easier to digest

# Curd for weight loss

Curd can help weight loss:

- It is **low in calories** and adds protein
- It makes a meal more filling
- Plain curd is much better than **flavoured yoghurt**, which can contain 10–15 g of added sugar per cup

Watch the add-ons: curd rice with a lot of rice, or sweetened lassi, can be high in calories. Our guide to [eating late at night](/blog/eating-late-at-night-weight-loss) explains why the total amount matters more than the clock.

# Simple high-protein curd ideas

- **Hung curd dip** with cucumber and mint, eaten with grilled chicken or paneer
- **Curd with sprouts** and chopped onion, tomato and coriander
- **Curd and fruit bowl** with a few nuts (no sugar)
- **Mint curd** as a side with chapati, a regular side in our boxes

# Frequently asked questions

**Is curd rice good for dinner?**
Yes, if the portion is controlled. Use more curd and less rice for more protein and fewer calories.

**Does curd cause cold?**
Some people feel it does. Science has not shown dairy causes colds, which are caused by viruses. If you feel worse after curd, avoid it at night.

**Is curd better than milk at night?**
Curd is usually easier to digest for people with mild lactose intolerance. Both give protein and calcium.

**Can I eat curd with chicken or fish?**
Yes. Many Indian dishes use curd marinades and raita with meat and fish. There is no scientific reason to avoid this combination.
