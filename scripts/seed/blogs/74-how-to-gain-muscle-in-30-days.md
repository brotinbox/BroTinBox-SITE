> **Quick answer:** In 30 days, a beginner can realistically gain about **0.5–1 kg of muscle**, and an experienced lifter about **0.25–0.5 kg**. To get there: **train each muscle twice a week** with progressive overload, eat **1.6–2.2 g of protein per kg** of body weight, stay in a **small calorie surplus** (200–400 kcal), and sleep **7–8 hours**. Your **strength** will go up faster than your size, and that is the first sign it is working.

You can build visible muscle in 30 days if you are new to training, but probably less than social media suggests. Here is what is possible, and how to get the most out of one month.

# How much muscle can you gain in a month?

Rough guide for men (women typically gain about half these amounts):

- **First year of training:** about 0.5–1 kg a month
- **Second year:** about 0.25–0.5 kg a month
- **Third year and beyond:** about 0.1–0.25 kg a month

Your scale may go up more than this because of water, glycogen and some fat. That is normal.

# The 4 things that build muscle

## 1. Progressive overload

Your muscles grow when you ask them to do more over time:

- Add **weight**, or
- Add **reps** with the same weight, or
- Add **sets**

Write every workout down. If your numbers go up each week, you are on track.

## 2. Enough volume

Aim for about **10–15 hard sets per muscle group per week**, split over 2 sessions.

## 3. Enough protein

**1.6–2.2 g per kg** of body weight. For 65 kg, that is about 105–140 g a day, split into 3–5 meals of 25–40 g.

See [how much protein per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day).

## 4. Enough food and sleep

- **Small surplus:** 200–400 kcal above maintenance
- **Sleep:** 7–8 hours; muscle is built while you recover

If you have a lot of belly fat, you can build muscle at maintenance calories, especially as a beginner. Read [bulking vs cutting](/blog/bulking-vs-cutting).

# A simple 30-day training plan

Train **4 days a week**, upper/lower split.

**Day 1: Upper**

- Bench press: 3 × 6–10
- Bent-over row: 3 × 8–10
- Overhead press: 3 × 8–10
- Lat pulldown: 3 × 10–12
- Bicep curl: 2 × 10–12
- Tricep pushdown: 2 × 10–12

**Day 2: Lower**

- Squat: 3 × 6–10
- Romanian deadlift: 3 × 8–10
- Leg press or lunges: 3 × 10–12
- Leg curl: 2 × 10–12
- Calf raise: 3 × 12–15

**Day 3: Rest**

**Day 4: Upper** (same as day 1, try to add reps or weight)

**Day 5: Lower** (same as day 2, try to add reps or weight)

**Days 6–7: Rest or walk**

Stop each set **1–2 reps before failure** with good form. New to the gym? Start with our [gym workout plan for beginners](/blog/gym-workout-plan-for-beginners).

# What to eat in your 30 days

- **Breakfast:** 3 eggs, 2 chapatis, a glass of milk
- **Lunch:** 150 g chicken or paneer, 1 cup rice, dal, vegetables, curd
- **Evening:** banana and a cup of sundal

# Week-by-week: what to expect

- **Week 1:** soreness, learning form. Strength jumps quickly as your nervous system adapts.
- **Week 2:** less soreness, steady strength gains.
- **Week 3:** muscles feel fuller; clothes may fit tighter on the arms and shoulders.
- **Week 4:** clear strength gains; small but visible changes in photos.

# Supplements that can help (optional)

- **Creatine monohydrate:** 3–5 g a day. One of the few proven ones. See [is creatine good for health](/blog/is-creatine-good-for-health).
- **Whey protein:** convenient if you cannot hit your protein from food. Not required.

Avoid "mass gainers" full of sugar and anything promising steroid-like results.

# Common mistakes

- **Doing only arms and chest.** Train your whole body, especially legs and back.
- **Switching programmes every week.**
- **Training to failure on every set,** then being too sore to train again.
- **Eating too little protein.**
- **Expecting a new body in 30 days.** It is the start of the change.

# Frequently asked questions

**Can I gain muscle without the gym?**
Yes, with push-ups, pull-ups, squats, lunges and resistance bands, especially as a beginner. A gym makes progressive overload easier.

**Can I gain muscle while losing fat?**
Yes, beginners and people returning to training often can, with high protein and strength training.

**Should I do cardio while building muscle?**
Yes, 2–3 short sessions or daily walking is good for your heart and will not stop muscle gain.
