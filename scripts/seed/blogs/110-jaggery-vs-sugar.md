> **Quick answer:** **Jaggery and sugar have almost the same calories** (about **380–390 kcal per 100 g**, or about **15–20 kcal per teaspoon**) and both raise blood sugar. Jaggery keeps tiny amounts of **iron, potassium and other minerals**, but at normal teaspoon amounts these are too small to matter. For **weight loss and diabetes, jaggery is not a "healthy sugar"**; the amount you use matters more than which one you choose.

Many Indian families switch from white sugar to jaggery (vellam or karupatti) in tea, sweets and payasam, believing it is healthier. Here is how they actually compare.

# What they are

- **White sugar:** made from sugarcane juice that is refined to remove everything except sucrose.
- **Jaggery (vellam):** sugarcane juice boiled down without full refining, so it keeps some molasses and minerals.
- **Palm jaggery (karupatti):** made from palm sap, popular in Tamil Nadu.

# Nutrition compared (per 100 g)

**White sugar**

- Calories: about 390 kcal
- Sugar: about 100 g
- Minerals: essentially none

**Jaggery**

- Calories: about 380 kcal
- Sugar: about 80–90 g (mostly sucrose)
- Minerals: small amounts of iron, potassium, magnesium and calcium (varies a lot by batch)

# The teaspoon reality

You eat sugar or jaggery by the **teaspoon**, not 100 g at a time.

**1 tsp (about 5 g) of jaggery gives:**

- About **19 kcal**
- A very small amount of iron and potassium, far less than a serving of dal, greens or a banana

So switching from sugar to jaggery in your tea does **not** meaningfully improve your iron or mineral intake.

# Blood sugar and diabetes

Both are mainly sucrose and both **raise blood sugar**. Jaggery is **not safe for people with diabetes** in larger amounts just because it is "natural".

If you have diabetes, treat jaggery, palm jaggery, honey and sugar the same way: limit all of them, and follow your doctor's advice.

# Weight loss

For weight loss, **calories are almost identical**. What matters:

- **How many cups of sweet tea or coffee** you drink a day
- **Sweets, payasam and laddoos**
- **Packaged juices and soft drinks**

**Example:** 3 cups of tea with 2 tsp sugar or jaggery each = about 100–115 kcal a day, or about 3,000 kcal a month. Cutting that alone could save roughly 0.4 kg of fat a month.

# Does jaggery "clean the lungs" or help digestion?

These are traditional beliefs. There is **no strong scientific evidence** that jaggery detoxes the body, cleans the lungs, or treats anaemia. Enjoy it for its taste, not as medicine.

# Quality concerns

- Some jaggery is made with **chemical clarifiers** or added colour to look lighter.
- Darker, plain jaggery from a trusted source is usually less processed.
- Store it in a dry, airtight container to avoid moisture and fungus.

# Which should you choose?

- **For taste in traditional dishes** (pongal, payasam, adhirasam): jaggery is great
- **For tea and coffee:** reduce the amount, whichever you use
- **For weight loss:** reduce both
- **For diabetes:** limit both, following medical advice

# How to cut sugar without misery

- **Reduce slowly:** go from 2 tsp to 1.5 to 1 over a few weeks
- **Add flavour instead:** cardamom, ginger or cinnamon in tea
- **Sweeten with fruit:** banana or dates in oats and shakes
- **Keep sweets for occasions,** not every day

See [how to read nutrition labels](/blog/how-to-read-nutrition-labels-india) to spot hidden sugar in packaged foods.

# Frequently asked questions

**Is jaggery good for weight loss?**
No more than sugar. Both have similar calories.

**Is palm jaggery (karupatti) healthier?**
It has a slightly different taste and mineral profile, but calories and blood sugar effects are similar.

**Can I eat jaggery after meals?**
A small piece occasionally is fine for most healthy people. Just count it.
