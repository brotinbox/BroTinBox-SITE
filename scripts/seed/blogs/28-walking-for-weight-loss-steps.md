> **Quick answer:** Walking helps weight loss mainly by adding steady, easy calorie burn, and it is one of the few habits people can keep for years. For a 70 kg person, 10,000 steps (about 7–8 km) burns roughly **250–300 kcal**. Research on long-term health shows most of the benefit by about **7,000–8,000 steps a day** for older adults and around **8,000–10,000** for younger adults. Walking works best with a calorie deficit and enough protein; it cannot outwalk a poor diet.

Walking is the most underrated tool in fat loss. It needs no gym, no equipment and no recovery days. You can do it at Race Course, on the lake walkways at Ukkadam and Kurichi, on your terrace, or up and down your own street.

But how much do you really need, and does it actually move the scale? Here are the numbers.

# Where 10,000 steps came from

The 10,000 figure did not come from a study. It became popular in Japan in the 1960s, when a pedometer was sold under a name that meant "10,000-step meter". It was a round, memorable number.

Since then, researchers have tested it. In a large 2022 analysis of step-count studies, the risk of early death fell as daily steps went up, then levelled off at about **6,000–8,000 steps for adults over 60** and about **8,000–10,000 steps for younger adults**. So 10,000 is a good goal, but it is not a magic line. Going from 3,000 to 7,000 steps does far more for you than going from 10,000 to 14,000.

# How far, and how many calories?

These are averages. Taller people take longer strides, and heavier people burn more per step.

- **Steps per kilometre:** about 1,300–1,400 for most adults
- **10,000 steps:** about 7–8 km
- **Calories burned walking 1 km:** roughly 0.5 kcal per kg of body weight
- **A 70 kg person walking 1 km:** about 35 kcal
- **A 70 kg person walking 10,000 steps:** about 250–300 kcal
- **A 30-minute brisk walk (70 kg):** about 100–140 kcal

To put that in context, 250–300 kcal a day adds up to about 1,750–2,100 kcal a week. That is roughly a quarter of a kilo of fat, from walking alone, if your eating stays the same.

# Why walking works better than the numbers suggest

## 1. It does not make you ravenous

Hard workouts can increase appetite, and many people eat back the calories they burned. Walking burns energy without much extra hunger.

## 2. You can do it every day

A hard gym session needs recovery. A walk does not. Consistency matters more than intensity for fat loss, and walking is the easiest habit to keep for months.

## 3. It helps your blood sugar after meals

A 10–15 minute walk after lunch or dinner helps your muscles use the sugar from the meal, which reduces the rise in blood sugar. This is helpful for everyone, and especially useful for people with prediabetes or diabetes. If you have diabetes, follow your doctor's advice on exercise.

## 4. It stops the "slowdown" when you diet

When you eat less, your body quietly moves less: you sit more, fidget less and take the lift. Tracking steps catches this. If your weight loss has stalled, our guide to the [weight loss plateau](/blog/weight-loss-plateau-why-scale-stopped) explains why daily movement is often the missing piece.

# A simple plan to build up

1. **Measure your normal day.** Use your phone or watch for one week, without changing anything. Note your average.
2. **Add 2,000 steps a day.** For most people, that is a 15–20 minute walk.
3. **Hold it for two weeks**, then add another 1,000–2,000.
4. **Stop at a number you can keep.** For most adults, 7,000–10,000 a day is a great target.

Easy ways to add steps:

- Walk 10 minutes after lunch and 10 minutes after dinner.
- Take phone calls standing or walking.
- Park further away, or get off the bus one stop early.
- Walk to the nearby shop instead of ordering.

# Walking in Coimbatore weather

Coimbatore is milder than most of Tamil Nadu, but afternoons can still be hot.

- **Walk early morning or after 5:30 PM** on hot days.
- **Carry water** on walks longer than 30–40 minutes.
- **Wear proper walking shoes**, not slippers, if you are walking more than a few kilometres.
- **On rainy days**, walk indoors: corridors, a mall, your terrace or simply around your house.

# What walking cannot do on its own

Walking cannot cancel out a large calorie surplus. One extra plate of biryani can easily equal 10,000 steps. Walking also does not build much muscle, so pair it with some strength training 2–3 times a week if you can.

The best results come from three things together:

- **A modest calorie deficit.** See our [calorie deficit guide](/blog/calorie-deficit-how-to-calculate).
- **Enough protein** to protect your muscle. See [how much protein you need per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day).
- **Daily walking**, plus strength training if possible.

# Frequently asked questions

**Is walking enough to lose weight?**
It can be, if your eating is in a deficit. Walking alone without changing your diet usually gives slow results. Together with measured meals, it works very well.

**Is it better to walk in the morning or evening?**
The best time is the time you will actually stick to. A walk after a meal has an extra benefit for blood sugar.

**Does brisk walking burn more than slow walking?**
Per kilometre, the difference is fairly small. Brisk walking covers more distance in the same time, so you burn more per minute. It is also better for your heart.

**Do I need 10,000 steps every day?**
No. Any increase from your current average helps. Most of the health benefit appears by about 7,000–8,000 steps a day.
