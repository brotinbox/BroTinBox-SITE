> **Quick answer:** Most Indian adults need roughly **1,600–2,000 kcal a day (women)** and **2,000–2,600 kcal a day (men)** to maintain weight, depending on size and activity. To calculate yours: work out your **BMR** with the Mifflin-St Jeor formula, multiply by an **activity factor (1.2–1.7)**, then **subtract about 500 kcal to lose weight** or **add 300–500 kcal to gain muscle**. Check the result against your actual weight trend for 2 weeks and adjust.

"How many calories should I eat?" has no single answer. It depends on your body, your activity and your goal. Here is a simple way to find your number.

# Step 1: Calculate your BMR

**BMR (basal metabolic rate)** is what your body burns at complete rest. The **Mifflin-St Jeor** formula is one of the most accurate simple estimates:

**Men:**
BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) + 5

**Women:**
BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) − 161

**Example (man):** 75 kg, 172 cm, 30 years
= 750 + 1,075 − 150 + 5 = **1,680 kcal**

**Example (woman):** 62 kg, 158 cm, 30 years
= 620 + 987.5 − 150 − 161 = **about 1,297 kcal**

# Step 2: Multiply by your activity level

- **Sedentary** (desk job, little exercise): × 1.2
- **Lightly active** (walking, exercise 1–3 days a week): × 1.375
- **Moderately active** (exercise 3–5 days a week): × 1.55
- **Very active** (hard exercise 6–7 days or physical job): × 1.725

This gives your **maintenance calories (TDEE)**.

**Man above, moderately active:** 1,680 × 1.55 = **about 2,600 kcal**
**Woman above, lightly active:** 1,297 × 1.375 = **about 1,780 kcal**

Most people **overestimate** their activity. If unsure, choose the lower level.

# Step 3: Adjust for your goal

**Lose weight:** maintenance − 300 to 500 kcal

- Man: about 2,100–2,300 kcal
- Woman: about 1,300–1,500 kcal
- Do not go below about 1,200 kcal (women) or 1,500 kcal (men) without medical advice

See [how to calculate a calorie deficit](/blog/calorie-deficit-how-to-calculate).

**Gain muscle:** maintenance + 300 to 500 kcal

See [calories to build muscle](/blog/calories-to-build-muscle).

**Maintain:** eat at maintenance.

# Step 4: Test and adjust

Formulas are estimates. Your real number can be 10–15% different.

1. Eat your target calories for **2 weeks**
2. Weigh 3–4 times a week and use the **weekly average**
3. Compare with your goal: lose about 0.5–1% of body weight a week, or gain about 0.25–0.5 kg a week
4. Adjust by **100–200 kcal** if needed

# Quick estimates (if you do not want the maths)

**Maintenance:**

- Sedentary: body weight (kg) × 25–28
- Moderately active: × 29–33
- Very active: × 34–38

# Calories are not everything

Once you have your number, the quality matters:

- **Protein:** 1.2–2.2 g per kg depending on your goal. See [how much protein per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day).
- **Fibre:** vegetables, dal, whole grains
- **Fats:** nuts, fish, measured oil
- **Carbs:** rice, chapati, millets, fruit in sensible portions

# Common calorie traps in Indian food

- **Oil:** 1 tbsp = about 120 kcal
- **Ghee on chapati:** 1 tsp = about 45 kcal
- **Sugar in tea:** 2 tsp = about 32 kcal, times 3–4 cups a day
- **Parotta:** about 250–300 kcal each
- **Biryani plate:** often 700–1,000 kcal

See [calories in South Indian food](/blog/calories-in-south-indian-food).

# Frequently asked questions

**Is 1,200 kcal a day enough?**
For most adults, it is too low to sustain, except some smaller women under guidance.

**Do I need to count calories forever?**
No. Count for a few weeks to learn portions, then use the plate method.

**Why am I not losing weight on 1,500 kcal?**
You may be underestimating portions or oil. Track carefully for a week. See [why the scale stopped moving](/blog/weight-loss-plateau-why-scale-stopped).
