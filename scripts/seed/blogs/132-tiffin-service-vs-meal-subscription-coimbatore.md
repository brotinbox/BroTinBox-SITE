If you live alone, work long hours or are a student in Coimbatore, you have probably considered a tiffin service or a meal subscription. Here is how they compare.

# What a typical tiffin service offers

- **Home-style South Indian meals:** rice, sambar or kuzhambu, rasam, poriyal, curd
- **Chapati and kurma** for dinner in some places
- **Monthly payment,** often lower per meal than a restaurant
- **Delivered to home, hostel or office**

**Good points**

- Affordable
- Familiar, comforting food
- Better than eating out every day

**Limitations**

- **Low protein:** often 12–20 g per meal
- **Large rice portions**
- **Oil and salt not controlled**
- **No calorie or macro information**
- **Same few dishes** repeated

# Which should you choose?

**Choose a tiffin service if:**

- Your main need is **affordable, home-style food**
- You do not have a specific weight or fitness goal
- You can add your own protein (eggs, curd, sprouts)

**Choose a measured meal subscription if:**

- You want to **lose weight** or **build muscle**
- You want to **know your protein and calories**
- You train at a gym and struggle to eat enough protein
- You want less oil and measured portions

**Or combine both:** a tiffin for one meal, a measured protein box for the other.

# Make a tiffin healthier

- **Ask for less rice** and more poriyal
- **Add protein:** 2 boiled eggs, a cup of curd, sprouts or sundal
- **Skip appalam and fried sides** most days
- **Drink buttermilk** instead of sugary drinks

# Frequently asked questions

**Is a tiffin service healthy?**
It is usually healthier than eating out daily, but often low in protein and high in rice.

**Why does a measured subscription cost more?**
More protein (chicken, fish, paneer), weighed portions and measured oil cost more to prepare.

**Can students afford a meal subscription?**
The Lite box monthly plan starts at ₹159 a meal (veg). Many students take one box a day and cook or use a mess for the other meal.
