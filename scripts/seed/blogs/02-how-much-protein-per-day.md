> **Quick answer:** A healthy, inactive adult needs about **0.8 g of protein per kg of body weight** per day (ICMR-NIN sets it at 0.83 g/kg). If you train regularly or are trying to lose fat without losing muscle, aim for **1.4–2.0 g per kg**. A 70 kg person who goes to the gym should eat roughly **100–140 g of protein a day**, spread across 3–4 meals.

Protein is the one nutrient most Indian diets fall short on. A typical day of idli, sambar, rice, rasam and a small portion of dal or curry tastes great, but it often delivers far less protein than an active adult needs. The result: you feel hungry soon after meals, lose muscle when you diet, and recover slowly from workouts.

This guide shows you how to calculate your number and how to reach it with everyday Indian food.

# How to calculate your daily protein requirement

Use your current body weight in kilograms and pick the line that matches you:

- **Sedentary adult, general health:** 0.8–1.0 g per kg
- **Walking, yoga or light exercise most days:** 1.0–1.2 g per kg
- **Adults over 60** (to slow age-related muscle loss): 1.0–1.2 g per kg
- **Gym training 3–5 days a week:** 1.4–2.0 g per kg
- **Losing fat while lifting weights:** 1.6–2.2 g per kg (higher protein protects muscle during a calorie deficit)

## Worked examples

- **60 kg office worker, mostly sitting:** 60 × 0.9 = **about 55 g per day**
- **70 kg man lifting 4 days a week:** 70 × 1.6 = **about 112 g per day**
- **55 kg woman doing strength training and trying to lose fat:** 55 × 1.8 = **about 100 g per day**

If you carry a lot of extra body fat, calculate using your goal weight instead of your current weight. This avoids an unrealistically high number.

> Important: if you have kidney disease, are pregnant, or have been told to limit protein, follow your doctor's advice rather than these general ranges.

# Why the total is not enough: spread it across meals

Your body builds and repairs muscle best when each meal carries a meaningful dose of protein. Research suggests roughly **0.3–0.4 g per kg per meal**, which works out to **20–40 g per meal** for most adults.

This is where the typical Indian day goes wrong. Breakfast is often 6–10 g of protein (two dosas with chutney), lunch is mostly rice, and almost all the protein lands at dinner. Moving 25–30 g of protein into breakfast is one of the easiest improvements you can make. # How much protein is in common Indian foods?

Approximate values, which vary with brand and recipe:

- **Chicken breast, cooked, 100 g:** about 31 g
- **1 whole egg:** about 6 g
- **Paneer, 100 g:** about 18–20 g
- **Soya chunks, dry, 50 g:** about 26 g
- **Fish such as mackerel, cooked, 100 g:** about 20–24 g
- **Cooked dal, 1 katori (about 150 g):** about 7–9 g
- **Boiled chana or rajma, 100 g:** about 8–9 g
- **Curd, 100 g:** about 3–4 g (hung curd: about 8–10 g)
- **Milk, 1 glass (250 ml):** about 8 g
- **Peanuts, 30 g:** about 7–8 g
- **Cooked rice, 1 cup:** about 4 g
- **2 idlis:** about 4 g

For a deeper comparison of the four most popular sources, read [chicken vs eggs vs paneer vs soya](/blog/chicken-eggs-paneer-soya-protein-comparison).

# A sample 110 g protein day with Indian food

- **Breakfast (about 30 g):** 3-egg bhurji + 1 chapati + 1 glass milk
- **Lunch (about 30 g):** 100 g grilled chicken or 150 g paneer, 1 katori dal, rice, vegetables
- **Snack (about 15 g):** chana sundal + buttermilk
- **Dinner (about 35 g):** fish curry or soya chunk stir-fry, sprouted green gram salad, 2 chapatis

None of this needs protein powder. Powder is convenient, not essential.

# Frequently asked questions

**Is too much protein bad for the kidneys?**
In healthy adults, protein intakes up to about 2 g per kg have not been shown to harm kidney function. People with existing kidney disease are different and should follow medical advice.

**Can vegetarians get enough protein?**
Yes. Combine dairy (paneer, curd, milk), soya, and legumes (chana, rajma, kollu, green gram) across the day. Our Tuesday veg box shows it can be done with **43.8 g in one meal**.

**Do I need protein on rest days?**
Yes. Muscle repair continues for 24–48 hours after training. Keep your intake roughly the same every day.

**Is it better to eat protein before or after a workout?**
Total daily protein matters far more than exact timing. Having a protein-rich meal within a few hours either side of training is enough.
