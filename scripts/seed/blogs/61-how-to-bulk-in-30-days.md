> **Quick answer:** In 30 days, a realistic lean bulk adds about **1–2 kg on the scale**, of which roughly **0.5–1 kg is muscle** for a beginner. To get there, eat about **300–500 kcal above maintenance**, take **1.6–2.2 g of protein per kg** of body weight, lift **3–5 days a week** with progressive overload, and sleep 7–8 hours. Gaining much faster than this mostly adds fat.

"Bulk" just means eating in a calorie surplus to build muscle. Thirty days is enough to start a good bulk and see real strength gains. It is not enough to transform your body. Here is how to make the month count.

# What 30 days of bulking can do

For a beginner who trains well and eats enough:

- **Scale weight:** about 1–2 kg up
- **Muscle:** about 0.5–1 kg (beginners gain faster than experienced lifters)
- **Strength:** often the biggest change, with most lifts going up every week
- **Look:** a little fuller in the arms, chest and shoulders

Part of the first-week jump is water and food in your gut, not muscle. That is normal.

If you have been training for 2+ years, expect less: maybe 0.25–0.5 kg of muscle in a month.

# Step 1: Find your maintenance calories

Maintenance is what you eat now to stay the same weight. A quick estimate:

- **Mostly sitting job, training 3–4 days:** body weight (kg) × 30–33
- **Active job or training 5–6 days:** body weight (kg) × 34–38

For a 60 kg person training 4 days a week: about 60 × 32 = **1,920 kcal**.

Better still, track what you eat for one week. If your weight stays flat, that is your maintenance.

# Step 2: Add a small surplus

Add **300–500 kcal a day** on top of maintenance.

- Skinny and struggling to gain? Aim for 500.
- Already carrying some belly fat? Aim for 250–300.

For our 60 kg example: 1,920 + 400 = **about 2,300 kcal a day**.

Our guide on [how many calories you need to build muscle](/blog/calories-to-build-muscle) goes deeper into setting the number.

# Step 3: Hit your protein every day

Aim for **1.6–2.2 g of protein per kg** of body weight. For 60 kg, that is about **100–130 g a day**.

Easy Indian sources:

- **Chicken breast:** about 31 g protein per 100 g cooked
- **Eggs:** about 6 g each
- **Paneer:** about 18–20 g per 100 g
- **Soya chunks:** about 50 g per 100 g dry
- **Curd, milk, dal, chana, rajma**

Split it across 3–4 meals, about 25–40 g each. See [how much protein per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day) to set your exact target.

# Step 4: Train for muscle, not just sweat

Food without training just makes you heavier. A simple plan for 30 days:

- **3–5 sessions a week**, each muscle trained about twice
- **Compound lifts first:** squat, bench press, rows, overhead press, Romanian deadlift
- **3–4 sets of 6–12 reps**, stopping 1–2 reps before failure
- **Add weight or reps every week** (progressive overload)

A [push pull legs plan](/blog/push-pull-legs-workout-plan) works very well for this.

# A sample 2,300 kcal bulking day (Indian)

- **Breakfast:** 3 eggs (omelette), 2 chapatis, a glass of milk
- **Mid-morning:** banana with 2 tbsp peanut butter
- **Lunch:** 150 g grilled chicken, 1 cup rice, dal, vegetables, curd
- **Evening:** sundal (1 cup chana) and tea
- **Dinner:** 100 g paneer or fish, 2 chapatis, vegetables
- **Before bed:** a glass of milk

That gives roughly **2,300 kcal and 120–130 g protein**. Vegetarians can swap chicken for soya chunks and paneer.

# How to track progress

- **Weigh yourself 3–4 mornings a week** and use the weekly average.
- **Target:** about 0.25–0.5 kg up per week.
- **Gaining faster than 0.5 kg a week?** Cut 200 kcal.
- **Not gaining after 2 weeks?** Add 200 kcal.
- **Track lifts.** If your numbers go up, you are building muscle.

# Common bulking mistakes

- **Dirty bulking:** parotta, biryani and sweets every day add fat fast. Read [dirty bulk vs clean bulk](/blog/dirty-bulk-vs-clean-bulk).
- **Low protein:** extra rice alone will not build muscle.
- **Skipping legs:** your biggest muscles drive the most growth.
- **Poor sleep:** under 6 hours a night slows recovery.
- **Expecting a new body in a month:** 30 days is the start, not the finish.

# Frequently asked questions

**Can I bulk without supplements?**
Yes. Whey and creatine help, but food covers everything. Our guide on [protein shakes without protein powder](/blog/protein-shake-without-protein-powder) has easy options.

**Should I bulk or cut first?**
If you are skinny or average, bulk. If you have a lot of belly fat, cut first. See [bulking vs cutting](/blog/bulking-vs-cutting).

**Will I get fat?**
A small surplus with high protein keeps fat gain low. Some fat gain is normal in a bulk.
