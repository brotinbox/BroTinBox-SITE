> **Quick answer:** **Soya chunks** have about **50–52 g protein per 100 g dry** (about **25 g per 50 g serving**, about 170 kcal), while **paneer** has about **18–20 g per 100 g** with about **260–320 kcal**. Per calorie, soya gives roughly **twice the protein** with far less fat, and it is **much cheaper per gram of protein**. Paneer wins on **taste, familiarity, calcium and ease of cooking**. For weight loss and budget, choose **soya**; for variety and taste, use **both**.

Soya and paneer are the two biggest protein sources for Indian vegetarians. Here is how they compare on protein, calories, cost and health.

# Nutrition compared

**Soya chunks (dry, per 100 g)**

- Calories: about 340–350 kcal
- Protein: about 50–52 g
- Fat: about 0.5–1 g
- Carbs: about 30–33 g
- Fibre: about 13 g

**Per typical serving (50 g dry, about 150 g after soaking):** about **25–26 g protein and 170 kcal**

**Paneer (full-fat, per 100 g)**

- Calories: about 260–320 kcal
- Protein: about 18–20 g
- Fat: about 20–25 g
- Carbs: about 1–3 g
- Calcium: high

**Per typical serving (100 g):** about **18–20 g protein and 260–320 kcal**

# Protein per 100 kcal

- **Soya chunks:** about 15 g protein per 100 kcal
- **Paneer:** about 6–7 g protein per 100 kcal

For the same calories, soya gives you more than **twice the protein**.

# Cost per gram of protein

Soya chunks are one of the **cheapest protein foods** in India. Paneer costs much more per gram of protein. If you are on a budget (students, hostel life), soya is hard to beat. See [gym diet for college students](/blog/gym-diet-for-college-students).

# Which is better for weight loss?

**Soya.** It is high in protein and fibre and very low in fat, so it keeps you full on fewer calories.

Paneer can still fit; measure the portion, or use **low-fat paneer**. See [protein in paneer](/blog/protein-in-paneer).

# Which is better for muscle gain?

**Both work.**

- **Soya:** lots of protein cheaply
- **Paneer:** slow-digesting dairy protein (casein), and the extra fat helps people who struggle to eat enough calories

Many vegetarian lifters eat **soya at lunch and paneer at dinner**.

# Taste and cooking

**Soya**

- Neutral, slightly chewy; needs good masala
- Must be **boiled and squeezed** well
- Great as keema, pulao, pepper fry or in a bowl. Try our [smashed soya bowl](/blog/smashed-soya-bowl-recipe).

**Paneer**

- Mild and creamy; most people love it
- Quick to cook
- Great as tikka, bhurji or caramel onion paneer. See [tandoori paneer tikka](/blog/tandoori-paneer-tikka-recipe) and [caramel onion paneer](/blog/caramel-onion-paneer-recipe).

# Is soya safe?

For most people, yes, in normal food amounts (about **30–50 g dry a day**).

- **Testosterone:** studies have not shown that normal soya intake lowers testosterone in men. Read [soya chunks protein](/blog/soya-chunks-protein).
- **Thyroid:** people with hypothyroidism can usually eat soya; keep intake consistent and take thyroid medicine as prescribed, away from soya meals.
- **Allergy:** avoid if allergic to soy.
- **Gas or bloating:** soak, boil and squeeze well; start with smaller portions.

# Is paneer safe?

Yes. Watch for:

- **Calories** if losing weight
- **Quality:** buy fresh from trusted shops; some loose paneer is adulterated
- **Lactose:** most people tolerate paneer well because it is low in lactose

# Best of both: a weekly plan

- **Mon, Wed, Fri:** soya (keema, pulao or bowl)
- **Tue, Thu, Sat:** paneer (tikka, bhurji or caramel onion)
- **Daily:** dal, curd and sprouts

Compare with tofu too in [paneer vs tofu](/blog/paneer-vs-tofu).

# Frequently asked questions

**How much soya can I eat a day?**
About 30–50 g dry is a common, safe amount for most adults.

**Is soya better than paneer?**
For protein per calorie and per rupee, yes. For taste and calcium, paneer.

**Can I eat soya and paneer together?**
Yes. A soya and paneer bhurji is a great high-protein veg meal.
