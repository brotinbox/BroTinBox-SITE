> **Quick answer:** **Both fish and chicken are excellent protein.** Chicken breast has about **31 g protein per 100 g cooked** and very little fat. Most fish have about **20–26 g protein per 100 g cooked**, and oily fish like **mackerel and sardines** add **omega-3 fats** for heart and brain health. For pure protein per calorie, **chicken breast wins**. For overall health, **fish twice a week plus chicken on other days** is a great mix.

Chicken is the default protein for most gym-goers in Coimbatore. But fish is local, often cheaper, and comes with benefits chicken does not have. Here is how they compare.

# Nutrition per 100 g (cooked)

**Chicken breast (skinless)**

- Calories: about 165 kcal
- Protein: about 31 g
- Fat: about 3–4 g
- Omega-3: very little

**Chicken thigh (skinless)**

- Calories: about 180–210 kcal
- Protein: about 25–26 g
- Fat: about 8–11 g

**Oily fish (mackerel, sardine)**

- Calories: about 200–240 kcal
- Protein: about 22–25 g
- Fat: about 10–15 g, much of it **omega-3**
- Also: vitamin D, B12, selenium; sardines with bones add calcium

**Lean fish (seer, pomfret, tilapia, rohu)**

- Calories: about 120–150 kcal
- Protein: about 20–26 g
- Fat: about 1–5 g

Our kitchen's **grilled mackerel** portion (130 g) has about **305 kcal and 31.5 g protein**. See the [mackerel and omega-3 guide](/blog/mackerel-benefits-omega-3-indian-diet).

# Protein: chicken wins slightly

Chicken breast has the most protein per calorie of almost any common food. Fish is close behind, especially lean fish.

For muscle gain, both work. What matters is your **total protein for the day**. See [how much protein per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day).

# Health: fish wins

Oily fish gives you **EPA and DHA omega-3 fats**, which are linked to:

- **Heart health:** lower triglycerides and lower risk of heart disease
- **Brain and eye health**
- **Less inflammation**

Chicken does not provide meaningful omega-3. That is why health guidelines often suggest **about two servings of fish a week**, ideally oily fish.

# Weight loss: both work

- **Chicken breast** and **lean fish** are lowest in calories.
- **Oily fish** has more calories, but keeps you very full and brings healthy fats.

The cooking method matters most:

- **Grilled, tawa or curry:** good
- **Deep fried:** adds a lot of oil

See [grilled vs air fried](/blog/grilled-vs-air-fried).

# Cost and availability in Coimbatore

- **Chicken:** available everywhere, stable price, easy to cook
- **Mackerel and sardines:** usually among the cheapest protein sources at fish markets, though price varies with season and catch
- **Seer fish (vanjiram), pomfret:** more expensive

Freshness is the main challenge with fish. Buy from a trusted shop and cook the same day.

# Mercury: should you worry?

- **Small fish** like mackerel, sardines and anchovies are generally **low in mercury**.
- **Large predatory fish** like shark and some big tuna are higher.
- Pregnant women should avoid high-mercury fish and follow their doctor's advice.

# Which should you eat?

- **Maximum protein, minimum calories:** chicken breast
- **Heart health and omega-3:** oily fish
- **Budget:** mackerel, sardines, eggs and chicken
- **Best overall:** rotate both through the week

# Frequently asked questions

**Is fish better than chicken for weight loss?**
Lean fish and chicken breast are similar. Oily fish has more calories but healthy fats.

**Can I eat fish every day?**
Small, low-mercury fish can be eaten often. Variety is still a good idea.

**Do I need fish oil capsules if I eat fish?**
Usually not if you eat oily fish twice a week. Ask your doctor if you have high triglycerides.
