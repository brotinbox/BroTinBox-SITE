> **Quick answer:** Your weight can swing **1–2 kg in a day**, and that is almost always **water, not fat**. Water weight changes with **salt, carbs, hormones (periods), hard workouts, creatine, heat, sleep and food in your gut**. Gaining 1 kg of fat needs about **7,700 extra kcal**, so a 1.5 kg jump after a biryani dinner is not fat. To see real fat loss, track your **weekly average weight and waist**, not daily numbers.

You stuck to your diet all week, stepped on the scale, and it went **up**. Before you give up, understand water weight. It explains most scary scale jumps.

# Why water changes your weight

About **50–60% of your body is water**. Your body holds more or less water depending on many daily factors. Fat, on the other hand, changes slowly.

# The 8 most common causes of water weight

## 1. Salt

A salty meal (biryani, pickles, chips, restaurant food) makes your body hold extra water for 1–3 days.

## 2. Carbs

Your body stores carbs as **glycogen**, and each gram holds about **3 g of water**. More carbs = more water. That is why low-carb diets cause fast early "weight loss".

## 3. Periods

Many women retain **1–2 kg of water** in the days before and during their period.

## 4. Hard or new workouts

Sore muscles hold extra fluid while they repair. A tough leg day can show up on the scale for 2–4 days.

## 5. Creatine

Creatine draws water into your muscles, often **1–2 kg** in the first weeks. It is not fat. See [how to take creatine](/blog/how-to-take-creatine).

## 6. Food in your gut

A large dinner or more fibre can add 0.5–1.5 kg until it is digested.

## 7. Stress and poor sleep

High stress hormones can increase water retention.

## 8. Heat and travel

Hot weather, long flights and bus rides can change how much water you hold.

# Why you cannot gain fat overnight

- **1 kg of fat ≈ 7,700 extra kcal**
- A heavy meal of 1,500–2,000 kcal could add at most about 0.2–0.25 kg of fat, and only if it is above your daily needs

So a **1.5 kg jump overnight is not fat**. It will usually drop again in 2–4 days.

# How to tell water from fat

**Water weight**

- Changes fast (overnight, over a few days)
- Follows salty meals, more carbs, periods or hard workouts
- Comes back down within days

**Fat**

- Changes slowly, over weeks
- Follows your average calories over time
- Shows as steady changes in your waist and photos

# How to track weight the right way

- **Weigh 3–4 times a week**, in the morning, after the toilet, before eating
- **Use the weekly average**, not single days
- **Measure your waist** once a week
- **Take photos** every 2–4 weeks

If your weekly average is going down, you are losing fat, even if some days go up.

# How to reduce water retention (safely)

- **Drink enough water.** Being dehydrated can make your body hold more.
- **Keep salt moderate:** less pickle, papad and packaged snacks
- **Eat potassium-rich foods:** bananas, coconut water, vegetables, dal
- **Sleep 7–8 hours**
- **Walk daily**

**Avoid** "water pills", detox teas and laxatives sold for weight loss. They remove water, not fat, and can be harmful.

# Water weight in your first week of dieting

When you start eating less and fewer carbs, you often lose **1–3 kg in week one**. That is mostly water and glycogen. After that, loss slows to the real fat-loss rate of **0.5–1 kg a week**. Read [fat loss vs weight loss](/blog/fat-loss-vs-weight-loss).

# Frequently asked questions

**Why did I gain 2 kg after one cheat meal?**
Salt, carbs and food volume. It is mostly water and will settle in a few days. See [cheat day or cheat meal](/blog/cheat-day).

**Does drinking more water reduce water weight?**
It can help a little if you were under-drinking. It does not burn fat.

**How long does water weight last?**
Usually 2–5 days after the cause (salty meal, period, hard workout).
