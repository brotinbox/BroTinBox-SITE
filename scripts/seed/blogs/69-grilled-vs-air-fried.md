> **Quick answer:** **Both grilled and air-fried food are healthy choices** compared with deep frying. Both use very little oil, often **1 tsp or less per serving**. **Grilling** gives a smoky, charred flavour but can create more charring compounds at very high heat. **Air frying** is easier at home, gives a crispy texture, and is better for "fried" snacks. For chicken, fish and paneer, pick whichever you will actually cook regularly.

Grilled chicken and air-fried chicken both sound like diet food. But are they the same? Here is how they compare on calories, health, taste and convenience.

# How each method works

**Grilling**

- Direct high heat from below (gas grill, charcoal, grill pan, tawa) or above (oven grill)
- Fat drips away from the food
- Creates char marks and a smoky taste

**Air frying**

- A small, powerful oven that blows very hot air around the food
- A thin coat of oil gives a crisp outside
- Works like a mini convection oven

# Calories and oil

Both use far less oil than deep frying.

- **Deep frying:** food soaks up oil. A plate of chicken 65 can carry 2–3 tbsp of oil, which is 240–360 kcal from oil alone.
- **Air frying:** about 1 tsp of oil (about 40 kcal) for a whole batch.
- **Grilling:** a light brush of oil or marinade, often 1 tsp or less.

So per serving, grilled and air-fried food are **very close in calories**. The difference is usually the marinade, not the cooking method. A butter-basted tandoori chicken can have more calories than an air-fried one, and the other way around.

# Health: what to know

## Grilling

- Very high heat and burning can form **HCAs and PAHs**, compounds linked to cancer risk in large amounts over time.
- To reduce them, marinate meat (curd, lemon and spices help), do not burn the food black, cut off heavily charred bits, and use medium-high heat rather than open flames licking the meat.

## Air frying

- Lower risk of heavy charring
- For **starchy foods like potatoes**, air frying produces less acrylamide than deep frying
- Easy to overcook small pieces and dry them out

**Bottom line:** both are healthy for everyday cooking. Neither is a reason to avoid good food.

# Taste and texture

- **Grilled:** smoky, charred edges, juicy if done right. Best for tandoori chicken, fish, paneer tikka, kebabs.
- **Air fried:** crispy outside, softer inside. Best for "fried" snacks: chicken fry, fish fry, cutlets, potato wedges, vadai-style snacks.

# Convenience at home

**Grill pan or tawa**

- Cheap, already in most kitchens
- Needs attention: turning, watching heat
- Smoke in the kitchen

**Air fryer**

- Costs ₹3,000–8,000
- Set and forget, little smoke, easy cleaning
- Small capacity; you may need batches

# Which should you choose?

- **Tandoori chicken, fish, paneer tikka:** grill
- **Chicken fry, fish fry, cutlets, wedges:** air fry
- **Weight loss:** either; measure the oil and the portion
- **Cooking every day for a family:** a grill pan or oven tray is easier for big batches

# Healthy cooking tips for both

- **Use a curd marinade:** it keeps meat tender and adds protein
- **Measure oil with a spoon,** not a pour
- **Season well:** pepper, garlic, ginger, mint, lemon
- **Pair with fibre:** salad, poriyal, sprouts
- **Do not undercook chicken:** it should be white all the way through

# Frequently asked questions

**Is air-fried food as healthy as boiled food?**
Close. Boiled food has no added oil, but air-fried food only adds a little.

**Does air frying destroy protein?**
No. The protein stays; cooking only changes its texture.

**Is grilled chicken good for weight loss?**
Yes. It is one of the best high-protein, low-calorie foods. Watch the butter and the portion.
