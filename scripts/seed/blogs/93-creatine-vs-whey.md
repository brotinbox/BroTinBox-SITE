> **Quick answer:** **Creatine and whey do different jobs.** **Whey protein** is a convenient source of protein (about **20–25 g per scoop**) that helps you hit your daily protein target. **Creatine** (3–5 g a day) helps your muscles produce quick energy, so you can **lift more and recover faster**. If you already eat enough protein from food, **creatine adds more** than whey. If you struggle to reach your protein target, **whey helps more**. Many people use both safely.

"Should I buy whey or creatine?" is one of the most common questions from new gym-goers in India. They are often sold side by side, but they are not the same thing at all.

# What whey protein is

- A **milk protein**, separated during cheese and paneer making
- About **20–25 g protein per scoop**, quickly digested
- **Just food in powder form;** it does nothing that the same amount of protein from chicken, eggs or paneer would not do
- Types: **concentrate** (cheaper, a little lactose), **isolate** (purer, less lactose), **hydrolysate** (pre-digested, expensive)

# What creatine is

- A natural compound stored in your **muscles** for quick energy
- Taken as **creatine monohydrate**, 3–5 g a day
- **Not a protein,** has no calories worth counting
- Helps with **strength, power and muscle gain** over time with training

Read [is creatine good for health](/blog/is-creatine-good-for-health) and [how to take creatine](/blog/how-to-take-creatine).

# Side-by-side

**Main job**

- Whey: fills your protein gap
- Creatine: improves strength and training performance

**Do you need it?**

- Whey: only if you cannot get enough protein from food
- Creatine: never essential, but proven to help

**Dose**

- Whey: 1 scoop (20–25 g protein) as needed
- Creatine: 3–5 g every day

**Timing**

- Whey: any time; after training is convenient
- Creatine: any time, daily

**Cost per day (approx.)**

- Whey: higher per day
- Creatine: low per day

**Can vegetarians use it?**

- Whey: yes (it is from milk)
- Creatine: yes (supplement creatine is made synthetically, not from meat)

# Which should you buy first?

## Choose whey first if:

- You eat less than about **1.2 g protein per kg** of body weight
- You are **vegetarian** and find it hard to get protein
- You have **no time to cook** protein-rich food
- You skip meals often

## Choose creatine first if:

- You already eat enough protein (**1.6+ g per kg**)
- You **lift weights 3+ times a week**
- You want a **small, proven edge** in strength

## Use both if:

- You train seriously, want maximum results and can afford both

# Can you take them together?

Yes. Mix 3–5 g of creatine into your whey shake after training, or take them separately. There is no problem combining them.

# Food first, always

Neither supplement replaces good food. Before buying either, check:

- Are you eating **protein at every meal**? See [how much protein per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day).
- Are you **training consistently** with a plan?
- Are you **sleeping 7–8 hours**?

You can hit your protein without powder: see [protein shakes without protein powder](/blog/protein-shake-without-protein-powder) and [protein powder vs real food](/blog/protein-powder-vs-real-food-india).

# Safety and buying tips

- Buy from **authorised sellers** with an **FSSAI licence** and batch number on the pack
- Prefer brands with **third-party testing**; fake whey is a real problem in India
- Avoid products with **"proprietary blends"** and hidden ingredients
- **Lactose intolerant?** Try whey isolate, or plant protein
- **Kidney disease?** Talk to your doctor before using either

# Frequently asked questions

**Does whey or creatine build more muscle?**
Neither builds muscle alone. Whey helps if your protein is low; creatine helps you train harder. Training does the building.

**Will creatine or whey make me gain fat?**
No. Whey has calories like any food. Creatine adds water weight inside muscles, not fat.

**Is whey safe for daily use?**
Yes, for healthy people, as part of a normal diet.
