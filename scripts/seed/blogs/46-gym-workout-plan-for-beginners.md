> **Quick answer:** For your first month in the gym, train **3 days a week** (for example Monday, Wednesday, Friday) with a **full-body workout** of **5–6 exercises**, **2–3 sets of 8–12 reps**, using light-to-moderate weights you can control. Focus on learning form, not lifting heavy. Add a little weight each week, walk daily, and eat enough protein.

Your first day at the gym can feel overwhelming. Machines everywhere, people who look like they know what they are doing, and no idea where to start.

This is a simple, safe plan for your first 4 weeks.

# Why full body, 3 days a week?

- **Each muscle is trained 3 times a week**, which is great for beginners.
- **Rest days in between** let your body recover and reduce soreness.
- **Fewer exercises to learn**, so you get good at the basics fast.
- **Fits a busy schedule.** Three hours a week is enough to see real changes.

After 4–8 weeks you can move to a 4-day upper/lower split or a [push pull legs plan](/blog/push-pull-legs-workout-plan).

# The beginner full-body workout

Do this on Monday, Wednesday and Friday (or any three non-consecutive days).

1. **Leg press or goblet squat:** 3 sets of 10–12
2. **Chest press machine or dumbbell bench press:** 3 sets of 10–12
3. **Lat pulldown:** 3 sets of 10–12
4. **Seated cable row:** 3 sets of 10–12
5. **Dumbbell shoulder press (seated):** 2 sets of 10–12
6. **Plank:** 3 sets of 20–40 seconds

Optional at the end: **dumbbell curls and triceps pushdowns**, 2 sets of 12 each.

# Why machines first?

Machines guide the movement, so they are safer while you learn. In weeks 3–4, start adding free-weight versions:

- Goblet squat → barbell squat (with a trainer's guidance)
- Chest press machine → dumbbell bench press
- Romanian deadlift with light dumbbells

# How heavy should I lift?

In week 1, pick a weight you could lift about **15 times**, but stop at 10–12. That leaves room to focus on form.

Every week:
- If all sets feel easy at 12 reps, **increase the weight slightly**.
- If you cannot finish 8 reps with good form, **reduce the weight**.

Write your weights down. Progress is the goal.

# Warm-up and cool-down

**Warm-up (5–10 minutes):**
- 5 minutes on the treadmill or cycle
- Arm circles, hip circles, bodyweight squats
- One light set of your first exercise

**Cool-down (5 minutes):**
- Slow walk
- Light stretches for legs, chest and back

# The 4-week progression

- **Week 1:** 2 sets per exercise, light weights, learn the movements
- **Week 2:** 3 sets per exercise, same or slightly heavier
- **Week 3:** 3 sets, add weight where it feels easy
- **Week 4:** 3 sets, keep adding weight; start trying free-weight versions

# What about cardio?

- **Walk 7,000–10,000 steps a day.** This matters more than treadmill sessions for most beginners. See [walking for weight loss](/blog/walking-for-weight-loss-steps).
- **10–15 minutes of cardio** after weights is fine if you enjoy it.
- Do not spend an hour on the treadmill and skip weights. Strength training builds the shape most people want.

# Soreness: what is normal?

It is normal to feel sore 1–2 days after your first few workouts (called DOMS). It gets much less after 2–3 weeks.

Not normal: **sharp pain, pain in a joint, or pain that gets worse during an exercise**. Stop and ask a trainer or doctor.

# Eat for your goal

Training without the right food gives slow results:

- **Protein:** about 1.6 g per kg of body weight daily. See [how much protein per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day).
- **Fat loss:** a small calorie deficit plus daily walking.
- **Muscle gain:** a small calorie surplus.

Our [beginner gym diet chart](/blog/gym-diet-chart-for-beginners) gives a full Indian day of eating, veg and non-veg.

# Beginner mistakes to avoid

1. **Copying advanced routines** from Instagram.
2. **Lifting too heavy too soon** with poor form.
3. **Training the same muscle every day.**
4. **Skipping legs.**
5. **Not eating enough protein.**
6. **Quitting after week 2** because results are not visible yet. Visible changes usually take 6–8 weeks.

# Frequently asked questions

**How long should a beginner workout last?**
About 45–60 minutes including warm-up.

**Can I train every day as a beginner?**
It is better to start with 3 days and add more later. Walk on the other days.

**Should I hire a personal trainer?**
A few sessions to learn form is very helpful, especially for squats and deadlifts.

**When will I see results?**
Strength improves in 2–3 weeks. Visible changes usually show in 6–12 weeks with good diet.
