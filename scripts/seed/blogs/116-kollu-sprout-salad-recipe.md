> **Quick answer:** To make **kollu (horse gram) sprout salad**, soak dry kollu overnight, drain and let it **sprout for 1–2 days** in a damp cloth, then **steam or boil the sprouts for 8–10 minutes** until tender. Toss with **onion, tomato, cucumber, carrot, green chilli, coriander, lemon, salt and pepper**, with an optional mustard and curry leaf tempering. **100 g of sprouted kollu** gives about **135 kcal, 9 g protein and 8 g fibre**.

Kollu is a traditional Kongu and South Indian legume, packed with fibre and plant protein. Sprouting makes it lighter and easier to cook. This simple salad is a version of the **Kollu Sprout Bowl** side in our BroTin Box menu.

# Ingredients (serves 2)

**Sprouts**

- ½ cup dry kollu (horse gram), about 90–100 g
- Water for soaking

**Salad**

- 1 small onion, finely chopped
- 1 tomato, deseeded and chopped
- ½ cucumber, chopped
- 1 small carrot, grated
- 1 green chilli, finely chopped
- 2 tbsp chopped coriander
- Juice of ½–1 lemon
- ¼ tsp black pepper powder
- ¼ tsp roasted cumin powder
- Salt to taste

**Optional tempering**

- ½ tsp oil
- ¼ tsp mustard seeds
- A few curry leaves
- 1 tbsp grated coconut

# Method

## 1. Soak (day 1, night)

Wash the kollu well, removing any stones. Soak in plenty of water for **8–12 hours** or overnight.

## 2. Sprout (day 2–3)

- Drain the water.
- Tie the kollu in a damp cotton cloth, or keep it in a covered bowl or sprout maker.
- Leave in a warm spot for **1–2 days**, sprinkling water once or twice a day.
- Small white tails will appear. Kollu sprouts more slowly than green gram; be patient.

## 3. Cook the sprouts

Raw sprouts can carry bacteria, and kollu is also easier to digest when cooked.

- **Steam** the sprouts for **8–10 minutes**, or
- **Pressure cook** with a pinch of salt for **1 whistle** on low flame, or
- **Boil** for **10–12 minutes** until tender but still holding shape

Drain and cool.

## 4. Make the salad

In a bowl, mix the cooked sprouts with onion, tomato, cucumber, carrot, green chilli and coriander. Add pepper, cumin and salt. Squeeze in lemon juice just before serving.

## 5. Tempering (optional)

Heat oil, add mustard seeds and curry leaves. When they splutter, pour over the salad. Top with grated coconut for a sundal-style finish.

# Nutrition

For **100 g sprouted kollu** (before adding vegetables):

- **Calories:** about 135 kcal
- **Protein:** about 9 g
- **Fibre:** about 8 g

With vegetables and lemon, a serving bowl is about **170–200 kcal and 10–11 g protein**.

# Ways to eat it

- **Evening snack** instead of bajji or biscuits
- **Side with lunch** to add protein and fibre to a rice meal
- **With grilled chicken or paneer** for a high-protein plate
- **Breakfast** with a boiled egg and a cup of curd

# Tips

- **Store sprouts** in the fridge in a closed box for up to 2 days.
- **Add lemon only before serving** so the vegetables stay crisp.
- **If sprouts smell sour or slimy,** throw them away.
- **Mix with green gram sprouts** for a quicker, softer salad.

# Is kollu good for everyone?

Kollu is nutritious for most people. Because it is rich in oxalates, people with a history of **kidney stones** should ask their doctor how much is right for them. Cook it well, and drink enough water.

# Frequently asked questions

**Does kollu help weight loss?**
It helps by keeping you full with fibre and protein. It does not burn fat by itself.

**Can I eat raw kollu sprouts?**
We recommend cooking them for safety and easier digestion.

**How is this different from sundal?**
Sundal is cooked kollu tempered with coconut; this salad adds raw vegetables and lemon for crunch and freshness.
