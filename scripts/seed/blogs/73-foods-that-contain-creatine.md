> **Quick answer:** Creatine is found mainly in **meat and fish**. Raw **herring** has about **6.5–10 g per kg**, **beef and pork** about **4.5–5 g per kg**, **salmon and tuna** about **4–4.5 g per kg**, and **chicken and other fish** roughly **3–4 g per kg**. Milk has only traces, and **plant foods have almost none**. Since a supplement dose is 3–5 g, you would need to eat **about 1 kg of meat or fish a day** to match it, which is why many lifters use creatine monohydrate.

Creatine supplements are popular, but creatine itself is a natural part of food. If you prefer to get it from your plate, here is where it comes from and how much you can realistically eat.

# How much creatine do you need?

- Your body **makes about 1 g a day** in the liver and kidneys.
- A non-vegetarian diet adds about **0.5–1 g a day**.
- That keeps your muscles at a normal level.

To **fill your muscle stores fully**, which is what helps performance, studies use **3–5 g a day** on top of this. That is where supplements come in.

# Foods with the most creatine

Approximate amounts per kg of **raw** food. Cooking reduces creatine somewhat, especially long boiling or frying.

**Fish**

- **Herring:** about 6.5–10 g per kg
- **Salmon:** about 4.5 g per kg
- **Tuna:** about 4 g per kg
- **Cod and other white fish:** about 3 g per kg

Local fish like **mackerel (kaanaangeluthi), sardines (mathi) and seer fish (vanjiram)** also contain creatine, in a broadly similar range to other fish. Exact figures for Indian species are not well measured.

**Meat**

- **Pork:** about 5 g per kg
- **Beef:** about 4.5 g per kg
- **Mutton:** similar range to beef
- **Chicken:** about 3–4 g per kg

**Other foods**

- **Milk:** about 0.1 g per kg (very little)
- **Eggs:** very little
- **Paneer, curd, cheese:** very little
- **Plant foods:** essentially none

# What this means on your plate

A typical 150 g chicken portion has about **0.5 g of creatine**. A 150 g fish portion has about **0.5–0.7 g**.

So:

- **2 meat or fish meals a day:** about 1–1.5 g of creatine from food
- **To reach 5 g from food alone:** about **1–1.2 kg of meat or fish a day**

That is a lot of food, and expensive. It is why supplements are popular for people who want the full benefit.

# Can vegetarians get creatine from food?

Not really. Vegetarian and vegan diets contain almost no creatine. Your body still makes about 1 g a day, so you are not "deficient", but your muscle stores are usually lower.

This is why **vegetarians often see bigger gains from creatine supplements**. Our [protein in dal, chana and rajma](/blog/protein-in-dal-chana-rajma) guide helps vegetarians cover their protein.

# Does your body need help to make creatine?

Your body builds creatine from three amino acids: **arginine, glycine and methionine**. Eating enough protein gives you plenty of all three. You do not need special foods to "boost" production.

Make sure you get enough protein each day. See [how much protein per day](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day).

# Food vs supplement: which is better?

**Food**

- Comes with protein, iron, zinc, B12 and omega-3 (in fish)
- Hard to reach 3–5 g of creatine a day
- No supplement cost

**Creatine monohydrate**

- 3–5 g in one scoop, very cheap per day
- Proven to help strength and muscle gain
- Safe for healthy adults; see [is creatine good for health](/blog/is-creatine-good-for-health)

Many people do both: eat meat or fish for nutrition, and add a supplement for performance.

# A creatine-rich day (non-veg)

- **Breakfast:** eggs and chapati (very little creatine, but good protein)
- **Lunch:** 150–200 g grilled mackerel or chicken
- **Dinner:** 150–200 g chicken, mutton or fish curry

About **1–1.5 g of creatine** from food, plus plenty of protein.

# Frequently asked questions

**Does cooking destroy creatine?**
Some of it. Long, high-heat cooking turns part of it into creatinine. Grilling or quick cooking keeps more.

**Is there creatine in eggs or milk?**
Only tiny amounts. They are great protein, but not creatine sources.

**Do I need creatine if I eat meat every day?**
No one needs it. Food keeps your levels normal. A supplement is only for extra performance.
