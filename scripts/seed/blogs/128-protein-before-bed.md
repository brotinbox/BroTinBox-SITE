> **Quick answer:** **Protein before bed can help a little**, especially if you train in the evening or struggle to hit your daily protein. Studies using about **30–40 g of slow-digesting protein (casein)** before sleep found it supported **overnight muscle repair**, and in some studies **slightly better strength and muscle gains** with training. Good Indian options: **a glass of milk, a bowl of curd or hung curd, or 75–100 g paneer**. But **total daily protein matters far more** than timing.

You have probably heard "never eat before bed". You may also have heard bodybuilders drink a protein shake at night. Who is right? Here is what the evidence says.

# What happens overnight

While you sleep (7–9 hours), you do not eat. Your muscles continue to repair, but without new protein coming in, muscle breakdown can slightly outpace building.

A slow-digesting protein before bed provides **amino acids through the night**, supporting repair.

# What research shows

- Studies giving about **40 g of casein protein** before sleep found **higher overnight muscle protein synthesis** compared with no protein.
- A 12-week training study found people who had **casein before bed** gained slightly more strength and muscle size than a group who did not, though total daily protein also differed.
- More recent research suggests the effect is **small**, and mostly matters if your **daily protein is otherwise not enough**.

**Bottom line:** it is a useful extra, not a magic trick.

# Who benefits most

- People who **train in the evening**
- People who **cannot reach their protein target** in 3 meals
- **Older adults** trying to keep muscle
- People in a **calorie deficit** protecting muscle

# Best protein foods before bed (Indian)

**Slow-digesting (dairy casein)**

- **Milk:** 1 glass (250 ml) = about 8 g protein
- **Curd:** 1 cup = about 5–6 g; **hung curd** about 10 g
- **Paneer:** 75–100 g = about 14–20 g
- **Casein protein powder:** about 24 g per scoop

**Other options**

- **2–3 boiled eggs:** about 12–18 g
- **Whey:** digests fast, but still adds protein. See [best time to take whey](/blog/best-time-to-take-whey).
- **Soya or chana** in a small portion (some people find them heavy at night)

# How much?

- **About 20–40 g of protein**
- Keep it **light**: 150–300 kcal, so it does not disturb sleep or push you over your calories

**Easy combos**

- Milk + 1 tbsp peanut butter: about 12 g protein, about 250 kcal
- Hung curd (1 cup) + a few berries or a date: about 10–12 g, about 150 kcal
- 100 g paneer with pepper: about 18–20 g, about 260–320 kcal

# Does eating before bed make you fat?

**Not by itself.** Weight gain depends on total calories over the day, not the time. A small, high-protein snack within your calorie target will not cause fat gain.

What causes problems is **large, oily late-night meals or snacking on sweets** while watching TV. See [eating late at night and weight loss](/blog/eating-late-at-night-weight-loss).

# Will it affect sleep?

- A **light protein snack** usually does not disturb sleep
- **Heavy, spicy or oily food** close to bed can cause acidity and poor sleep
- Leave **30–60 minutes** between the snack and lying down if you get reflux

Read [sleep and muscle growth](/blog/sleep-and-muscle-growth) for more.

# Is curd at night okay?

For most healthy people, yes. Some feel heavy or get mucus; if so, choose milk or paneer instead. See [is curd good at night](/blog/is-curd-good-at-night).

# Frequently asked questions

**Should I drink a protein shake before bed?**
Only if you need it to reach your daily protein. Milk or curd works just as well.

**Is milk before bed good for muscle gain?**
Yes. It provides slow-digesting casein and some carbs.

**Does protein before bed help fat loss?**
Indirectly, by protecting muscle and reducing late-night hunger.
