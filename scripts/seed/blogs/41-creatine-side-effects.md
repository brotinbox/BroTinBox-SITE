The link to **hair loss** comes from a single small 2009 study that has not been confirmed. Every few months a reel goes viral saying creatine "damages your kidneys" or "makes your hair fall". Meanwhile, sports scientists rate it as one of the very few supplements that actually work. So what is the truth?

# What creatine is

Creatine is a natural compound made in your body from amino acids. It is also found in **meat and fish**. It helps your muscles quickly recycle energy during short, intense efforts like lifting weights or sprinting.

Taking creatine as a supplement tops up your muscle stores, which can help you:

- Do 1–2 more reps per set
- Recover a little faster between sets
- Gain more strength and muscle over time when combined with training

Vegetarians often see a bigger effect because they get very little creatine from food.

# Side effect 1: water weight

**Is it real?** Yes.

Creatine pulls water **into your muscle cells**. In the first 1–4 weeks, many people gain about **1–2 kg** on the scale.

- This is **not fat** and not "bloating" under the skin in most people.
- Muscles may look slightly fuller.
- If you stop creatine, this water leaves in a few weeks.

If you are tracking weight loss, just expect this jump and do not panic.

# Side effect 2: kidney damage

**Is it real?** Not in healthy people at normal doses, based on current research.

The confusion comes from **creatinine**, a waste product your kidneys filter. Blood tests use creatinine to check kidney function. Taking creatine can raise creatinine levels slightly, which can look like a kidney problem on a report even when the kidneys are fine.

Multiple long-term studies in healthy adults taking 3–5 g a day have not found kidney damage.

What to do:

- If you get a blood test, **tell your doctor you take creatine**.
- If you have **kidney disease, diabetes with kidney involvement, or high blood pressure**, speak to your doctor before taking it.

# Side effect 3: hair loss

**Is it real?** Unproven.

This fear comes from one small 2009 study on rugby players. It found that a hormone called DHT rose after creatine loading. DHT is linked to male pattern baldness. But:

- The study did **not measure hair loss** at all.
- The DHT levels stayed within the normal range.
- Later studies have **not repeated** the DHT finding.

If you have a strong family history of baldness and are worried, you can skip it. But there is no solid evidence that creatine causes hair loss.

# Side effect 4: stomach upset

**Is it real?** Sometimes.

Large doses at once, especially during a "loading phase" of 20 g a day, can upset the stomach. The fix:

- Skip loading, take **3–5 g a day**
- Take it with a meal and water

# Side effect 5: cramps and dehydration

Older claims said creatine causes cramps and dehydration, especially in hot weather. Studies have not supported this. Still, in a warm city like Coimbatore, drink enough water through the day, whether or not you take creatine.

# How to take creatine safely

1. **Choose plain creatine monohydrate.** It is the most researched and usually the cheapest. Fancy forms are not better.
2. **Take 3–5 g a day, every day**, including rest days. Timing does not matter much.
3. **Loading is optional.** It fills your muscles in about a week instead of about a month.
4. **Buy from trusted sellers.** Fake supplements are common in India; buy from the brand or authorised sellers.
5. **Drink enough water.**

# Who should not take creatine without medical advice

- People with kidney or liver disease
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women
- Teenagers under 18 (focus on food and training first)
- Anyone on medicines that affect the kidneys

# Creatine vs protein powder: which first?

They do different jobs. Protein builds muscle tissue; creatine helps you train harder. If your food protein is already enough, creatine gives more benefit per rupee. If your protein is low, fix food first. Read [is whey protein enough to build muscle](/blog/is-whey-protein-enough-to-build-muscle).

# Frequently asked questions

**Is creatine a steroid?**
No. It is a natural compound found in meat and fish and made by your own body.

**Will I lose my gains if I stop?**
You will lose the extra water and maybe a little performance. The muscle you built through training stays if you keep training and eating well.

**Can women take creatine?**
Yes. The benefits and safety profile are similar.

**Should I cycle creatine?**
There is no evidence that cycling is needed.
