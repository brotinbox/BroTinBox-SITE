> **Quick answer:** One cooked bowl of **dal** (about 150 g, made from about 35–40 g dry dal) has about **7–9 g of protein**. **100 g of cooked chickpeas (chana)** has about **8–9 g**, **100 g of cooked rajma** about **8–9 g**, and **100 g of cooked green gram (moong)** about **7 g**. On dry weight, most dals and legumes have about **20–25 g of protein per 100 g**.

Dal is the backbone of Indian vegetarian protein. But is a bowl of dal really "high protein"? Here are the numbers, and how to make dal and legumes do more work in your diet.

# Protein per 100 g dry (uncooked)

These are typical values:

- **Toor dal (arhar):** about 22 g protein
- **Moong dal (split green gram):** about 24 g
- **Masoor dal (red lentil):** about 24–25 g
- **Urad dal:** about 24–25 g
- **Chana dal:** about 20–22 g
- **Whole green gram (moong):** about 23–24 g
- **Kabuli chana (white chickpeas):** about 19–20 g
- **Kala chana (black chickpeas):** about 18–20 g
- **Rajma (kidney beans):** about 22–24 g
- **Horse gram (kollu):** about 22 g

On paper, these look like high-protein foods. The catch is what happens when you cook them.

# Protein per 100 g cooked

Cooking adds a lot of water, so the protein per 100 g drops:

- **Cooked dal (thick):** about 6–8 g
- **Cooked chickpeas:** about 8–9 g
- **Cooked rajma:** about 8–9 g
- **Cooked whole green gram:** about 7 g
- **Cooked kollu:** about 7–8 g

# Protein per normal serving

- **1 bowl of dal (about 150 g cooked):** about 7–9 g
- **1 bowl of sambar:** about 5–7 g (it has more water and vegetables)
- **1 cup of chana sundal (about 150 g):** about 12–14 g
- **1 bowl of rajma (about 150 g):** about 12–13 g
- **1 cup of sprouted green gram salad:** about 7–10 g

So a bowl of dal gives you roughly the protein of **one and a half eggs**. Good, but not enough on its own for a high-protein meal.

# Are dals a complete protein?

Not quite. Dals are low in an amino acid called **methionine**, while rice and wheat are low in **lysine**. Eaten together, they cover each other. That is why **dal-rice, sambar-idli and rajma-chawal** are good combinations: together they make a much better protein.

You do not need to eat them in the same meal. Eating a mix over the day is enough.

# How to get more protein from dals and legumes

1. **Make dal thicker.** A thin, watery dal has less protein per bowl.
2. **Eat a bigger portion of dal and a smaller portion of rice.**
3. **Add legumes as a side.** Chana sundal, sprouted moong salad or kollu.
4. **Pair with dairy.** A bowl of curd adds about 6–8 g of protein.
5. **Use besan (gram flour).** Besan chilla has about 10–12 g of protein per serving.
6. **Mix with soya or paneer** to boost a vegetarian meal. See [soya chunks protein](/blog/soya-chunks-protein) and [protein in paneer](/blog/protein-in-paneer).

# A high-protein vegetarian day using dals

For about 85–100 g of protein:

- **Breakfast:** 2 besan chillas with curd, about 18 g
- **Mid-morning:** chana sundal, about 12 g
- **Lunch:** thick dal, 100 g paneer, rice or chapati, curd, about 35 g
- **Evening:** sprouted green gram salad, about 8 g
- **Dinner:** rajma or soya curry with chapati, about 18–20 g

# Are dals good for weight loss?

Yes. Dals and legumes are:

- **High in fibre**, which keeps you full
- **Moderate in calories** (a bowl of dal is about 120–180 kcal depending on oil and ghee)
- **Low in fat** when cooked simply

Watch the tadka. A generous ghee tempering can add 100+ kcal to one bowl. Our guide to [high-fibre Indian foods](/blog/high-fibre-indian-foods) covers more options, and [kollu for weight loss](/blog/kollu-horse-gram-benefits-weight-loss) looks at horse gram in detail.

# Frequently asked questions

**Which dal has the most protein?**
Masoor, moong and urad dal are among the highest at about 24–25 g per 100 g dry.

**Is dal enough protein for muscle gain?**
Not on its own. Combine dal with dairy, soya, paneer or eggs to reach a high protein target.

**Do sprouts have more protein?**
Sprouting slightly changes the nutrients and makes them easier to digest. Per gram of dry legume, protein is similar.

**Does dal cause gas?**
Some people get gas from legumes. Soaking, pressure cooking and adding hing and ginger help.
