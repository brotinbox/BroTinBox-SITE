> **Quick answer:** In your first week on a meal subscription, expect some adjustment. Portions may feel too big or too small, your digestion may need time to adapt to more fibre, and the scale may bounce around. Set your delivery routine, store and reheat your food safely, use skips when you need them, and judge the plan after 3–4 weeks, not 3 days.

Starting a meal subscription is exciting for about a day. Then a box arrives while you are in the shower, a colleague suggests biryani, and you wonder if you ordered the right plan.

This is normal. Here is what your first week may look like, and how to give yourself the best chance of sticking with it.

# Before you start

## Try one box first

If you have never tried a service before, do not commit to a month straight away. Our [daily trial box](/tomorrow) lets you taste the food and see the portions, with no subscription required.

## Check your area and delivery slot

We deliver **Monday to Saturday**, in two slots: **7:00–9:00 AM** and **7:00–9:00 PM**. There is no delivery on Sundays. Check your pincode on our [delivery areas page](/delivery-areas) to confirm coverage and any delivery fee.

## Know your numbers

Depending on the day, our non-veg boxes contain about 770–977 kcal and 58–81 g of protein, and our veg boxes about 625–897 kcal and 29–48 g of protein. Compare that with your own target using our [calorie deficit guide](/blog/calorie-deficit-how-to-calculate) and [protein guide](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day). Then decide whether one box a day or two is the right fit by looking at [our plans](/plans).

## Check the allergens

Each box lists its allergens, which can include dairy, egg, fish, gluten, peanut and soy. If you have an allergy, read the list on the [menu](/menu) carefully for each day, and read our [food safety page](/food-safety) before you order.

# Your first week, day by day

## Days 1–2: Getting used to the routine

The main job is to get into a simple routine. Know when your box arrives, and be ready for it.

**Food safety matters:** eat your box within 2 hours of delivery, or refrigerate it at 5°C or below within that time. Refrigerated meals should be eaten within 24 hours of delivery. Reheat until piping hot, and reheat only once. Keep salads and curd cold, and do not reheat them.

## Days 2–3: The portion question

Many people feel that the box is either surprisingly filling or a bit small. Both are common.

- **Too much?** A box can be a big meal for a smaller or less active person. You can split it into two sittings, as long as you refrigerate the second part within 2 hours and eat it within 24 hours.
- **Too little?** If you are used to large portions, give it several days. Eat slowly, starting with the protein and salad. If you are still hungry after a week, you may simply need a bit more food. Add a simple side such as fruit or curd.

## Days 3–4: Your digestion adjusts

Our boxes include a weighed legume and a salad, and that may be a lot more fibre than you are used to. Some gas or bloating can happen at first. Drink more water, chew well, and walk after your meal. Our [fibre guide](/blog/high-fibre-indian-foods) has more tips. If you feel severe or lasting discomfort, reach out to a doctor.

## Days 4–5: The temptation test

This is when the novelty wears off. Someone orders parotta at work, or there is a family function. You do not need to be perfect. Have the meal, enjoy it, and go back to your plan. Our [eating out guide](/blog/eating-out-high-protein-coimbatore) can help you choose.

## Day 6: Plan your Sunday

Our kitchen is closed on Sundays, so work out what you will eat that day. Try cooking at home with a similar balance of protein, vegetables and a measured carb. A normal family lunch is completely fine.

## Day 7: Check in, and do not judge the scale yet

Your weight may move up or down during the first week because of changes in salt, carbs, fibre and water. Note your weight, your waist, your energy and your hunger, and leave it there. Three to four weeks gives you a much more useful picture. See [how to tell if you have really stalled](/blog/weight-loss-plateau-why-scale-stopped).

# Skips, pauses and changes

Life happens, so learn the rules on day one.

- **The cutoff is 9:00 PM IST the night before delivery.** Before then, you can skip, pause or change a meal free of charge.
- **After the cutoff, the meal is locked.** It will be cooked and delivered, and it counts as used.
- **Skipped or paused meals on a plan become meal credits.** Your end date moves forward, so you still receive every meal you paid for.
- **You can pause for 1 to 30 days at a time.**

For the full details, including cancellations and refunds, read our [refund and cancellation policy](/refund-policy). Set a phone reminder for 8:30 PM if you think you might forget.

# How to make it last beyond week one

## Eat the same way, even outside the box

A box handles one or two meals. The rest of the day still counts. Keep your other meals simple, with some protein and vegetables. Try our [high-protein breakfast ideas](/blog/high-protein-indian-breakfast-ideas) if you need some inspiration.

## Track only a few things

Weigh yourself a few times a week, and measure your waist every 2 weeks. Write down how you feel. Do not obsess over each day's number.

## Tell us what is not working

If something is wrong with your food or your delivery, tell us as soon as you can. You can reach us through our [contact page](/contact). We would rather fix a problem than lose you to it.

# Frequently asked questions

**How soon will I see results?**
Most people can start to see a trend in 3–4 weeks. It depends on your starting point, your total calories, your activity and how consistent you are.

**Will I get bored of the food?**
That is why we use a 30-day rotation rather than the same few boxes. Different mains, legumes, salads and carbs come around through the month.

**What happens if I miss a delivery?**
Read our [refund and cancellation policy](/refund-policy) for how missed and locked meals work, and contact us quickly if something goes wrong.

**Can I use a subscription if I am not trying to lose weight?**
Yes. Plenty of people simply want a reliable, measured meal without having to cook. Our boxes also suit [people trying to gain muscle](/blog/indian-gym-diet-muscle-gain), when combined with enough total food.
